## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

Novo Nordisk's pipeline is essentially an attempt to build the next semaglutide before the current one erodes: one molecule is roughly three-quarters of revenue and its patents have begun to expire. CagriSema is the closest answer, now under FDA review, with amycretin behind it — both engineered to strip off more weight than Wegovy does. The bets outside obesity are thinner, and got thinner in July 2026 when the IL-6 antibody ziltivekimab failed its first large cardiovascular outcomes trial.

## History: 1995–2025

### 1995–2009: Insulin engineering and the GLP-1 seed
Novo Nordisk entered the late 1990s as the world's insulin specialist, formed by the 1989 merger of Danish rivals Novo and Nordisk and controlled then as now by the Novo Nordisk Foundation. The company spun off its enzymes business as Novozymes in 2000 to focus on pharmaceuticals. Growth came from converting the world from human insulin to patent-protected analogs — NovoRapid (insulin aspart) from 1999, Levemir (insulin detemir) from 2004 — plus NovoSeven (eptacog alfa) in hemophilia and Norditropin in growth hormone. The pivotal science, though, was quieter: in the mid-1990s Lotte Bjerre Knudsen's team stabilized the gut hormone GLP-1 into a daily injectable, liraglutide. Victoza (liraglutide) was approved in 2010 and became a multi-billion-dollar diabetes franchise, validating both the mechanism and the fatty-acid-acylation chemistry that would later produce semaglutide.

### 2010–2016: Victoza's rise, Tresiba's stumble, and a reset
The Victoza decade also exposed vulnerabilities. Tresiba (insulin degludec), the next-generation basal insulin, was rejected by the FDA in 2013 over cardiovascular signals and only reached the US in 2015 after the DEVOTE outcomes trial. Saxenda — liraglutide 3.0 mg — became the first GLP-1 approved for obesity in 2014, seeding a market almost nobody took seriously. In 2016, US payer pressure on insulin pricing forced a profit warning and about 1,000 job cuts; long-time CEO Lars Rebien Sørensen departed and Lars Fruergaard Jørgensen took over in January 2017. US insulin pricing would keep generating political heat — congressional hearings, state lawsuits, and eventually the 2023 move to $35 co-pay caps and steep list-price cuts, followed by the decision to discontinue Levemir in the US in 2024.

### 2017–2023: Semaglutide changes everything
Ozempic (semaglutide), a once-weekly GLP-1 with markedly better efficacy, was approved in the US in December 2017. Rybelsus, the first oral GLP-1 (semaglutide with SNAC absorption technology), followed in 2019. Then came Wegovy in June 2021 — semaglutide 2.4 mg for obesity, delivering ~15% weight loss in STEP-1 — and demand instantly outran supply. The 2023 SELECT trial showed a 20% reduction in major cardiovascular events in people with obesity, transforming the drug from "lifestyle" to cardiometabolic medicine. Novo became Europe's most valuable listed company, and its market capitalization peaked above $600B in 2024. The company plowed cash into capacity: in a linked 2024 transaction, parent Novo Holdings agreed to buy CDMO Catalent for $16.5B, with Novo Nordisk acquiring three fill-finish sites for $11B (closed December 2024). Bolt-on M&A diversified the pipeline: Dicerna for $3.3B (2021, RNAi), Forma Therapeutics for $1.1B (2022, sickle cell), Inversago for up to $1.075B (2023, CB1 blockers), and Cardior for up to €1.025B (2024, heart failure).

### 2024–2025: The cliff edge of expectations
Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) proved more efficacious, and in December 2024 Novo's next-generation hope CagriSema (cagrilintide/semaglutide) delivered 20.4–22.7% weight loss in REDEFINE-1 — strong, but below the promised ~25% — and the stock cratered. Compounded semaglutide in the US grew to rival Novo's own GLP-1 volumes; a short-lived Hims & Hers partnership collapsed acrimoniously in June 2025. After three guidance cuts and a ~46% share-price decline in 2025, the board ousted Jørgensen in May 2025; International Operations chief Maziar Mike Doustdar became CEO on 7 August 2025. He cut ~9,000 jobs (DKK 8B annual savings), and an October 2025 governance clash with the Novo Nordisk Foundation saw chair Helge Lund and directors resign, with former CEO Lars Rebien Sørensen installed as chair. Doustdar lost a bruising ~$10B bidding war with Pfizer for obesity biotech Metsera in November 2025 but closed the $4.7B (+$0.5B CVR) acquisition of Akero Therapeutics (efruxifermin, MASH) in December 2025. The year ended with genuine wins: Wegovy's MASH approval (August), the FDA approval of the oral Wegovy pill (22 December 2025) — the first oral GLP-1 for obesity — and the CagriSema NDA filing (18 December 2025). Semaglutide's failure in Alzheimer's (EVOKE, late 2025) closed off one moonshot.

## Current strategic bets

Novo enters 2026 guiding to a sales *decline* of 5–13% at CER — an extraordinary reversal driven by US price resets and the first semaglutide patent expiries — while betting it can trade price for volume and defend leadership with a deeper obesity pipeline. Under the November 2025 agreement with the US administration, cash-pay Ozempic/Wegovy prices fell to $349/month (from $499), with a $245/month government-negotiated price for Medicare/Medicaid GLP-1 access and the semaglutide Medicare Maximum Fair Price taking effect in 2027. Management argues volume growth, the oral Wegovy launch (January 2026, priced for access) and new indications (MASH, CKD, CV) can substantially offset price.

The pipeline is concentrated on next-generation weight management. CagriSema is filed with the FDA (decision expected late 2026), though it missed non-inferiority versus tirzepatide 15 mg in REDEFINE-4 (Feb 2026; 23% weight loss at 84 weeks) — a marketing problem more than a clinical one. Amycretin (zenagamtide), a unimolecular GLP-1/amylin co-agonist showing ~22% weight loss at 36 weeks in phase 1b/2a, entered phase 3 in obesity (Q1 2026, subcutaneous and oral) with type 2 diabetes phase 3 to follow — arguably Novo's most important internal asset. Cagrilintide monotherapy is also in phase 3. The notable China-originated bet is UBT251, a GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple agonist licensed from United Laboratories (Mar 2025; $200M upfront, up to ~$2B) that posted 19.7% weight loss at 24 weeks in Chinese phase 2 — Novo's answer to Lilly's retatrutide, with rights excluding Greater China. Beyond obesity: efruxifermin (FGF21, phase 3 MASH, via Akero), Mim8/denecimig (next-gen hemophilia A bispecific, filed US/EU), Awiqli US launch (H2 2026), Icosema (icodec/semaglutide combo, approved Japan/China 2026), and Omeros' zaltenibart licensed for rare blood disorders (2025). Smaller discovery deals (Septerna, Deep Apple, Gensaic, Lexicon's LX9851) target oral small-molecule and next-gen cardiometabolic mechanisms.

## Key risks

- **Single-molecule concentration:** semaglutide ≈74% of FY2025 revenue (~DKK 228B). Any semaglutide-specific safety, pricing, or competitive shock is a company-level event.
- **Patent cliff, two waves:** compound LOE already hit Canada (Jan 2026, patent lapsed) and China/India/Brazil/Turkey and others (Mar 2026) — markets holding ~40% of the world's population, with 40+ generic entrants in India alone; then the core wave: EU ~Mar 2031 (SPC), US Dec 2031/2032. Guidance already embeds a 2026 revenue decline of 5–13% CER.
- **Competition:** Lilly's Zepbound overtook Wegovy in US obesity during Q4 2025; orforglipron (oral, small molecule) launching 2026; retatrutide phase 3 showed up to ~28.7% weight loss. CagriSema failed non-inferiority vs tirzepatide (REDEFINE-4).
- **US pricing/IRA:** Medicare MFP for semaglutide from 2027 (~$245/month); MFN deal cut cash prices ~30–70%; gross margin already fell to 81.0% (from 84.7%) in 2025.
- **Pipeline execution:** REDEFINE-1 and REDEFINE-4 both underdelivered vs expectations; EVOKE (Alzheimer's) failed; amycretin phase 3 data are years out.
- **Litigation/other:** hundreds of US GLP-1 product-liability suits (gastroparesis claims, consolidated MDL); compounding enforcement ongoing; governance friction between Foundation and board surfaced in Oct 2025; ~DKK 60B/yr capex creates fixed-cost leverage if volumes disappoint.
