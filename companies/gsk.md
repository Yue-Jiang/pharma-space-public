## History: 1995-2025

### Origins and the 2000 merger
GSK descends from two British pharmaceutical dynasties. Glaxo, a New Zealand-born dried-milk business turned drugmaker, became a global force on the back of Zantac (ranitidine), the anti-ulcer drug that in 1988 became the world's best-selling medicine. In 1995 Glaxo acquired Wellcome for roughly £9 billion (est.) — then Britain's largest merger — forming Glaxo Wellcome, which brought HIV pioneer AZT. SmithKline Beecham, itself the product of a 1989 transatlantic merger, contributed Paxil, Augmentin and a vaccines business. In January 2000 the two agreed an all-share merger valued around $75–76 billion (est.), creating GlaxoSmithKline, briefly the world's largest pharmaceutical company, with UK headquarters and roughly 7% of the global drug market at the time (est.).

### 2000s: the Advair engine and Avandia's fall
The 2000s were powered by Advair/Seretide (fluticasone/salmeterol), the inhaled asthma/COPD combination that peaked around $8 billion a year (est.), plus Avandia (rosiglitazone) in diabetes. Avandia became the cautionary tale: a 2007 meta-analysis linked it to cardiovascular risk, leading to severe US restrictions and an EU market withdrawal in 2010, erasing what had been a multi-billion-dollar franchise. In 2012 GSK paid $3 billion in the then-largest healthcare-fraud settlement in US history, covering off-label promotion of Paxil and Wellbutrin and withheld Avandia safety data. In 2014 a Chinese court fined GSK ~£300 million ($490 million) for bribery — the largest corporate fine in China at the time — after a scandal that decimated its China business. Meanwhile GSK built ViiV Healthcare (2009), an HIV joint venture with Pfizer (Shionogi joined 2012), whose integrase inhibitor dolutegravir (Tivicay, 2013; Triumeq, 2014) became the backbone of modern HIV therapy and one of GSK's most valuable assets.

### 2008-2016: Witty era — diversification and the Novartis swap
CEO Andrew Witty (2008–2017) responded to the Advair patent cliff with breadth: Stiefel dermatology (~$3.6B, 2009), Human Genome Sciences (~$3B, 2012, bringing lupus drug Benlysta), and consumer health scale. The signature move was the 2014–15 three-part Novartis transaction: GSK sold its marketed oncology portfolio to Novartis for $16 billion, bought Novartis's vaccines business (ex-flu) for ~$5.25 billion plus milestones, and folded consumer health into a GSK-majority joint venture. The deal made GSK a vaccines-and-consumer heavyweight but left it without commercial oncology — a decision later reversed at cost.

### 2017-2021: Walmsley reboots the pharma engine
Emma Walmsley — the first woman to run a major pharma company — became CEO in April 2017 and refocused capital on the pipeline, hiring Hal Barron as chief scientific officer (succeeded by Tony Wood in 2022). Shingrix, launched 2017, became a runaway blockbuster. GSK bought Tesaro for $5.1 billion (closed 2019) to re-enter oncology with Zejula (niraparib), and formed a consumer-health JV with Pfizer (2019) as a staging ground for separation. Blenrep (belantamab mafodotin), the first anti-BCMA agent, won accelerated US approval in 2020 but was withdrawn from the US market in late 2022 after the confirmatory DREAMM-3 trial failed. In 2021 activist Elliott Management took a large stake and pressed for change; Walmsley held her course.

### 2022-2025: Haleon, Zantac closure, and a BD sprint
In July 2022 GSK demerged its consumer business as Haleon — the largest European listing in a decade — leaving a pure biopharma company. Bolt-ons followed: Affinivax (~$2.1B upfront, pneumococcal vaccines, 2022), Sierra Oncology ($1.9B, momelotinib — approved as Ojjaara in 2023), Bellus Health ($2B, camlipixant, 2023), Aiolos Bio ($1B upfront, anti-TSLP originated in China, 2024), IDRx (~$1B upfront, KIT inhibitor, 2025) and efimosfermin for MASH from Boston Pharmaceuticals ($1.2B upfront, May 2025). Arexvy, the first RSV vaccine approved anywhere (May 2023), rocketed to ~£1.2 billion, then collapsed to ~£0.6 billion after US advisory-committee recommendations narrowed in 2024 — a lesson in US vaccine-policy risk. In October 2024 GSK largely closed the Zantac chapter, agreeing to pay up to $2.2 billion to settle ~80,000 US state-court cases (93% of pending claims) plus $70 million for a whistleblower suit, without admitting liability. 2025 delivered five major FDA approvals — Blenrep (reinstated in October with a third-line-plus label), Exdensur (depemokimab), Nucala in COPD, Penmenvy (MenABCWY vaccine) and Blujepa (gepotidacin, a first-in-class oral antibiotic) — and sales of £32.7 billion. In September 2025 Walmsley announced she would step down; chief commercial officer Luke Miels took over on 1 January 2026, inheriting the £40 billion 2031 target and the dolutegravir cliff.

## Current strategic bets

GSK's stated strategy is to more than offset the 2027–2028 dolutegravir loss of exclusivity with specialty medicines and pipeline launches, holding its >£40 billion 2031 revenue outlook (consensus sits several billion below — management calls the gap "doable").

Key bets for 2025–2026:

- **Ultra-long-acting respiratory biologics.** Exdensur (depemokimab), the first twice-yearly anti-IL-5 biologic, approved in the US December 2025 for severe eosinophilic asthma; GSK sees up to ~$4 billion peak sales, with nasal-polyps and EGPA expansion ahead. Nucala's COPD approval (May 2025) extends the IL-5 franchise.
- **Hepatitis B functional cure.** Bepirovirsen (antisense, licensed from Ionis) posted positive B-Well 1/2 phase III data (Jan 2026; ~19–26% functional cure vs 0% placebo, NEJM May 2026); filings underway in US/EU/Japan/China with first decisions expected late 2026.
- **Oncology rebuild.** Blenrep's US relaunch (Oct 2025) plus earlier-line DREAMM studies; Jemperli expansion (AZUR-1 in rectal cancer read out 2026); velzatinib (ex-IDRx KIT inhibitor) in phase III for GIST; B7-H3 ADC risvutatug rezetecan (licensed from Hansoh, est.) starting pivotal trials in small-cell lung cancer.
- **China-originated pipeline at scale.** The July 2025 Hengrui Pharma alliance: $500M upfront, up to 12 programs and ~$12 billion in milestones, headlined by HRS-9821, a PDE3/4 inhibitor for COPD. This follows earlier Hansoh ADC licenses and the Aiolos (Hengrui-originated) anti-TSLP deal — GSK is among the heaviest users of Chinese-origin licensing.
- **HIV beyond dolutegravir.** ViiV's long-acting injectables (Cabenuva, Apretude — 30% of US HIV sales) plus ultra-long-acting regimens (Q4M dosing; CUATRO phase III) to bridge the LOE.
- **MASH.** Efimosfermin (FGF21 analog) entered phase III in 2025.

Setbacks temper the story: camlipixant (the $2B Bellus asset) was discontinued for refractory chronic cough in July 2026 after mixed CALM-1/CALM-2 results, and the belrestotug (TIGIT) program was terminated in 2025 with a £471M impairment.

## Key risks

- **Dolutegravir cliff (2027–2028):** dolutegravir-based products generated ~$5.65B in 2025; HIV overall is £7.7B, ~24% of revenue. Largest single overhang; mitigation depends on long-acting injectable uptake.
- **Vaccine concentration and US policy:** Shingrix (£3.6B) has plateaued in the US ("harder-to-reach consumers"); Arexvy fell ~50% from its 2023 peak after ACIP narrowed RSV recommendations; further US vaccine-policy turbulence (ACIP overhaul) is a structural risk to a £9.2B segment.
- **Trelegy LOE ~2030:** £3.0B at risk from the largest General Medicines product (US generic entry estimated 2030).
- **IRA / pricing:** Medicare Part D redesign already pressuring US channel pricing (called out in 2025 results); potential Medicare negotiation exposure for mature brands; UK/EU pricing pressure.
- **Pipeline execution:** camlipixant failure (2026) and belrestotug termination (2025) show conversion risk in the very pipeline expected to fill the 2031 gap; Blenrep returned with a narrower-than-hoped US label.
- **Residual litigation:** ~7% of Zantac state cases sat outside the 2024 settlement plus federal appeal risk (largely resolved economics, est.); ordinary-course IP challenges on Trelegy and HIV patents.
