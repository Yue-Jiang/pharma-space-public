## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

AbbVie's problem is arithmetic: Skyrizi and Rinvoq now carry the company, and almost everything below exists to give it a third and fourth act before those two peak. The nearest-term bets are in neuroscience — tavapadon, a once-daily Parkinson's pill awaiting an FDA decision, on top of the Vyalev infusion already launched — and in a widening oncology bench of antibody-drug conjugates and T-cell engagers, two of which reached the market in the past year. The longer-dated bets are a $10.9B move for an anti-IL-13 antibody that could be dosed as rarely as quarterly, and a first entry into obesity that deliberately avoids the GLP-1 mechanism everyone else is crowding into.

## History: 1995-2025

### Inside Abbott and the birth of Humira (1995–2012)

AbbVie's story begins inside Abbott Laboratories, whose pharmaceutical division developed and, from 2003, commercialized Humira (adalimumab), the first fully human anti-TNF monoclonal antibody, initially for rheumatoid arthritis. Over the following decade Humira accumulated indications across rheumatology, dermatology, and gastroenterology and became the world's best-selling drug. In October 2011 Abbott announced it would split in two, and on January 1, 2013 AbbVie began trading as an independent research-based biopharma under CEO Richard Gonzalez, carrying Humira — then roughly half of its ~$18.8B revenue — plus a thin late-stage pipeline. The concentration risk was obvious from day one; nearly everything AbbVie did for the next decade was, in some way, preparation for Humira's patent cliff.

### Deal-making to diversify (2013–2018)

In 2014 AbbVie agreed to buy Shire for about $54B in a tax-inversion deal, then abandoned it after the US Treasury tightened inversion rules, paying a ~$1.6B breakup fee. In 2015 it paid $21B for Pharmacyclics, acquiring US co-rights to Imbruvica (ibrutinib) with Janssen — a bet on hematology-oncology that paid off commercially for years, though competition later eroded it. In 2016 it bought Stemcentrx for $5.8B upfront for the lung-cancer ADC rovalpituzumab tesirine (Rova-T), which failed in trials and was scrapped by 2019 — one of the industry's costlier pipeline write-offs. Internally, the period produced two critical successes: Venclexta (venetoclax, with Genentech), the first BCL-2 inhibitor, and the hepatitis C combination Mavyret (glecaprevir/pibrentasvir, 2017), which won share on price after the earlier Viekira Pak underperformed against Gilead.

### The Humira fortress and its critics (2016–2022)

AbbVie protected Humira with a famously dense patent estate — well over one hundred patents — and settled with every biosimilar maker to keep US copies off the market until 2023, while conceding Europe in 2018. Critics in Congress and antitrust plaintiffs called it a "patent thicket"; a class action alleging anticompetitive conduct was dismissed, and the dismissal was upheld by the Seventh Circuit in 2022. The strategy bought time that mattered: Skyrizi (risankizumab, anti-IL-23, licensed from Boehringer Ingelheim) and Rinvoq (upadacitinib, oral JAK1 inhibitor, internally discovered) both launched in 2019 and were deliberately positioned across Humira's indications and beyond. Humira peaked at about $21.2B in 2022 — the biggest single-product franchise in pharma history to that point.

### Allergan and the aesthetics pivot (2019–2020)

In June 2019 AbbVie announced the $63B acquisition of Allergan, closing in May 2020. The deal brought Botox (therapeutic and cosmetic), Vraylar, Juvederm, eye care, and the migraine assets Ubrelvy and (via Allergan's earlier deal) atogepant/Qulipta. It roughly halved AbbVie's Humira dependence overnight and created a large, cash-generative aesthetics and neuroscience base, at the cost of significant debt.

### Over the cliff — and out the other side (2023–2025)

US Humira biosimilars arrived in January 2023 (Amjevita first, then a wave in mid-2023), and Humira fell from ~$21B to $4.5B by 2025. Revenue dipped in 2023 ($54.3B) but AbbVie returned to growth in 2024 ($56.3B) and hit a record $61.16B in 2025 — CEO Rob Michael, who succeeded Gonzalez in July 2024, noted this came despite ~$16B of cumulative US Humira erosion. Skyrizi ($17.6B, +50%) and Rinvoq ($8.3B, +39%) reached a combined $25.9B in 2025, beating AbbVie's own 2027 target two years early and exceeding peak Humira. Meanwhile AbbVie bought ImmunoGen ($10.1B, closed Feb 2024) for the ovarian-cancer ADC Elahere, and Cerevel Therapeutics ($8.7B, closed Aug 2024) for neuroscience. Cerevel's centerpiece, the schizophrenia drug emraclidine, failed both Phase 2 EMPOWER trials in November 2024 — wiping ~$40B off AbbVie's market cap in a day and triggering a ~$3.5B impairment — but the other Cerevel asset, tavapadon (Parkinson's), cleared three Phase 3 trials. Vyalev, the 24-hour subcutaneous levodopa/carbidopa prodrug infusion, was approved in the US in October 2024 after two earlier rejections, and Emrelis (telisotuzumab vedotin), AbbVie's first internally developed solid-tumor ADC, was approved for c-Met-high non-small cell lung cancer in May 2025.

## Current strategic bets

AbbVie guided 2026 revenue to ~$67B (+9.5%) and is spending heavily to build growth beyond 2029. The clearest bets:

1. Immunology depth beyond Skyrizi/Rinvoq: the pending $10.9B acquisition of Apogee Therapeutics (announced June 22, 2026, closing expected Q3 2026) adds zumilokibart, a half-life-extended anti-IL-13 antibody in Phase 3 for atopic dermatitis with quarterly-or-less dosing, plus an IL-13/TSLP combination for asthma. Capstan Therapeutics ($2.1B, closed Aug 2025) adds in vivo anti-CD19 CAR-T via targeted lipid nanoparticles — a potential immune-reset approach for autoimmune disease. Nimble Therapeutics (Jan 2025) adds an oral IL-23R peptide.
2. Neuroscience as the second pillar: Vyalev is expected to pass $1B in 2026; tavapadon (D1/D5 partial agonist, once-daily oral for Parkinson's) has an FDA decision expected in Q3 2026; AbbVie sees >$5B peak for the Parkinson's franchise. In psychiatry, AbbVie agreed to acquire Gilgamesh Pharmaceuticals' bretisilocin (a psychedelic-derived Phase 2 asset for major depressive disorder) for ~$1.2B (Aug 2025). The migraine franchise (Ubrelvy + Qulipta) is guided toward $5B+ peak.
3. Obesity entry: the Gubra license (Mar 2025; $350M upfront, up to ~$1.9B milestones) for GUB014295/ABBV-295, a long-acting amylin analog — AbbVie's stated entry into metabolic disease.
4. Oncology via ADCs and bispecifics: Elahere and Emrelis on market; Temab-A (ABBV-400, c-Met ADC) in Phase 3 colorectal cancer with breakthrough designation; etentamig (ABBV-383, BCMAxCD3) in late-stage myeloma; China/India-originated licenses include Simcere's SIM0500 (GPRC5D/BCMA/CD3 trispecific, Jan 2025, >$1B total) and IGI's ISB 2001 (CD3/BCMA/CD38 trispecific, Jul 2025, ~$1.9B total).
5. Policy positioning: in January 2026 AbbVie signed a voluntary agreement with the Trump administration — Medicaid most-favored-nation-style pricing and TrumpRx direct-to-consumer participation in exchange for tariff exemption — alongside a $100B, 10-year US R&D and manufacturing pledge.

## Key risks

- **Re-concentration in immunology**: Skyrizi + Rinvoq = $25.9B, ~42% of 2025 revenue; both face low-single-digit annual pricing pressure and eventual LOEs (Skyrizi ~2033 est.; Rinvoq US settlements reportedly to 2037). JAK-class safety labeling remains a constraint on Rinvoq.
- **Mid-decade LOEs**: Venclexta (~2028 est.) and Vraylar (~2029) put roughly $6.4B of 2025 revenue at risk; Imbruvica ($2.9B, declining ~7-20%/yr) takes an IRA-negotiated Medicare price from January 2026, and Botox has been selected for a later negotiation round.
- **Humira tail**: still $4.5B in 2025, falling ~40-50%/yr.
- **Pipeline execution**: emraclidine's Phase 2 failure ($3.5B impairment) and Rova-T show acquisition risk; heavy 2024-2026 BD spending (ImmunoGen, Cerevel, Capstan, Apogee, 30+ deals) must convert.
- **Policy/pricing**: IRA negotiation exposure; Medicaid MFN pricing concessions under the January 2026 White House agreement; aesthetics remains consumer-cyclical (segment -6.1% in 2025).
- **Balance sheet**: sizable debt from Allergan and subsequent deals; negative shareholders' equity (~-$3.3B, 2025).
