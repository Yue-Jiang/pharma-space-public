## History: 1995-2025

### 1995-2004: French national champions converge
Modern Sanofi is the product of serial French consolidation. Sanofi was founded in 1973 as a subsidiary of the oil group Elf Aquitaine; Synthélabo grew under L'Oréal's ownership. The two merged in 1999 to form Sanofi-Synthélabo, the same year Germany's Hoechst and France's Rhône-Poulenc combined into Aventis. In 2004, with the encouragement of the French government (which preferred a domestic buyer to a rumored Novartis bid), Sanofi-Synthélabo acquired the much larger Aventis for roughly €55 billion — one of the biggest pharma deals ever at the time — creating sanofi-aventis, then the world's third-largest drugmaker. The name was simplified to Sanofi in 2011.

### 2004-2014: Blockbusters, cliffs, and the Genzyme pivot
The merged company lived off a broad primary-care portfolio: the antiplatelet Plavix (clopidogrel, partnered with Bristol Myers Squibb), anticoagulant Lovenox (enoxaparin), insomnia drug Ambien (zolpidem), antihistamine Allegra (fexofenadine), chemotherapy Taxotere (docetaxel), and the long-acting insulin Lantus (insulin glargine), which became its biggest product. A brutal cliff loomed: Plavix lost US exclusivity in 2012, wiping out billions in sales. CEO Chris Viehbacher (2008-2014) responded by diversifying — consumer health (Chattem, 2010), animal health (Merial), generics, and above all the $20.1 billion acquisition of rare-disease pioneer Genzyme in 2011, which brought Cerezyme, Fabrazyme, Myozyme and a durable rare-disease franchise. The 2003-era antibody alliance with Regeneron was expanded repeatedly, seeding what became Dupixent. Viehbacher was abruptly fired in late 2014 amid board friction and a mishandled warning on US diabetes pricing.

### 2015-2019: Brandicourt years — Dupixent arrives, much else stumbles
Under Olivier Brandicourt (2015-2019), Lantus lost exclusivity in 2015 and US insulin pricing pressure compounded the decline. The PCSK9 cholesterol antibody Praluent (alirocumab, with Regeneron) disappointed commercially and drew a long patent war with Amgen. The dengue vaccine Dengvaxia became a reputational crisis in 2017 when the Philippines suspended its school program after Sanofi disclosed risks in previously uninfected children. The clear bright spot was Dupixent (dupilumab), approved in 2017 for atopic dermatitis and destined to become one of the industry's biggest franchises. In 2018 Sanofi paid $11.6 billion for hemophilia specialist Bioverativ and ~€3.9 billion for nanobody firm Ablynx (Cablivi). In 2019 ranitidine (Zantac, which Sanofi sold OTC in the US) was pulled from the market over NDMA contamination, spawning mass litigation.

### 2019-2024: Hudson's "Play to Win"
Paul Hudson arrived from Novartis in September 2019 and refocused the company: exit from diabetes and cardiovascular research, prioritization of Dupixent, vaccines, and first-in-class pipeline bets, and a string of bolt-on acquisitions — Synthorx ($2.5B, 2019), Principia ($3.7B, 2020, source of rilzabrutinib and tolebrutinib rights), Kadmon ($1.9B, 2021, Rezurock), Translate Bio ($3.2B, 2021, mRNA), Kymab (~$1.5B, 2021, amlitelimab), Provention Bio ($2.9B, 2023, Tzield), and Inhibrx (~$1.7B, 2024). The COVID era was mixed: Sanofi/GSK's protein-based vaccine arrived late, a rare miss for one of the world's largest vaccine makers. The API unit was spun off as EUROAPI (2022). An October 2023 decision to drop the 2025 margin target and spend more on R&D — announced alongside plans to separate consumer health — triggered a ~19% one-day share drop (est.). Zantac exposure was largely defused via settlements (about $100 million for ~4,000 cases in April 2024, then $200-250 million (est.) for 10,000+ more), and US courts subsequently gutted much of the remaining science. Beyfortus (2023 launch, with AstraZeneca) and Altuviiio (2023) became fast-growing new franchises.

### 2025-2026: Pure-play biopharma, pipeline reckoning
On April 30, 2025 Sanofi closed the sale of a 50% controlling stake in consumer unit Opella to CD&R at a ~€16 billion enterprise value, netting ~€10 billion and retaining 48.2% — completing the pivot to pure-play biopharma. It redeployed capital aggressively: Blueprint Medicines ($9.1B upfront, up to $9.5B, systemic mastocytosis drug Ayvakit), Vigil Neuroscience (~$470M, TREM2 Alzheimer's asset, est.), Vicebio (~$1.15B, respiratory vaccines, est.), plus large licensing deals. FY2025 delivered €43.6 billion in sales (+9.9% CER) with Dupixent at ~$17.8 billion. But the pipeline narrative cracked: tolebrutinib failed in primary progressive MS and, despite breakthrough designation and positive HERCULES data in non-relapsing secondary progressive MS, drew a surprise FDA complete response letter in December 2025. In February 2026 the board ousted Hudson and recruited Merck KGaA chief (and former Genzyme-integration leader) Belén Garijo, effective April 29, 2026 — the first woman to run Sanofi.

## Current strategic bets

Sanofi enters 2026 as a pure-play biopharma with an immunology-first strategy and an explicit goal of managing the early-2030s Dupixent cliff. The central bet is **amlitelimab** (anti-OX40L, from Kymab), which posted positive phase 3 atopic dermatitis readouts (COAST 2, SHORE) in late 2025 and is positioned as a lower-frequency-dosing successor/complement to Dupixent. **Rilzabrutinib (Wayrilz)** is the newest launch — first BTK inhibitor for immune thrombocytopenia, approved in the US (Aug 2025), EU (Dec 2025), and Japan (Jun 2026), with additional immunology indications in development. **Tolebrutinib** remains a high-value, high-uncertainty neurology asset: provisionally approved in the UAE, under EU review, but facing a US complete response letter that Sanofi is contesting; a related IFRS impairment test was flagged with FY2025 results. **Itepekimab** (anti-IL-33, COPD, with Regeneron) had split phase 3 results in 2025 (est.), leaving its path unclear. **Duvakitug** (anti-TL1A, partnered with Teva) showed clinically meaningful phase 2b maintenance data in ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease. In vaccines, Sanofi is defending its influenza leadership while facing US policy hostility, and is building next-generation respiratory combinations (Vicebio) and an mRNA platform (Translate Bio legacy).

Business development has leaned into AI-discovered and China-originated assets: a $1.7 billion deal with Earendil Labs (April 2025, two AI-derived bispecific antibodies for autoimmune/IBD indications), a bispecific pact with Dren Bio (~$600M upfront, 2025, est.), and a $1.53 billion global license (March 2026) for rovadicitinib, a first-in-class oral JAK/ROCK inhibitor from Sino Biopharm's Chia Tai Tianqing — a rare full-global-rights deal from a Chinese pharma. Management strategy under Garijo emphasizes R&D productivity, execution rigor, and governance; a €1 billion buyback was announced for 2026. Sanofi also reached an agreement with the US government in late 2025 to lower medicine costs (most-favored-nation-style pricing framework) while protecting innovation incentives.

## Key risks

- **Dupixent concentration and 2031 cliff**: Dupixent was ~36% of FY2025 sales (~40% of Q1 2026 sales); the US compound patent expires March 2031. Sanofi touts a "vigorous defense" and formulation IP out to 2045, but biosimilar developers are already mobilizing. Revenue at risk is on the order of €15B+/year.
- **Pipeline execution**: tolebrutinib's US complete response letter (Dec 2025) and PPMS failure, plus mixed itepekimab data, drove the abrupt CEO change; amlitelimab must convert trial wins into a franchise capable of absorbing part of the Dupixent gap.
- **Vaccine business under US policy pressure**: 2026 vaccine sales guided "slightly negative" partly due to US vaccine policy shifts and skepticism; flu pricing competition in Europe is intense.
- **US pricing / IRA**: insulins (Lantus/Toujeo) already face Medicare negotiation and $35 copay dynamics; the late-2025 US government pricing agreement and potential MFN mechanics compress US margins; ~51% of sales are US-based, adding tariff and policy exposure.
- **Residual litigation**: Zantac state-court cases continue against Sanofi even after 2024 settlements (~$300-350M total, est.) and favorable Delaware rulings (Dec 2025/Apr 2026); Praluent/patent and antitrust probes (EU Dupixent-related inquiry reported) linger.
- **Leadership transition**: Garijo inherits a strategy reset with an interim-CEO gap behind her and investor skepticism (shares fell ~6% on her appointment).
- **Legacy erosion**: Lantus, Aubagio, Lovenox, Plavix continue structural declines; Opella deconsolidation removes a stable cash stream (though 48.2% equity retained).
