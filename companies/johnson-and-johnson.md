## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

J&J's problem is arithmetic: Stelara fell from roughly $10.4B to $6.1B in a single year, and Darzalex — now over $14B — faces its own patents early in the 2030s. The answer management has chosen is breadth rather than one replacement blockbuster: an oral pill that does what an injected biologic does (icotrokinra), a drug-device that treats bladder cancer without removing the bladder (the TARIS platform), T-cell engagers aimed at prostate cancer and leukaemia, and a rebuilt neuroscience portfolio. Notably absent is any obesity programme — J&J has said openly it would rather spend the money on oncology, immunology and the brain.

## History: 1995-2025

### 1995-2005: Building the pharma engine
Through the late 1990s J&J's pharma arm (Janssen/Ortho-McNeil) rode Risperdal (risperidone), Procrit (epoetin alfa) and Duragesic. The pivotal move was the **1999 acquisition of Centocor (~$4.9B)**, bringing Remicade (infliximab), which became a multi-billion-dollar anchor of J&J immunology for two decades. **ALZA was acquired in 2001 (~$10.5B stock)** for drug-delivery technology, and **Scios in 2003 ($2.4B)** for Natrecor — later a partial failure after safety questions crushed sales. Setbacks: heartburn drug **Propulsid (cisapride) was withdrawn from the US market in 2000** after fatal arrhythmias, and Eprex faced pure red cell aplasia issues in Europe.

### 2006-2012: Diversification, recalls and patent cliff #1
J&J bought **Pfizer Consumer Healthcare (2006, $16.6B)**. Risperdal lost exclusivity (2007-08) and Topamax followed (2009) — the response was pipeline replenishment: **Cougar Biotech (2009, ~$970M)** delivered Zytiga (abiraterone, approved 2011); Stelara (2009), Simponi (2009), Invega Sustenna (2009), Xarelto (2011) and Zytiga launched in quick succession. The era was marred by the **2010-2011 McNeil consumer recalls** (Tylenol plant-quality failures, FDA consent decree) and the **DePuy ASR metal-on-metal hip recall (2010)**, and by the **2012 Phase 3 failure of bapineuzumab** in Alzheimer's. **Synthes (2012, $19.7B)** was the era's biggest deal (MedTech). In 2011 J&J licensed ibrutinib from Pharmacyclics (~$975M deal), one of the most lucrative in-licensing bets in industry history.

### 2013-2019: Oncology transformation
Imbruvica (2013) and **Darzalex (2015, licensed from Genmab)** transformed J&J into a top-tier oncology company; Darzalex became the biggest drug in company history. Stelara expanded into Crohn's (2016) and became a >$6B brand. **Actelion was acquired in 2017 for $30B** — J&J's largest-ever pharma deal — adding the pulmonary arterial hypertension franchise (Opsumit, Uptravi, Tracleer). Zytiga went generic (2018-19) and Remicade faced US biosimilars from 2016, both absorbed by portfolio growth. Litigation escalated: the **$4.69B Missouri talc verdict (2018)**, a **$8B punitive Risperdal gynecomastia verdict (2019, later slashed)**, and the **$572M Oklahoma opioid ruling (2019)**. Spravato (esketamine), approved 2019, was a genuine neuroscience first.

### 2020-2023: COVID, Kenvue split, and reinvention
J&J's single-shot **COVID-19 vaccine (EUA Feb 2021)** was a scientific success but a commercial disappointment — rare clotting events (TTS) triggered an April 2021 pause, and the vaccine was ultimately discontinued (2023). **Momenta (2020, $6.5B)** brought nipocalimab. Carvykti — developed with China's Legend Biotech (Nanjing) under a 2017 deal — was **approved in 2022**, becoming the industry's most successful CAR-T launch. J&J settled most nationwide opioid claims (~$5B over 9 years, 2021) and pursued the controversial "Texas two-step" talc bankruptcies (LTL Management, rejected twice). The defining structural move: **Kenvue, the consumer-health business, was spun off/IPO'd in 2023**, leaving a two-segment company; pharma was rebranded **"Johnson & Johnson Innovative Medicine" (2023)**.

### 2024-2025: The Stelara cliff and the rebuild
Stelara — once ~$11B/year — lost EU exclusivity in 2024 and US exclusivity in January 2025; FY2025 sales fell 41.3% to $6.08B, a ~1,040bp drag on segment growth. J&J still grew Innovative Medicine 6% to $60.4B in 2025 on Darzalex (>$14B), Tremfya (>$5B), Erleada, Carvykti and new launches. Deals accelerated: **Ambrx (2024, ~$2.0B, ADCs)**, **Proteologix (2024, $850M, bispecific antibodies)**, **Intra-Cellular Therapies (Jan 2025, $14.6B — Caplyta**, the largest pharma deal of 2025), **Halda Therapeutics (Nov 2025, $3.05B)**. The third talc bankruptcy (Red River Talc, $8-10B plan) was **rejected March 31, 2025**; J&J returned to the tort system and reversed ~$7B of reserves. FY2025 closed at $94.2B with 2026 guidance of ~$100.5B.

## Current strategic bets

- **Oncology depth (multiple myeloma + lung + prostate + bladder):** four MM franchises (Darzalex, Carvykti, Tecvayli, Talvey — ~80% of MM patients touch a J&J drug); Tecvayli+Darzalex Faspro data positioning bispecifics as early as 2L. Carvykti guided toward $5B+ peak. Rybrevant/Lazcluze (chemo-free EGFR NSCLC, OS benefit vs osimertinib; SC Faspro approved 2025). **Inlexzo/TAR-200** (approved 2025), an intravesical gemcitabine-releasing system for BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer — pitched as a $5B+ asset. Prostate: pasritamig (KLK2 T-cell engager, Phase 3) plus Halda's HLD-0915 (oral AR-BRD4 RIPTAC, Ph1/2) for resistance settings.
- **Immunology beyond Stelara:** Tremfya (>$10B ambition) and **icotrokinra** (oral IL-23R peptide, Protagonist-partnered; filed 2025 on four positive Ph3s incl. head-to-head superiority vs Sotyktu; approved US March 2026 as Icotyde; Ph3 UC/PsA ongoing). **Imaavy (nipocalimab)**, FcRn blocker approved April 2025 for gMG — "pipeline-in-a-product" across Sjogren's, wAIHA, HDFN, lupus.
- **Neuroscience rebuilt via M&A:** Caplyta (MDD adjunct approval 2025; $5B peak claim), Spravato, plus posdinemab/anti-tau and seltorexant (Ph3 MDD insomnia data positive 2024).
- **Modality bets:** cell therapy (Carvykti), bispecific T-cell engagers, targeted protein degradation/induced proximity (Halda RIPTAC), ADCs (Ambrx), oral peptides (icotrokinra), intravesical drug-device (TAR-200), FcRn. Notably **no obesity/GLP-1 position** — management has explicitly prioritized oncology/immunology/neuroscience instead.
- **Cardiovascular via MedTech** (Abiomed 2022 $16.6B, Shockwave 2024 $13.1B, V-Wave 2024) rather than pharma; milvexian (oral Factor XIa, BMS-partnered, Ph3) is the main CV pharma bet.
- **BD posture:** >$32B deployed in R&D + M&A in 2025; management targets 5-7% CAGR through 2030; 10 assets claimed with $5B+ peak potential (Talvey, Tecvayli, Imaavy, Caplyta, Inlexzo, Rybrevant/Lazcluze, Icotyde among them). China-origin exposure is mainly the Legend Biotech Carvykti partnership rather than recent in-licensing.

## Key risks

- **Patent cliffs:** Stelara erosion continuing (from ~$10.4B in 2024 to $6.1B in 2025, heading toward low single-digit billions); Xarelto US generics ~2027; Imbruvica declining on competition; Simponi and Invega LAI franchise eroding late-2020s; Darzalex Faspro patents to ~2031/32 — the next decade-defining cliff.
- **Pipeline/portfolio concentration:** Darzalex alone is ~15% of company revenue; multiple myeloma franchise concentration; Tremfya must keep absorbing Stelara switch volume.
- **IRA / pricing policy:** Stelara, Xarelto and Imbruvica selected for Medicare price negotiation (negotiated prices effective 2026); Part D redesign already denting Erleada/Stelara; broader US drug-pricing and tariff policy exposure.
- **Litigation:** ~60,000-67,000+ talc claims back in the tort system after the third bankruptcy rejection (Mar 2025); J&J reversed ~$7B reserves and vows to litigate (won 16 of 17 ovarian trials); press reports (July 2026) of a possible ~$5.5B settlement framework remain unfinalized. Residual opioid, Risperdal and DePuy tail risk.
- **Biosimilar execution risk:** Stelara biosimilars (Amgen, Teva, Samsung Bioepis/Sandoz et al.) eroding faster in US (−54.9% Q4 2025 US).
