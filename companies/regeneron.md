## History: 1995-2025

### 1988-2007: Long apprenticeship of a science-first biotech
Neurologist Leonard Schleifer founded Regeneron in Tarrytown, New York in 1988, recruiting Columbia scientist George Yancopoulos in 1989; the two have run the company together ever since — the longest-standing founder partnership among large-cap biopharma. The name reflects early ambitions in nerve regeneration, and the first two decades were commercially barren: neurotrophic-factor programs for ALS and obesity failed in the clinic (the Axokine obesity program collapsed in 2003). What the company built instead was platform technology — the "Trap" fusion-protein technology and later the VelociGene and VelocImmune mouse platforms for making fully human antibodies at speed, platforms so productive that Yancopoulos and his team are credited as principal inventors of all of the company's FDA-approved drugs. VelocImmune was also licensed to others (AstraZeneca, Astellas), and Sanofi signed a broad antibody discovery alliance in 2007 that would shape Regeneron's next fifteen years. The first approval, Arcalyst (rilonacept) for rare auto-inflammatory syndromes, came only in 2008 — twenty years after founding.

### 2008-2015: Eylea changes everything; the Sanofi antibody engine
Eylea (aflibercept), a VEGF-trap injected into the eye, was approved for wet age-related macular degeneration in November 2011 and became one of biotech's great launches, eventually exceeding $9B in annual global sales at peak (with Bayer commercializing outside the US). A sister molecule, Zaltrap (ziv-aflibercept, 2012, with Sanofi), flopped in colorectal cancer amid a pricing controversy. From the Sanofi alliance came Praluent (alirocumab), a PCSK9 cholesterol antibody approved in 2015 — first-to-market by weeks against Amgen's Repatha, triggering a decade of patent litigation in which Amgen sought to block Praluent entirely. Regeneron and Sanofi ultimately prevailed when the US Supreme Court's 2023 Amgen v. Sanofi decision invalidated Amgen's broad genus claims on enablement grounds. Commercially, however, Praluent disappointed badly against payer resistance. In 2014 Regeneron founded the Regeneron Genetics Center, which has since sequenced nearly three million volunteers linked to health records — the industrial-scale human-genetics engine behind many pipeline choices.

### 2015-2019: Dupixent era begins
Dupixent (dupilumab), the IL-4/IL-13-blocking antibody co-developed with Sanofi, was approved for atopic dermatitis in 2017 and grew into one of the industry's biggest franchises, adding asthma, nasal polyps, eosinophilic esophagitis and eventually COPD (2024) — the first biologic ever approved for COPD. Kevzara (sarilumab, 2017) and Libtayo (cemiplimab, 2018), a PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor born of a 2015 Sanofi immuno-oncology pact, followed. Sanofi sold its ~$13B equity stake in Regeneron in May 2020, but the product collaborations continued.

### 2020-2023: COVID windfall and portfolio consolidation
REGEN-COV (casirivimab + imdevimab), a VelocImmune-derived antibody cocktail for COVID-19, received emergency authorization in November 2020 (famously given to President Trump) and drove Regeneron's revenue to a $16.1B peak in 2021 before Omicron rendered it ineffective and the FDA pulled its authorization in January 2022. Regeneron used the cash to consolidate: buying out Sanofi's Libtayo rights for $900M upfront in 2022, acquiring Checkmate Pharmaceuticals (2022, ~$250M) and hearing-focused Decibel Therapeutics (2023, up to ~$213M with CVRs), and deepening genetic-medicine alliances with Alnylam (siRNA) and Intellia (CRISPR). Eylea HD, the 8 mg dose allowing longer intervals between injections, was approved in August 2023 — the pre-planned answer to looming Eylea patent expiry.

### 2023-2025: The biosimilar siege and the pivot beyond Eylea
Aflibercept biosimilars arrived: after Regeneron failed to win an injunction, Amgen launched Pavblu in October 2024, and 2025 settlements with Sandoz, Formycon, Celltrion, and Alvotech/Teva cleared a wave of further US entrants for Q4 2026. Combined US Eylea/Eylea HD sales fell 27% in 2025 to $4.4B, squeezed also by Roche's Vabysmo and cheap compounded bevacizumab. Meanwhile Dupixent (+26% to $17.8B) more than compensated at the profit line. 2025 was eventful: a first-ever dividend (February); mixed Phase 3 COPD results for itepekimab (May); the Hansoh obesity in-licensing (June); an aborted $256M purchase of bankrupt 23andMe (outbid in July); Lynozyfic's US approval (July); a striking Phase 3 win for garetosmab in the ultra-rare bone disease FOP (September); Libtayo's adjuvant CSCC approval (October); and repeated manufacturing-related FDA rejections of the Eylea HD prefilled syringe traced to a Catalent/Novo Nordisk plant. FY2025 revenue: $14.3B, R&D spend $5.9B (~41% of revenue).

## Current strategic bets

Regeneron enters 2026 arguing that four blockbusters (Dupixent, Eylea HD, Eylea, Libtayo) fund an unusually broad, mostly homegrown pipeline of ~50 clinical candidates, with R&D spend guided to ~$6.6B in 2026.

- **Defend the retina franchise.** Eylea HD gained RVO and monthly-dosing approvals (Nov 2025) and an EU RVO approval (Jan 2026); a prefilled syringe (via a new fill-finish partner) is the key 2026 catalyst after repeated CRLs. Q2 2026 Eylea HD US sales hit a record $596M (+52%).
- **Obesity, "quality of weight loss."** A differentiated combination strategy: trevogrumab (anti-GDF8/myostatin) ± garetosmab on top of semaglutide (Phase 2 COURAGE) to preserve muscle, plus the China-originated olatorepatide (HS-20094), a dual GLP-1/GIP agonist in-licensed from Hansoh Pharma (June 2025; $80M upfront, up to $1.93B milestones) that showed up to ~19% weight loss in a China Phase 3; global Phase 3 planned for 2026.
- **Immunology beyond Dupixent.** Itepekimab (IL-33) in COPD gave split AERIFY results (one hit, one miss, May 2025) — additional Phase 3 work underway plus bronchiectasis and rhinosinusitis; allergy bets include birch (REGN5713-5715) and cat allergen antibodies.
- **Oncology depth.** Fianlimab (LAG-3) + Libtayo Phase 3 in melanoma (readout 2025/2026); bispecific franchise building with Lynozyfic (BCMAxCD3, approved), Ordspono (CD20xCD3, EU approved; US resubmission), vonsetamig, ubamatamab (MUC16xCD3), and costimulatory bispecifics.
- **Genetic medicines.** DB-OTO (lunsotogene parvec), an AAV gene therapy restoring hearing in children with otoferlin mutations — BLA filed Dec 2025 with one of FDA's first National Priority Vouchers, decision expected H1 2026; garetosmab BLA for FOP under priority review; cemdisiran (C5 siRNA, with Alnylam) filed in myasthenia gravis (2026); Alnylam CNS siRNA programs (SOD1, HTT, synuclein, MAPT), Intellia CRISPR TTR program (nexiguran ziclumeran), factor XI antibodies, and a Tessera gene-editing collaboration (Jan 2026).
- **Capital & infrastructure.** ~$9B committed to US manufacturing/R&D expansion, including a ~$2B Saratoga Springs, NY bulk biologics plant; dividend + buybacks ($3.8B returned in 2025); Sanofi development balance fully repaid by mid-2026, boosting collaboration economics.

## Key risks

- **Eylea cliff, happening now**: US franchise fell 27% to $4.4B in 2025; settlements open the door to Sandoz, Formycon, Celltrion, Alvotech/Teva and others from Q4 2026, on top of Amgen's Pavblu (launched Oct 2024), Vabysmo competition, and compounded bevacizumab. Original Eylea (~$2.8B US, 2025) is largely at risk; the franchise defense depends on Eylea HD conversion.
- **Dupixent concentration and dependence on Sanofi**: Dupixent drives the majority of Regeneron's profit growth (Sanofi collaboration revenue $5.2B in 2025), but Sanofi records sales and co-controls the program; any IP, pricing, or partnership friction is a concentrated exposure. Key IP runs to ~2031+ (est.).
- **Manufacturing execution**: repeated Eylea HD prefilled-syringe CRLs tied to the Catalent Indiana (Novo Nordisk) site ceded ground to Vabysmo in 2024-2025; remediation and alternate fillers are in motion but not complete.
- **US pricing policy / IRA**: Eylea's Medicare Part B exposure and potential future negotiation selection of Dupixent, plus most-favored-nation pricing pressure across the industry (exposure noted; specifics unconfirmed, est.).
- **Pipeline binary risk**: itepekimab's split Phase 3s show the replacement-engine risk; fianlimab, obesity combos and gene therapies are unproven commercially.
- **Litigation**: aflibercept biosimilar IP litigation largely settled in 2025; a long-running DOJ False Claims Act case over Eylea copay-foundation support remains ongoing (est.); dual-class-like founder voting control is a governance consideration.
