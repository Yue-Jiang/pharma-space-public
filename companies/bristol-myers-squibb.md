## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

Bristol Myers Squibb spends the rest of this decade handing Eliquis, Opdivo and Revlimid to generic competitors, so every asset below is judged on whether it can arrive in time. 2026 was built as the answer: six registrational readouts in a single year, and the first two of a new class of oral protein degraders — CELMoDs — reaching the FDA, one of them already approved. Beyond the degraders the company is betting on cell therapy pushed out of cancer into autoimmune disease, on radiopharmaceuticals, and on two large oncology programmes it licensed from Chinese biotechs rather than discovered itself.

## History: 1995-2025

### 1995-2001: Blockbuster era and the Sanofi alliance
Through the late 1990s BMS rode Pravachol (statin), Taxol (paclitaxel, commercialized from an NIH CRADA), Glucophage, and — via a 1997-launched alliance with Sanofi — Plavix (clopidogrel) and Avapro. In 2001 BMS bought DuPont Pharmaceuticals for $7.8B, sold Clairol to P&G for ~$5B, and paid ~$2B for a 20% stake in ImClone Systems to co-develop Erbitux. The FDA's December 2001 refusal-to-file for Erbitux triggered the ImClone insider-trading scandal (Sam Waksal, Martha Stewart) and a large writedown for BMS.

### 2002-2006: Accounting scandal and the Plavix crisis
In 2002-2003 BMS admitted to "channel stuffing" — inflating revenue by ~$2.5B via wholesaler inventory loading — restated results, paid a $150M SEC settlement (2004), ~$300M to shareholders, and operated under a deferred prosecution agreement (2005). In August 2006 Apotex launched generic clopidogrel "at risk" after a botched settlement negotiation, flooding the market before an injunction; CEO Peter Dolan was ousted in September 2006. BMS later won the Plavix patent trial, preserving exclusivity to 2012.

### 2007-2012: "String of Pearls" and the biopharma pivot
Under Jim Cornelius and then Lamberto Andreotti, BMS shed non-pharma businesses (ConvaTec sold for $4.1B in 2008; Mead Johnson nutrition IPO'd 2009 and fully split off) and executed the "String of Pearls" bolt-on strategy. The defining pearl: **Medarex, acquired 2009 for $2.4B**, bringing ipilimumab (Yervoy, approved 2011 — the first checkpoint inhibitor) and nivolumab (Opdivo). Misses included Inhibitex ($2.5B, 2012), whose hepatitis C nucleotide BMS-986094 was halted after a patient death, forcing a $1.8B writedown, and Amylin ($5.3B, 2012, diabetes, with AstraZeneca) — the diabetes business was sold to AZ in 2014. Plavix's May 2012 US patent cliff erased ~$6.5B of annual US sales; Abilify (Otsuka partnership) and Eliquis (approved Dec 2012) picked up the load.

### 2013-2018: Immuno-oncology leadership — and the Keytruda stumble
Opdivo's December 2014 US approval put BMS first in PD-1 (with Merck's Keytruda close behind). Opdivo dominated 2L cancers, but the August 2016 failure of CheckMate-026 — testing Opdivo monotherapy in first-line NSCLC with a broad PD-L1 cutoff — ceded the largest immuno-oncology market to Keytruda, a strategic wound from which Opdivo never fully recovered. Eliquis scaled toward category leadership in anticoagulation.

### 2019-2022: Celgene — the $74B bet
In November 2019 BMS closed the **$74B acquisition of Celgene**, then the largest pure-pharma deal ever, acquiring Revlimid, Pomalyst, Abraxane, Reblozyl, two CAR-Ts (Breyanzi and Abecma, both approved 2021) and the CELMoD protein-degradation platform; Otezla was divested to Amgen for $13.4B to clear antitrust. BMS layered on **MyoKardia ($13.1B, 2020)** for mavacamten (Camzyos, approved 2022) and **Turning Point Therapeutics ($4.1B, 2022)** for repotrectinib (Augtyro). Revlimid's volume-limited generics began March 2022, starting a multi-year erosion. 2022 brought three launches: Opdualag, Camzyos, Sotyktu.

### 2023-2025: The next cliff and the Boerner rebuild
Christopher Boerner became CEO in November 2023 and immediately executed a ~$23B deal spree: **Mirati ($4.8B + $1B CVR, Krazati)**, **Karuna Therapeutics ($14.0B, KarXT)**, **RayzeBio ($4.1B, actinium-225 radiopharmaceuticals)**, plus an ex-China license to SystImmune's EGFR×HER3 bispecific ADC iza-bren ($800M upfront, up to $8.4B). Cobenfy (KarXT) was approved September 2024 — the first genuinely new schizophrenia mechanism in decades — but its 2025 launch was slow ($155M) and the ARISE adjunctive-schizophrenia trial missed in April 2025, as did Camzyos in non-obstructive HCM (ODYSSEY-HCM) and Opdualag in adjuvant melanoma. BMS ran a ~$3B cost-cutting program through 2027 and in 2025 signed the **BioNTech BNT327 co-development deal (up to $11.1B)**, acquired 2seventy bio (~$286M, consolidating Abecma) and Orbital Therapeutics ($1.5B, RNA/in vivo CAR-T), and agreed to Most-Favored-Nation Medicare pricing terms with the US administration. FY2025 revenue held flat at $48.2B as the Growth Portfolio (+17%) offset Revlimid/Pomalyst collapse.

## Current strategic bets

**2026 is the pivotal data year.** One major event has landed: Zenbexus (iberdomide) received FDA accelerated approval on August 13 after EXCALIBER-RRMM showed a higher minimal-residual-disease-negative complete-response rate for the iberdomide regimen than for the comparator. Continued approval may depend on confirmatory clinical benefit. The remaining 2026-2027 slate includes milvexian (secondary stroke prevention; AF pushed to Q1 2027), admilparant (IPF, ALOFT-IPF), mezigdomide (SUCCESSOR-2, positive interim reported), arlo-cel (QUINTESSENTIAL), RYZ101 (ACTION-1, GEP-NETs), and Sotyktu in lupus (POETYK SLE-1/2).

**Modality bets:** targeted protein degradation (CELMoDs), radiopharmaceuticals (RayzeBio), cell therapy including in-vivo CAR-T (Orbital's RNA platform), bispecific antibodies and ADCs. Two of the biggest oncology bets are **China-originated**: iza-bren (SystImmune/Biokin) and BNT327 (originally Biotheus, acquired by BioNTech). Therapeutic priorities: oncology/hematology, neuroscience (Cobenfy franchise), cardiovascular (milvexian, Camzyos LCM), immunology (Sotyktu LCM, zola-cel). Management strategy: >$30B of BD since 2023, ~$3B cost reduction, and translating a "data-rich" 2026 pipeline into growth that outruns the 2028-2030 LOE wave.

## Key risks

- **Eliquis cliff:** $14.4B (30% of revenue). EU LOE 2026 (management guides a $1.5-2B step-down in 2027); US LOE ~April 2028. Also first-cycle IRA "negotiated" Medicare price effective Jan 2026 (~56% list discount).
- **Opdivo cliff:** ~$10B franchise faces US biosimilars ~late 2028; mitigation via subcutaneous Opdivo Qvantig conversion is unproven at scale.
- **Residual legacy erosion:** Revlimid + Pomalyst + Sprycel + Abraxane (~$6.5B in 2025) continue declining 30-55%/yr.
- **Pipeline concentration/execution:** growth case leans heavily on unproven launches (Cobenfy, whose adjunctive-schizophrenia trial already missed) and 2026-2027 binary readouts (milvexian, admilparant, CELMoDs); Camzyos and Opdualag expansion trials failed in 2025.
- **Pricing/policy:** IRA negotiation hits Eliquis (2026) then Pomalyst; Orencia selected for later cycles; MFN pricing agreement with US government adds further US price pressure; ~69% of revenue is US-based.
- **Balance sheet:** ~$50B debt load from the Celgene/Karuna era constrains further large M&A.
- **Litigation:** the Second Circuit vacated the dismissal of the Celgene contingent-value-rights suit on August 13, 2026 and remanded it. The ruling addressed the trustee's capacity to sue and BMS's waiver or ratification of that challenge, not whether BMS breached the agreement's diligent-efforts obligation. The multi-billion-dollar merits dispute over the missed Breyanzi approval deadline is active again; routine IP challenges continue across the Growth Portfolio.
