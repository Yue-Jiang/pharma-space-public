## History: 1995-2025

### 1995-2009: From mid-tier German family firm to primary-care powerhouse
Founded in 1885 by Albert Boehringer in Ingelheim am Rhein, the company remained family-controlled through the twentieth century — a fact that shapes everything about how it operates. Through the late 1990s BI built a strong primary-care portfolio: Micardis (telmisartan) for hypertension, Mirapex (pramipexole) for Parkinson's disease, Flomax (tamsulosin) for benign prostatic hyperplasia, and the COPD franchise that would define the next decade. In 2002 BI launched Spiriva (tiotropium), a once-daily inhaled bronchodilator co-promoted with Pfizer, which became the world's leading COPD medicine and BI's first multi-billion-euro blockbuster. A consumer health business (Dulcolax, Buscopan, Mucosolvan) rounded out the group alongside a small animal-health unit and a biologics contract-manufacturing arm (later branded BioXcellence) that made BI a major CDMO decades before that was fashionable.

### 2010-2015: Pradaxa's rise and stumble; the Lilly alliance pays off
In 2010 the FDA approved Pradaxa (dabigatran etexilate), the first of the novel oral anticoagulants to challenge warfarin in atrial fibrillation. Sales ramped quickly, but so did reports of severe bleeding in a drug that initially had no reversal agent. In May 2014 BI paid $650 million to settle roughly 4,000 US federal and state lawsuits over Pradaxa bleeding injuries, while continuing to defend the drug's benefit-risk profile. The company answered the scientific criticism directly: Praxbind (idarucizumab), a specific reversal antidote, won FDA approval in October 2015. Meanwhile a 2011 diabetes alliance with Eli Lilly delivered Trajenta (linagliptin) in 2011 and Jardiance (empagliflozin) in 2014. The 2015 EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial made Jardiance the first glucose-lowering drug to show a reduction in cardiovascular death — a landmark that transformed SGLT2 inhibitors from diabetes drugs into cardio-renal medicines. October 2014 brought a second franchise-defining approval: Ofev (nintedanib) for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, establishing BI as the leader in pulmonary fibrosis. The same years saw the patent cliff of Micardis (2014) and the start of Spiriva's long erosion.

### 2016-2020: The great Sanofi swap and portfolio focus
In 2016 BI and Sanofi agreed one of the industry's largest asset swaps: BI's consumer health business (enterprise value €6.7B) went to Sanofi in exchange for Sanofi's Merial animal-health unit (€11.4B), with BI paying €4.7B cash to balance the ~€22.8B ($25B) transaction. Closing in January 2017, the deal made BI the world's second-largest animal-health company (behind Zoetis) — brands like NexGard, Frontline and Heartgard joined Vetmedin — and completed BI's transformation into a two-pillar company: innovative human pharma plus animal health, with no consumer business. Prescription growth now rode on Jardiance and Ofev as Spiriva and Pradaxa aged. BI also kept building its biosimilars capability, winning FDA approval in 2017 for Cyltezo (adalimumab-adbm), which in 2021 became the first interchangeable adalimumab biosimilar (US launch 2023).

### 2021-2024: Pipeline rebuild — ADCs, immunology, obesity
With its big brands maturing, BI spent the early 2020s buying and partnering for innovation: NBE-Therapeutics (antibody-drug conjugates, ~$1.5B, closed 2021, est.), T3 Pharmaceuticals (bacteria-based cancer therapies, ~CHF 450M, 2023, est.), plus platform deals (Synaffix ADC technology, 2025). Spevigo (spesolimab), the first IL-36 receptor antibody, was approved in 2022 for generalized pustular psoriasis. The obesity wave reached BI through its long-standing Zealand Pharma collaboration: survodutide, a glucagon/GLP-1 receptor dual agonist licensed from Zealand, posted striking Phase 2 weight-loss and MASH data in 2023-2024 and moved into a broad Phase 3 program. In 2024 BI announced plans for 25 new launches over the next decade.

### 2025: Generational handover and two launches
2025 was a pivot year. In January the Phase 3 CONNEX program of iclepertin, a GlyT1 inhibitor for cognitive impairment in schizophrenia (1,840 patients, 41 countries), missed all primary and secondary endpoints — a major CNS setback. But the rest of the year delivered: Hernexeos (zongertinib), the first oral targeted therapy for previously treated HER2-mutant non-small cell lung cancer, was FDA-approved and launched in the US in August 2025 (also cleared in China); Jascayd (nerandomilast), a PDE4B inhibitor and the first new IPF therapy in over a decade, was approved in the US and China in October 2025 and added progressive pulmonary fibrosis in December. Governance changed generations too: on July 1, 2025 Christian Boehringer stepped down after 18 years chairing the Shareholders' Committee, succeeded by Hubertus von Baumbach, while Shashank Deshpande — the first non-family chairman in recent memory to come up through operations in the US, Japan and Germany — took over as Chairman of the Board of Managing Directors. FY2025 closed at €27.8B (+7.3% adj.), with Jardiance at €8.8B and R&D spend at a sector-leading 22.9% of sales.

## Current strategic bets

BI's stated ambition is roughly 25 new launches across the decade to 2035, funded by private-ownership patience and a 20%+ R&D ratio. The 2025-2026 priorities:

- **Obesity/MASH — survodutide.** The glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonist (licensed from Zealand Pharma) read out Phase 3 SYNCHRONIZE-1 in April 2026: 16.6% mean weight loss vs 3.2% placebo at 76 weeks, with data suggesting relatively preserved lean mass and up to 63% liver-fat reduction. Two Phase 3 MASH trials (LIVERAGE, ~1,800 pts; LIVERAGE-Cirrhosis, ~1,590 pts) are ongoing, with FDA Breakthrough status in F2/F3 MASH. BI is a late entrant vs Lilly/Novo in obesity but is positioning survodutide on liver and visceral-fat differentiation; a triple GLP-1/GIP/NPY2 agonist (BI 3034701) enters Phase 2 in mid-2026.
- **Oncology — zongertinib expansion and next waves.** Hernexeos is in a global Phase 3 for first-line HER2-mutant NSCLC (77% response rate in treatment-naïve Phase 1b data). Behind it: ADC platforms (NBE, Synaffix), T-cell engagers, and T3 Pharma's bacterial delivery approach.
- **Fibrosis franchise defense.** Jascayd is explicitly the Ofev succession plan ahead of Ofev's late-decade LOE, launched first in the US and China with EU review ongoing (est.).
- **Immunology via China-originated BD.** In January 2026 BI licensed SIM0709, a preclinical TL1A×IL-23 bispecific for inflammatory bowel disease, from Shanghai-based Simcere for up to ~€1.06B (worldwide ex-Greater China rights) — part of a broader pattern of European pharma sourcing candidates from Chinese biotechs. Earlier 2025 deals: Cue Biopharma CUE-501 B-cell depleter for autoimmunity ($12M upfront, ~$345M milestones), Re-Vana ocular sustained-delivery (> $1B potential), and out-licensing Spevigo to LEO Pharma (June 2025) to focus resources.
- **Portfolio pruning and manufacturing.** The halt of the inhaled cystic fibrosis gene therapy BI 3720931 (2026) and the iclepertin discontinuation show willingness to cut. Meanwhile BI is investing in manufacturing (new Japan facility; ~$314M Yamagata expansion) and remains a top-tier biologics CDMO via BioXcellence, including in Shanghai.

## Key risks

- **Jardiance cliff and IRA squeeze.** €8.8B (~32% of group sales) faces a Medicare-negotiated price from January 2026 (empagliflozin was in the first Part D negotiation cohort) and estimated US generic entry ~2029; some patents run into the 2030s but the base case is a steep post-2029 erosion. Combined with Ofev's late-decade LOE (~€3.8B), roughly 45% of 2025 human-pharma revenue is at risk within ~5 years.
- **Two-product concentration.** Jardiance + Ofev = €12.6B of €22.7B Human Pharma sales (2025). New launches (Hernexeos, Jascayd) address smaller populations and will ramp slowly; survodutide enters a brutally competitive obesity market years behind Lilly and Novo.
- **Obesity execution risk.** Survodutide's weight loss (~16.6%) trails tirzepatide; Phase 2 discontinuation rates (~25%) raised tolerability questions that topline Phase 3 disclosure did not fully resolve. MASH differentiation is promising but unproven commercially.
- **Litigation/regulatory history.** Pradaxa bleeding litigation cost $650M (2014); as a private company BI has capacity to absorb shocks but less currency (no stock) for large M&A. US tariff and drug-pricing policy uncertainty has made the company notably cautious in guidance.
- **CNS/pipeline attrition.** The iclepertin Phase 3 failure (Jan 2025) and the CF gene-therapy halt (2026) underline attrition risk in the "25 launches" plan.
