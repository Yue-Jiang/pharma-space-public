## History: 1995-2025

### Antiviral foundations (1987-2000)
Gilead was founded in 1987 by Michael Riordan, a 29-year-old physician, and built its identity around nucleotide antivirals. Its first commercial breakthroughs were modest — Vistide (cidofovir) for CMV retinitis in 1996 — but two events that year mattered more: John Martin became CEO, and Gilead licensed its neuraminidase inhibitor oseltamivir to Roche, which marketed it as Tamiflu and paid royalties for decades. The 1999 acquisition of NeXstar Pharmaceuticals (~$550M, est.) brought AmBisome (liposomal amphotericin B) and real commercial infrastructure. Donald Rumsfeld chaired the board from 1997 until joining the Bush administration in 2001 — a recurring source of Tamiflu-era controversy.

### Building the HIV empire (2001-2010)
Viread (tenofovir disoproxil fumarate, TDF) launched in 2001 and became the backbone of modern HIV therapy. Gilead bought Triangle Pharmaceuticals for ~$464M (2003) to get emtricitabine (Emtriva), then executed the single-tablet-regimen playbook: Truvada (2004), and Atripla (2006, with Bristol Myers Squibb) — the first complete once-daily HIV regimen in one pill. Diversification attempts followed — Myogen ($2.5B, 2006, pulmonary hypertension drug Letairis) and CV Therapeutics ($1.4B, 2009, angina drug Ranexa) — but HIV drove the business. Truvada's 2012 approval for pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) later created an entirely new prevention market.

### The Sovaldi decade: triumph, then gravity (2011-2016)
In November 2011 Gilead paid $11.2B for Pharmasset — a company with no revenue — for its hepatitis C nucleotide sofosbuvir. Wall Street balked; it became one of the best acquisitions in pharma history. Sovaldi (sofosbuvir) launched in December 2013 at $84,000 per course ("the $1,000 pill"), triggering a Senate Finance Committee investigation and a national debate about curative-drug pricing. Harvoni (sofosbuvir/ledipasvir) followed in 2014. HCV revenue peaked around $19B in 2015, lifting total revenue to ~$32.6B — then collapsed, because a cure shrinks its own market and AbbVie's competition compressed prices. Meanwhile Gilead pivoted its HIV franchise from TDF to the safer tenofovir alafenamide (TAF): Genvoya (2015), Descovy and Odefsey (2016), Vemlidy (2016) — resetting the patent clock ahead of Truvada's 2020-2021 LOE.

### Buying growth, mixed results (2017-2019)
With HCV deflating, Gilead paid $11.9B for Kite Pharma (2017), entering CAR-T cell therapy with Yescarta (approved October 2017). Biktarvy launched in 2018 and became the best-selling HIV drug in history. CEO John Milligan (2016-2018) departed after strategic drift; Roche's Daniel O'Day took over in March 2019. That year Gilead paid $5.1B for an expanded Galapagos collaboration (filgotinib) — which unraveled after a 2020 FDA rejection in rheumatoid arthritis — and its NASH candidate selonsertib failed twice in Phase 3.

### O'Day era: COVID windfall and the oncology push (2020-2024)
2020 was frenetic: Veklury (remdesivir) became the first approved COVID-19 antiviral (peaking at $5.6B in 2021); Gilead bought Forty Seven ($4.9B, anti-CD47 magrolimab — discontinued 2024 after safety and futility failures) and Immunomedics ($21B) for the Trop-2 ADC Trodelvy — its biggest deal ever. MYR GmbH (~$1.4B, 2021) added Hepcludex. Trodelvy became a blockbuster in breast cancer but stumbled elsewhere: the accelerated bladder-cancer approval was withdrawn in 2024, and lung-cancer trials disappointed. CymaBay ($4.3B, Feb 2024) delivered Livdelzi (seladelpar) for primary biliary cholangitis, approved August 2024. Litigation shadowed the era: Gilead won a 2023 jury verdict against the US government over CDC's Truvada-for-PrEP patents, and in June 2024 agreed to pay $40M to settle ~2,625 federal claims that it delayed safer TAF drugs to maximize TDF profits (California state cases continue).

### Lenacapavir's moment (2025)
Yeztugo (lenacapavir), a twice-yearly injectable capsid inhibitor, was approved for PrEP in June 2025 after near-100% efficacy in the PURPOSE trials — Science's 2024 "Breakthrough of the Year." Priced ~$28,000/year (est.) in the US, it booked $150M in its half-year launch; Gilead signed a no-profit Global Fund supply deal for up to 2M people, voluntary generic licenses covering 120 low- and middle-income countries, and $40/year generics arrive from 2027. FY2025 revenue was $29.4B (+2%), with HIV up 6% to $20.8B. In October 2025 Biktarvy patent settlements pushed earliest US generics to April 2036, and in December 2025 Gilead signed a most-favored-nation pricing agreement with the Trump administration (Medicaid MFN pricing, Epclusa direct-to-consumer, three-year tariff exclusion).

## Current strategic bets

Gilead enters 2026 with what management calls its strongest pipeline ever and no major loss of exclusivity expected until 2036 — a rare cliff-free decade it is using to build beyond HIV.

**HIV long-acting franchise.** The lenacapavir platform is the core bet: Yeztugo PrEP uptake (Citi models ~$2.8B by 2030, est.), a once-daily bictegravir/lenacapavir combination for treatment (potential launch late 2026), the first once-weekly oral regimen (islatravir + lenacapavir, partnered with Merck, filing toward 2027), and once-yearly lenacapavir formulations in development. GS-1720/GS-4182 (weekly oral) hit an FDA clinical hold in early 2026, a watch item.

**Oncology scale-up.** Nearly $13B of M&A in early 2026: Arcellx ($7.8B, closed April 2026) for anito-cel (anitocabtagene autoleucel), a BCMA CAR-T for multiple myeloma with a December 23, 2026 PDUFA date; Ouro Medicines ($2.2B, March 2026) for T-cell engagers; Tubulis ($3.15B upfront, up to $5B) for next-generation ADC platforms (lead asset GS-8824/TUB-040 in ovarian cancer). Trodelvy's first-line metastatic triple-negative breast cancer approval landed in 2026 (sales +26% YoY in Q2 2026). Kite is also pursuing in vivo CAR-T.

**Other.** Liver disease (Livdelzi expansion, Hepcludex US launch 2026) and early inflammation bets via licensing (LEO Pharma STAT6, Kymera degraders, Terray AI discovery — values est.). Notably, unlike most large-cap peers, Gilead has done little China-originated in-licensing; its BD centers on US/EU biotech.

## Key risks

- **HIV concentration:** $20.8B of $28.9B product sales (~72%) from HIV; Biktarvy alone is ~49% of product sales — franchise pricing/policy shocks hit hard.
- **2036 cliff:** Biktarvy US LOE (Apr 2036) is distant but enormous; TAF-family products (Descovy, Genvoya, Odefsey, Vemlidy — roughly $6-7B combined, est.) face LOE ~2031-2032.
- **US pricing/policy:** Medicare Part D redesign cost ~$1.1B in 2025; December 2025 MFN agreement (Medicaid pricing, MFN pricing on future launches); potential IRA negotiation exposure; ACA subsidy expiration and public-health/PrEP funding cuts could dent Yeztugo/Descovy.
- **Oncology execution:** Yescarta/Tecartus eroding under bispecific and in-class competition (-7% in 2025); Trodelvy's trial record outside breast cancer is mixed; anito-cel enters against entrenched Carvykti.
- **Litigation:** TDF product-liability cases continue in California state court after the $40M federal settlement (2024); routine ANDA challenges.
- **Veklury decay:** COVID revenue fell 49% to $911M in 2025 and remains unpredictable.
