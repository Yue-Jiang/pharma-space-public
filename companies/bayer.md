## History: 1995-2025

### 1995-2005: Diversified giant, statin disaster
Bayer entered the late 1990s as the archetypal German chemicals-and-pharma conglomerate, still best known for inventing Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) in 1897. The era's defining pharma event was a failure: in August 2001 Bayer withdrew its statin Lipobay/Baycol (cerivastatin) worldwide after reports of fatal rhabdomyolysis linked to roughly 100 deaths. Litigation cost well over $1 billion in settlements (est.) and knocked Bayer out of the top tier of pharma just as competitors consolidated. The company restructured into a holding with legally separate subgroups (HealthCare, CropScience, MaterialScience) in 2002-2003 and bought Roche's consumer health business in 2004, doubling down on OTC brands.

### 2006-2013: The Schering rebuild
In 2006 Bayer executed the biggest acquisition in its history to that point: Berlin-based Schering AG, for €86 per share — a transaction of roughly €16.5-17 billion — outbidding Merck KGaA. Schering brought women's health (Yasmin oral contraceptives, the Mirena intrauterine system), multiple sclerosis drug Betaferon, oncology and the radiology contrast-media franchise, and shifted Bayer Pharma decisively toward specialty medicine. Organic R&D then delivered a genuine blockbuster: Xarelto (rivaroxaban), an oral Factor Xa inhibitor discovered in Wuppertal, launched 2008 in Europe with Johnson & Johnson holding US rights. In 2011-2012 Bayer secured ex-US rights to Regeneron's Eylea (aflibercept), building an ophthalmology franchise. The period also seeded later trouble: Yasmin/YAZ venous-thromboembolism litigation cost about $2 billion (est.), and the 2013 acquisition of Conceptus (~$1.1 billion) brought the Essure contraceptive implant, later withdrawn amid injury claims settled for about $1.6 billion in 2020.

### 2014-2017: Doubling down on life sciences
Bayer bought Norway's Algeta (~$2.9 billion, 2014) for prostate-cancer radiopharmaceutical Xofigo (radium-223), and Merck & Co.'s consumer care business ($14.2 billion, 2014), becoming a top-tier OTC player. In 2015 it spun off MaterialScience as Covestro, completing the pivot to pure life sciences. Then came the fateful move: in 2016 Bayer agreed to acquire Monsanto for $128 per share — $63 billion — to create the world's largest seeds-and-crop-chemicals company.

### 2018-2022: Monsanto closes, glyphosate consumes the company
The Monsanto deal closed in June 2018. Within weeks, a California jury found for a plaintiff claiming Roundup (glyphosate) caused his non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Verdicts snowballed; more than 100,000 claims accumulated, and Bayer's market value at times fell below what it had paid for Monsanto. In 2020 Bayer agreed to pay up to $10.9 billion to settle the bulk of Roundup claims (plus ~$400M dicamba, ~$820M PCB, ~$1.6B Essure). CEO Werner Baumann survived an unprecedented 2019 shareholder no-confidence vote. To fund the mess, Animal Health was sold to Elanco for $7.6 billion (2020). Quietly, pharma rebuilt its pipeline: full ownership of BlueRock Therapeutics (stem cells, 2019, ~$600M for the remaining stake, est.), AskBio (AAV gene therapy, 2020, $2 billion upfront plus up to $2 billion milestones), KaNDy Therapeutics (elinzanetant, 2020, £425M upfront, est.), and Vividion Therapeutics (chemoproteomics, 2021, $1.5 billion). Nubeqa (darolutamide, with Orion) launched 2019; Kerendia (finerenone) and Verquvo (vericiguat, with Merck & Co.) followed in 2021.

### 2023-2025: Anderson's turnaround under twin cliffs
Bill Anderson (ex-Roche) became CEO in June 2023 with a mandate to contain litigation and revive performance. Five months in, disaster: the Phase III OCEANIC-AF trial of Factor XIa inhibitor asundexian — once billed as a €5 billion peak-sales asset — was stopped for lack of efficacy versus apixaban, and the stock hit multi-decade lows. Anderson responded with "Dynamic Shared Ownership," stripping management layers (thousands of roles cut, ~€2 billion savings targeted by end-2026), slashed the dividend ~95% to the legal minimum for three years, and — after review — kept the three-division conglomerate together. Meanwhile Xarelto's European patents expired (sales fell from €4.1B in 2023 to €2.34B in 2025) and Eylea met biosimilars. The response was execution: Nubeqa grew 57% to €2.4 billion in 2025, Kerendia 79% to €829 million (adding a US heart-failure indication in July 2025), and three approvals landed in 2025 — Beyonttra (acoramidis, EU, licensed from BridgeBio for $130M upfront, est.), Lynkuet (elinzanetant, FDA October 2025) and Hyrnuo (sevabertinib, FDA accelerated approval November 2025). Asundexian was resurrected by the positive Phase III OCEANIC-STROKE readout (secondary stroke prevention), announced at the turn of 2025/2026. On litigation, February 2026 brought a proposed $7.25 billion Roundup class settlement to resolve current and future claims, and on June 25, 2026 Monsanto won its landmark Roundup case at the US Supreme Court, which limited the state failure-to-warn cancer suits at the heart of the litigation — a win that may finally cap the tail risk.

## Current strategic bets

Bayer Pharma's 2025-2026 strategy is to bridge the Xarelto/Eylea contraction (management guides a "modest" base-business decline in 2026, returning to mid-single-digit growth in 2027) with four launch/growth assets — Nubeqa, Kerendia, Beyonttra, Lynkuet — plus asundexian as the next potential blockbuster after its OCEANIC-STROKE win (US Fast Track; filing expected 2026, est.). Sevabertinib (Hyrnuo) is being pushed into first-line HER2-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (FDA Priority Review granted May 2026; Breakthrough Therapy status in US and China).

Modality bets sit in platform subsidiaries: AskBio's AB-1002 gene therapy for congestive heart failure (Phase II) and REGENERATE-PD in Parkinson's; BlueRock's bemdaneprocel, a stem-cell-derived Parkinson's therapy that advanced directly to Phase III in 2025; Vividion's chemoproteomics feeding precision oncology/immunology candidates. Radiology is being refreshed with gadoquatrane (Phase III QUANTI program met endpoints).

Business development leans increasingly on China: a March 2025 global license from Puhe BioPharma for an oral MTA-cooperative PRMT5 inhibitor (MTAP-deleted tumors), an oncology deal with Kumquat Biosciences, and the Bayer Co.Lab Shanghai incubator (opened 2024). In ophthalmology, Bayer acquired Perfuse Therapeutics (April 2026 (est.), $300M upfront + up to $2.15B milestones) for a mid-phase eye-disease implant. Selected pipeline:

## Key risks

- **Twin patent cliffs.** Xarelto (€2.34B in 2025, was €4.1B in 2023) guided down another 35-40% in 2026; Eylea (€3.1B) guided down 20-25% on biosimilar price erosion. Roughly €5.4B of 2025 revenue is in structural decline.
- **Glyphosate/litigation overhang (group level).** Multi-billion-euro litigation provisions (≈€11-12B, est.) and heavy near-term cash payouts weigh on group free cash flow. The June 25, 2026 Supreme Court win limiting state failure-to-warn Roundup suits and the proposed $7.25B class settlement (Feb 2026) materially de-risk, but settlement finalization, opt-outs and residual claims persist.
- **Concentration of growth.** The equity case rests heavily on Nubeqa + Kerendia (€3.2B combined, 2025) and on asundexian succeeding where OCEANIC-AF failed; Nubeqa economics are shared with Orion, Beyonttra royalties flow to BridgeBio.
- **IRA / pricing.** Rivaroxaban was in the first Medicare negotiation cohort (negotiated prices effective 2026) — though US Xarelto economics accrue to Bayer as J&J license revenue; finerenone/darolutamide are plausible future selection candidates as US sales grow (est.).
- **Balance sheet.** Net financial debt ~€32-33B (est.) plus litigation cash-out constrains BD firepower; dividend at legal minimum through FY2026 (est.).
- **Franchise erosion elsewhere.** Hemophilia (Kovaltry/Jivi) losing share to novel agents; Adempas LOE late-decade; Consumer Health and Crop Science provide scale but little growth.
