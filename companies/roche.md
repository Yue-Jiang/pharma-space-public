Roche is the world's largest biotech by revenue and the only top-tier pharma that also leads global in vitro diagnostics. Genentech (South San Francisco) is its wholly owned innovation engine; it also holds a majority of Chugai (Japan). FY2025 IFRS net income was CHF 13.8B; R&D spend CHF 12.2B (~20% of sales).

## History: 1995–2025

### 1995–2008: From chemistry house to biotech empire
Roche entered the mid-1990s already transformed by two decisions: buying 60% of Genentech for $2.1 billion in 1990, and acquiring Syntex for $5.3 billion in 1994, which became its Palo Alto research base. In 1997 it paid $11 billion for Boehringer Mannheim, instantly becoming the world leader in in vitro diagnostics — the foundation of its distinctive "pharma plus diagnostics" model. The era carried a stain: in 1999 Roche pleaded guilty to leading a global vitamins price-fixing cartel and paid a then-record $500 million US criminal fine (plus a €462 million EU fine in 2001); it exited the business entirely in 2003, selling vitamins to DSM. In 2002 Roche took majority control of Japan's Chugai, an unusually hands-off arrangement that still endures. Meanwhile Genentech delivered the biologics that would define modern oncology: Rituxan (rituximab, 1997), Herceptin (trastuzumab, 1998), Avastin (bevacizumab, 2004) and Lucentis (ranibizumab, 2006). Tamiflu (oseltamivir) made Roche a household name during avian-flu and H1N1 stockpiling, later shadowed by the Cochrane data-transparency controversy. The 2008 purchase of Ventana ($3.4 billion) added tissue diagnostics.

### 2009–2016: The Genentech buyout and personalised healthcare
In March 2009 Roche paid $46.8 billion for the 44% of Genentech it didn't own — still its largest deal — while deliberately preserving Genentech's independent research culture. The strategy crystallised as "personalised healthcare": pair a targeted drug with a companion diagnostic, as with Zelboraf (vemurafenib) in BRAF-mutant melanoma. The HER2 franchise deepened with Perjeta (pertuzumab, 2012) and Kadcyla (trastuzumab emtansine, 2013). Setbacks were real: the FDA revoked Avastin's metastatic breast cancer indication in 2011, and Accutane (isotretinoin) had been pulled from the US market in 2009 amid generics and litigation. The $8.3 billion InterMune acquisition (2014) brought Esbriet (pirfenidone) for pulmonary fibrosis, and Tecentriq (atezolizumab, 2016) put Roche into checkpoint immunotherapy — though it never caught Keytruda.

### 2017–2022: Surviving a ~CHF 21 billion patent cliff
Few companies have faced a cliff like Roche's: Rituxan, Herceptin and Avastin — roughly CHF 21 billion of combined peak sales in 2018 — all lost exclusivity as biosimilars arrived in Europe from 2017 and the US from 2019. The response was a new generation launched in rapid succession: Ocrevus (ocrelizumab, 2017) became the fastest-launching multiple sclerosis drug ever; Hemlibra (emicizumab, 2017–18) redefined hemophilia A; Polivy (2019), Evrysdi (2020) and Vabysmo (faricimab, 2022) followed. Roche also spent ~$4.3 billion on data assets in 2018 (Flatiron Health, $1.9B; the rest of Foundation Medicine, $2.4B) and $4.8 billion on gene-therapy pioneer Spark Therapeutics (2019), plus an ex-US deal with Sarepta for the Duchenne gene therapy Elevidys (Dec 2019, $1.15B upfront). COVID brought a diagnostics windfall and Actemra's repurposing, then a sharp post-pandemic hangover. Late-stage neuroscience stumbled: tominersen (Huntington's) was halted in 2021, gantenerumab failed in Alzheimer's in November 2022, and the TIGIT antibody tiragolumab missed repeatedly — a costly string of misses that set up the next era's rebuild.

### 2023–2025: The Schinecker reset
Thomas Schinecker, previously diagnostics chief, became CEO in March 2023 and pruned the pipeline hard while buying external innovation. Roche paid $7.1 billion for Telavant (October 2023) to get the anti-TL1A antibody now called afimkibart for inflammatory bowel disease, then $2.7 billion upfront for Carmot Therapeutics (December 2023), acquiring the incretins that anchor its late-arriving but aggressive obesity push. In 2025 the pace accelerated: the Poseida CAR-T acquisition closed in January (~$1.1 billion plus contingent payments); a landmark March collaboration with Zealand Pharma on the amylin analog petrelintide carried a $1.65 billion upfront ($5.3 billion potential); and in September Roche agreed to buy 89bio for up to $3.5 billion for pegozafermin in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH). Itovebi (inavolisib) was approved for PIK3CA-mutant breast cancer in October 2024. In April 2025, facing US tariff threats and pricing pressure, Roche pledged $50 billion of US investment over five years. FY2025 closed strong: sales CHF 61.5 billion (+7% CER), net income CHF 13.8 billion, and a 39th consecutive dividend increase — with the top five growth drivers (Phesgo, Xolair, Ocrevus, Hemlibra, Vabysmo) generating CHF 21.4 billion.

## Current strategic bets

Roche's 2025–2026 story is a pipeline broadening beyond oncology into CVRM/obesity, neurology and immunology. In multiple sclerosis, fenebrutinib — an oral, brain-penetrant Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor — delivered three positive Phase III trials (FENhance 1 & 2 in relapsing MS, cutting relapse rates 51–59% vs teriflunomide; FENtrepid in primary progressive MS vs Ocrevus), with US filing planned mid-2026 and management guiding to >CHF 3 billion peak sales; a fatal-adverse-event imbalance across the trials (assessed as unrelated by investigators) is the key approvability question. In Alzheimer's, trontinemab uses Roche's Brain Shuttle transferrin-receptor technology to clear amyloid rapidly at low ARIA rates (91% amyloid-PET negativity in Phase Ib/IIa); Phase III TRONTIER trials began in 2025 with readouts expected around 2027–2028.

The obesity/CVRM franchise is the boldest bet: enicepatide (CT-388, GLP-1/GIP dual agonist from Carmot) entered Phase III in Q1 2026; petrelintide (amylin analog, Zealand partnership) is in Phase III after ~10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks in Phase IIb with placebo-like GI tolerability; CT-996 (oral GLP-1) reads out in 2026, and a petrelintide+enicepatide fixed-dose combination study starts mid-2026. Pegozafermin (FGF21 analog, from 89bio) adds MASH. In oncology, giredestrant (oral SERD) failed first-line metastatic breast cancer but won US Priority Review in the adjuvant setting (2026); divarasib posted positive Phase III data in KRAS G12C lung cancer; and CD20xCD3 bispecifics Columvi/Lunsumio expand in lymphoma. Immunology bets include Gazyva's new lupus nephritis indication and afimkibart (anti-TL1A) in IBD. China-originated BD is now core: two MediLink ADC licenses (YL211 c-MET, Jan 2024, ~$1B biobucks; YL201 B7H3, Jan 2026, $570M upfront/near-term). Diagnostics launched Axelios, a next-generation sequencing platform on its SBX technology, in 2026.

## Key risks

- **Ocrevus cliff**: EU LOE ~2028, US ~2029 — CHF 7.0B FY2025 revenue at risk; high-dose lifecycle study failed (2025), leaving the SC formulation and fenebrutinib as the MS franchise defense.
- **Near-term LOEs**: Perjeta EU expiry 2026 (~CHF 3.0B globally at risk, partly defended by Phesgo conversion); Xolair US biosimilars expected H2 2026 (CHF 3.1B US); legacy trio (Avastin/Herceptin/MabThera) still eroding (−CHF 0.7B in 2025).
- **Obesity execution risk**: Roche is years behind Lilly and Novo Nordisk; enicepatide/petrelintide must differentiate on tolerability in crowded Phase III landscapes; heavy capital committed (~$5B+ across Carmot, Zealand, 89bio).
- **Pipeline safety overhangs**: fenebrutinib fatal-AE imbalance (including suicidality questions) could complicate FDA review; Elevidys (ex-US partner Sarepta) saw patient deaths and shipment pauses in 2025.
- **Pricing/IRA/tariffs**: Part B-heavy infusion portfolio (Ocrevus, Tecentriq, Vabysmo, Polivy) is exposed as US Medicare negotiation extends to Part B drugs from 2028; US tariff and most-favored-nation pricing pressure motivated the $50B US investment pledge; strong Swiss franc is a persistent reported-results headwind (2025: +7% CER but only +2% in CHF).
- **Litigation/other**: history of major settlements (vitamins cartel, Accutane); ongoing biosimilar IP disputes; Diagnostics China exposure (volume-based procurement cut China diagnostics sales ~24% in 2025).
