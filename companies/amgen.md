## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

Amgen's growth after 2030 rests on three bets that are all still in trials. The biggest by far is MariTide, an obesity drug designed to be injected monthly or less often rather than weekly — Amgen is not claiming more weight loss than Lilly or Novo, it is claiming a more convenient schedule, and it has put six Phase 3 studies behind that claim. Behind MariTide sit olpasiran, which lowers a genetically inherited cholesterol-like particle that no approved drug currently treats, and a set of cancer and immunology assets; a fourth, quieter bet is the biosimilar unit, which is building copies of the biggest-selling cancer drugs in the world ahead of their patent expiries.

## History: 1995-2025

### 1995–2001: The EPO franchise era
Amgen entered the late 1990s as biotech's biggest success story, built on two recombinant proteins: Epogen (epoetin alfa, anemia in dialysis, 1989) and Neupogen (filgrastim, neutropenia, 1991). Long-running arbitration with Johnson & Johnson over the 1985 EPO license (J&J sold Procrit outside dialysis) and patent litigation against Transkaryotic Therapies defended the franchise. In 2001 Amgen won approval for two second-generation products, Aranesp (darbepoetin alfa) and Neulasta (pegfilgrastim), extending the franchise ahead of patent expiry. Kevin Sharer became CEO in 2000.

### 2002–2006: Scale through M&A
In 2002 Amgen acquired Immunex for ~$16B — then the largest biotech deal ever — gaining Enbrel, which became a multi-billion-dollar anti-TNF blockbuster. Tularik followed in 2004 (~$1.3B, oncology/small molecules) and Abgenix in 2006 (~$2.2B, fully human antibody platform, full rights to panitumumab/Vectibix). Revenue roughly tripled from ~$4B (2001) to ~$14.3B (2006), with the ESA franchise (Epogen + Aranesp) peaking near $7B.

### 2007–2011: The ESA safety crisis
2007 was Amgen's first existential shock: studies linked erythropoiesis-stimulating agents at high doses to cardiovascular events and tumor progression. The FDA imposed black-box warnings and REMS restrictions, and Medicare slashed reimbursement. Aranesp sales collapsed, and Amgen announced its first major restructuring (~14% of staff in 2007). In 2012 the company pleaded guilty to misbranding Aranesp and paid $762M — then the largest criminal/civil settlement by a biotech. The company rebuilt around denosumab: Prolia (osteoporosis) and Xgeva (bone metastases) were approved in 2010, and Amgen initiated its first dividend in 2011.

### 2012–2018: Bradway, oncology, and pipeline replenishment
Robert Bradway became CEO in May 2012 and pursued disciplined BD: Micromet ($1.16B, 2012 — BiTE platform, later Blincyto), deCODE Genetics ($415M, 2012 — human genetics for target discovery), and Onyx Pharmaceuticals ($10.4B, 2013 — Kyprolis). Repatha (PCSK9) was approved in 2015 but initially struggled against payer resistance; the 2017 FOURIER outcomes trial and later aggressive list-price cuts positioned it for the strong growth seen today. Amgen also launched a biosimilars unit (Amjevita approved 2016) while absorbing biosimilar erosion of its own legacy brands (Neupogen, then Neulasta and Epogen from 2018-2019).

### 2019–2023: Portfolio reshaping and the Horizon bet
In 2019 Amgen bought Otezla from Celgene for $13.4B (divested for the BMS-Celgene merger) and took a ~20.5% stake in China's BeiGene (now BeOne Medicines) for ~$2.7B to build Asia oncology presence. Lumakras (sotorasib), the first-ever approved KRAS G12C inhibitor, received accelerated approval in 2021 — a landmark scientific success, though the FDA later declined to convert to full approval at the original dose after mixed confirmatory data. Bolt-ons continued: Five Prime ($1.9B, 2021, bemarituzumab), Teneobio ($900M, 2021, bispecifics), ChemoCentryx ($3.7B, 2022, Tavneos). The defining move was Horizon Therapeutics for $27.8B (closed Oct 2023 after an FTC challenge settled) — Amgen's largest acquisition — adding rare-disease drugs Tepezza, Krystexxa and Uplizna. The IRS is separately pursuing Amgen for ~$10.7B (including penalties) over 2010-2015 transfer pricing tied to Puerto Rico manufacturing; the dispute remains unresolved.

### 2024–2025: Broad-based growth and the obesity pivot
Horizon integration drove a rare-disease business of ~$4.5B+/yr. MariTide Phase 2 obesity data (Nov 2024, full data June 2025) showed ~12-16% average weight loss at 52 weeks without plateau (~17% with T2D), triggering the large Phase 3 MARITIME program. Prolia's US patent expired Feb 2025, opening Amgen's biggest cliff since Epogen. FY2025 closed strong: revenues $36.8B (+10%), 14 blockbusters, record non-GAAP R&D spend of $7.2B, and the VESALIUS-CV trial (Nov 2025) showing Repatha cuts MACE in primary-prevention-type patients — supporting a major label expansion. Portfolio pruning continued: the rocatinlimab (atopic dermatitis) collaboration with Kyowa Kirin was terminated in early 2026.

## Current strategic bets

- **Obesity / cardiometabolic — MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide)**: monthly (or less frequent) antibody-peptide conjugate; GLP-1 agonist + GIPR antagonist. Phase 3 MARITIME chronic weight management studies (obesity ± T2D, 72 weeks) enrolling; Phase 3 outcomes studies in ASCVD, heart failure, and obstructive sleep apnea initiated in 2025. Backed by a broader obesity pipeline of oral and injectable, incretin and non-incretin candidates. This is Amgen's single biggest pipeline bet — a differentiated (dosing-interval) challenger to Lilly/Novo.
- **Cardiovascular expansion**: Repatha label broadened (Aug 2025) to high-risk primary prevention; VESALIUS-CV (25% 3P-MACE reduction) supports further growth toward a multi-billion franchise. Olpasiran, a siRNA against Lp(a), is in Phase 3 outcomes development (OCEAN(a)).
- **Oncology — bispecifics and protein degradation**: Imdelltra (DLL3xCD3 BiTE) received full approval in ES-SCLC; Blincyto growing 28%. Early bets: Dark Blue Therapeutics acquisition (Jan 2026, up to $840M — MLLT1/3 protein degrader for AML, first M&A since Horizon) and an exclusive license with DISCO Pharmaceuticals (announced Jan 7, 2026, up to $618M plus royalties — global rights to programs against an undisclosed cancer cell-surface target from DISCO's surfaceome-mapping platform). Xaluritamig (STEAP1 CD3-engager, prostate) in late-stage development.
- **Rare disease**: Uplizna new indications (IgG4-related disease — first-ever approved therapy, 2025; generalized myasthenia gravis), Tepezza ex-US expansion (EU approval 2025), positioning as a top rare-disease player post-Horizon.
- **Inflammation**: Tezspire (+52%) expanding into new indications (with AstraZeneca); rocatinlimab terminated — portfolio prioritization in favor of higher-conviction assets.
- **Biosimilars**: Pavblu (Eylea), Wezlana (Stelara), Bekemv (Soliris) launches offsetting innovator-side erosion.
- **China/Asia**: ~20.5% BeOne Medicines (ex-BeiGene) equity stake (large mark-to-market gains in 2025); no major China in-licensing deals to date — Amgen has been notably absent from the China licensing wave.
- **Stated strategy**: management guides FY2026 revenue of $37.0-38.4B, expecting six growth drivers (Repatha, Evenity, Tezspire, Blincyto, Imdelltra, Uplizna, plus rare disease) to outpace denosumab biosimilar erosion; capex ~$2.6B incl. US manufacturing expansion; buybacks capped at $3B.

## Key risks

- **Denosumab cliff (largest near-term risk)**: Prolia ($4.4B) + Xgeva ($2.1B) = ~$6.5B FY2025 sales; US RANKL patents expired Feb 2025 (EU Nov 2025); multiple biosimilars launched; management guides accelerated erosion in 2026.
- **Enbrel decline**: sales fell 33% to $2.2B in 2025; IRA-negotiated Medicare price (~67% below list) effective Jan 2026 accelerates the decline of a franchise that peaked above $5B.
- **Otezla LOE**: ~$2.3B at risk to US generic entry expected around 2028.
- **Pipeline concentration**: growth narrative leans heavily on MariTide succeeding in Phase 3 against entrenched, faster-moving Lilly/Novo obesity franchises; Phase 2 GI tolerability required dose-escalation redesign.
- **Pricing/policy exposure**: Enbrel in IRA round 1 (2026 prices); further Amgen products eligible in later rounds (15 more drugs selected for 2027, 15 for 2028); ~73-75% of product sales are US-based, maximizing exposure to US pricing reform (MFN proposals, tariffs).
- **Litigation/tax**: unresolved IRS transfer-pricing dispute of ~$10.7B including penalties (2010-2015 tax years); ongoing patent litigation with denosumab biosimilar makers.
- **Balance sheet**: still elevated debt from the $27.8B Horizon acquisition constrains large-scale M&A.
