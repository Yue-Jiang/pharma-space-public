## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

Lilly's pipeline is mostly an argument about how long the obesity franchise can last. Tirzepatide is a weekly injection whose US patent runs to about 2036, so the next generation is about convenience and depth of effect: a pill (orforglipron, approved as Foundayo in April 2026), a stronger triple-hormone injection (retatrutide), and combinations meant to preserve muscle rather than just cut weight. Around that core sit two smaller but strategically important bets — lowering Lp(a), a genetically inherited cholesterol-like particle no approved drug currently treats, and building an oncology and neuroscience portfolio that does not depend on metabolism at all.

## History: 1995-2025

### 1995–2001: Prozac era and the first cliff
Through the late 1990s Lilly was defined by Prozac (fluoxetine), which peaked around $2.6B/year — roughly a quarter of revenue. Zyprexa (olanzapine, 1996), Humalog (1996) and Evista (1998) launched under CEOs Randall Tobias and then Sidney Taurel (1998). In August 2000 a federal appeals court invalidated Lilly's Prozac patent extension; generics arrived in August 2001 and Prozac sales collapsed >70% within a year — a formative cliff that pushed Lilly toward pipeline diversification.

### 2002–2008: Diversification, Zyprexa's peak and legal reckoning
Lilly launched Strattera and Forteo (2002), Cialis (2003), and Cymbalta and Alimta (2004), plus Byetta via Amylin (2005). Zyprexa became the top seller (peak ~$4.8B) but generated massive litigation: ~$1.2B in product-liability settlements (2005–07) over diabetes risk, and in January 2009 a $1.415B federal settlement for off-label marketing — including a $515M criminal fine, then the largest in US history. In 2008 Lilly made its biggest acquisition to date: ImClone Systems for ~$6.5B (Erbitux, oncology pipeline).

### 2009–2014: The "Years YZ" patent cliff
Lilly faced sequential LOEs — Gemzar (2010), Zyprexa (Oct 2011, ~$5B/yr), Cymbalta (Dec 2013, ~$5B/yr), Evista (2014) — and revenue fell from $24.3B (2011) to ~$19.6B (2014). CEO John Lechleiter (2008–2016) famously refused defensive megamergers, protected the dividend, and kept R&D spending above 20% of sales while betting on the internal pipeline; the largest deal of the period was Novartis Animal Health ($5.4B, 2014) to bolster Elanco.

### 2015–2021: Rebuild, Alzheimer's failures, portfolio reshaping
The bet paid off in a launch wave: Trulicity (2014), Jardiance (2014, Boehringer alliance), Taltz (2016), Verzenio (2017), Emgality (2018). Failures were equally famous: solanezumab missed in the Phase 3 EXPEDITION3 Alzheimer's trial (Nov 2016), sepsis drug Xigris had been withdrawn (2011) after a failed confirmatory trial, and Lartruvo was withdrawn in 2019 after the ANNOUNCE trial failed. David Ricks became CEO in January 2017. Lilly spun off Elanco (IPO 2018, full separation 2019), refocusing as pure-play human pharma. Loxo Oncology was acquired for $8B (2019; delivered Retevmo and Jaypirca), Prevail Therapeutics for ~$0.9B (2021, gene therapy). Lilly's COVID antibodies (bamlanivimab, bebtelovimab) generated multibillion-dollar but transient revenue before variant escape.

### 2022–2025: The tirzepatide transformation
Mounjaro (May 2022) and Zepbound (Nov 2023; OSA indication Dec 2024) drove the steepest growth in big-pharma history: revenue rose $28.5B (2022) → $34.1B (2023) → $45.0B (2024) → $65.2B (2025). Lilly cut insulin list prices ~70% and capped out-of-pocket costs at $35/month (March 2023) amid pricing scrutiny. Kisunla (donanemab) was approved for Alzheimer's (July 2024). BD stayed bolt-on: Dice Therapeutics ($2.4B), Versanis ($1.9B, bimagrumab), POINT Biopharma ($1.4B, radioligands) in 2023; Morphic ($3.2B, oral integrins) in 2024; Scorpion Therapeutics' PI3Kα program (up to $2.5B), SiteOne (up to $1B, non-opioid pain) and Verve Therapeutics (~$1.3B, in vivo gene editing) in 2025. Lilly committed >$50B to US manufacturing expansion since 2020. In November 2025 it struck a landmark pricing/access agreement with the Trump administration (Medicare/Medicaid GLP-1 coverage, TrumpRx, tariff relief), and in 2025 became the first $1T-market-cap pharma.

## Current strategic bets

**Obesity/cardiometabolic dominance (core bet).** Next-generation incretins to defend and expand the franchise: **orforglipron** (oral small-molecule GLP-1, licensed from Chugai 2018) — approved as **Foundayo** April 1, 2026 (obesity), the fastest NME approval since 2002 via FDA's national priority voucher; T2D filing to follow; $98M sales in its first partial quarter. **Retatrutide** (GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple agonist) — positive Phase 3 TRIUMPH readouts in obesity and knee-OA (2025). **Bimagrumab** (muscle-preserving anti-ActRII, Ph2b) and **eloralintide** (amylin agonist, Ph2) for weight-loss quality. Mounjaro entered China's NRDL (2026), trading price for volume.

**Cardiovascular.** **Lepodisiran** (siRNA against Lp(a), Phase 3), **muvalaplin** (oral Lp(a) disruptor, Ph2 positive), **VERVE-102** (one-time in vivo base editing of PCSK9, Ph1b — via Verve acquisition).

**Neuroscience.** Kisunla global rollout (approved China, Japan, EU); **remternetug** (subcutaneous anti-amyloid, Ph3); non-opioid pain via SiteOne's Nav1.8 inhibitor STC-004 (Ph2-ready).

**Oncology.** Verzenio OS data (monarchE); **Jaypirca** head-to-head win vs. Imbruvica; **Inluriyo** (oral SERD) launched 2025; **olomorasib** (KRAS G12C, Ph3); STX-478 mutant-selective PI3Kα (from Scorpion); radioligand pipeline from POINT Biopharma; sofetabart mipitecan received FDA Breakthrough designation (2026).

**Modality bets.** Oral small molecules for mass-market cardiometabolic disease; siRNA; in vivo gene editing (Verve); gene therapy (Prevail); radioligands (POINT); ADC exposure remains modest relative to peers. Unlike many large-cap peers, Lilly's recent BD has been US-centric — no headline China-originated in-licensing; its main China play is out-licensing (mazdutide to Innovent, approved in China 2025) and NRDL access.

**Stated strategy (management).** Ricks: 2026 revenue guidance $80–83B (raised to $85–87B by Aug 2026), pipeline with >25 positive Phase 3 readouts in 2025, >$50B US manufacturing buildout, direct-to-consumer via LillyDirect (>1M patients added in 2025; self-pay Zepbound vials = ~1/3 of new obesity-brand starts).

## Key risks

- **Concentration risk:** tirzepatide = ~56% of 2025 revenue; any safety signal, supply disruption, or share loss (Novo's oral Wegovy launched Jan 2026; Viking, Structure and others in Ph3) hits the whole P&L.
- **Pricing erosion on GLP-1s:** realized prices already falling (-5% companywide in Q4 2025; steep international declines after China NRDL entry); Nov 2025 US government deal caps Medicare price at $245/month and TrumpRx cash price ~$346, pressuring net price per script even as volumes expand.
- **Patent cliffs:** Trulicity 2027 (~$4B), Jardiance 2028 (plus IRA-negotiated Medicare price from Jan 2026), Verzenio ~2029 (~$5.7B), Taltz ~2030 (~$3.4B); tirzepatide ~2036 (US) but earlier in some ex-US markets; compounded tirzepatide remains a leakage channel.
- **Policy/regulatory:** IRA Medicare negotiation rounds will reach more Lilly products; MFN pricing push; pharma-sector tariff threats (Nov 2025 deal provides only up to 3 years' relief).
- **Litigation:** GLP-1 gastroparesis/intestinal-injury MDL; insulin pricing lawsuits and state AG actions; ongoing IP disputes over tirzepatide compounding.
- **Execution risks:** Kisunla ramp is slow (~$70M/quarter in late 2025) amid amyloid-therapy safety monitoring burdens; massive manufacturing capex ($50B+) assumes sustained obesity-market growth.
