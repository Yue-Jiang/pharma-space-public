## Pipeline (clinical-stage)

Novartis has told investors it will grow straight through the biggest patent expiry in its history — Entresto, Promacta and Tasigna all going generic at once — which means the pipeline has to produce replacements on a schedule. The company is not chasing obesity; it is betting instead on cardiovascular risk factors that no approved drug addresses (Lp(a), factor XI clotting), on autoimmune kidney disease, and on RNA-based medicines that can be aimed at muscle tissue, the platform it paid roughly USD 12 billion for Avidity to acquire. Two 2026 readouts — pelacarsen in Lp(a) and remibrutinib in multiple sclerosis — are the ones management itself flags as making or breaking the growth story.

## History: 1995-2025

### 1996-2000: The merger that created Novartis
Novartis was formed in December 1996 by the roughly USD 30 billion merger of Basel neighbors Ciba-Geigy and Sandoz — at the time the largest corporate merger in history. The name derives from "novae artes," new skills. The combined company initially spanned pharmaceuticals, generics, agrochemicals, nutrition and consumer health. In 2000 it spun off its agribusiness, which merged with AstraZeneca's agrochemicals arm to form Syngenta — the first of many portfolio prunings.

### 2001-2012: Gleevec, Diovan, and empire building
The 2001 FDA approval of Gleevec (imatinib) transformed chronic myeloid leukemia from a fatal disease into a manageable condition and became the archetype of targeted cancer therapy — and a multi-billion-dollar franchise. Hypertension blockbuster Diovan (valsartan) peaked around USD 6 billion. Novartis built a generics powerhouse under the revived Sandoz name (Hexal and Eon Labs, ~USD 8.3B, 2005), bought Chiron for vaccines (USD 5.4B, 2006), and executed a staged ~USD 52 billion takeover of eye-care company Alcon from Nestlé (2008-2011) — a conglomerate-style bet later unwound. Gilenya (fingolimod), the first oral multiple sclerosis drug, launched in 2010. Diovan's 2012 US patent expiry opened a long cliff decade.

### 2013-2017: Portfolio surgery under Jörg Reinhardt
With Jörg Reinhardt as chair from 2013, Novartis executed a three-way asset swap in 2014-2015: it bought GSK's oncology portfolio (~USD 16B), sold its vaccines business to GSK, folded consumer health into a GSK joint venture, and sold animal health to Eli Lilly (USD 5.4B). Entresto (sacubitril/valsartan) and Cosentyx (secukinumab) both launched in 2015, seeding the next growth cycle just as Gleevec lost US exclusivity in 2016. India's Supreme Court had rejected the Glivec patent in 2013, a landmark in global pricing debates.

On 30 August 2017 the FDA approved **Kymriah (tisagenlecleucel)** for relapsed/refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia in patients up to age 25 — the first CAR-T therapy, and the first gene-modified cell therapy of any kind, ever approved. Developed as CTL019 and licensed from the University of Pennsylvania (Carl June's lab), it opened the cell-therapy era for the whole industry; a second approval followed in May 2018 for adult third-line large B-cell lymphoma (JULIET). Commercially, though, Novartis never converted first-mover status into leadership: manufacturing reliability problems, a 2021 miss in second-line large B-cell lymphoma where Gilead's Yescarta (ZUMA-7) and BMS's Breyanzi succeeded on the same CD19 target, and steady competitive erosion left Kymriah in decline (Q4 2025 net sales $85M, -21% year on year) and out of the company's top-brand disclosures. Novartis's cell-therapy energy shifted to radioligand therapy instead.

### 2018-2023: Narasimhan's "focused medicines company"
Vas Narasimhan, a physician and former chief medical officer, became CEO in February 2018 and systematically dismantled the conglomerate: the GSK consumer JV stake was sold for USD 13 billion (2018), Alcon was spun off (April 2019), the ~USD 20.7 billion Roche voting stake was sold back (2021), and Sandoz was spun off as an independent generics company (October 2023). In parallel he bought platforms: AveXis (USD 8.7B, 2018) brought gene therapy Zolgensma — approved 2019 at USD 2.1 million per dose, then the world's most expensive drug, and quickly embroiled in a data-manipulation scandal involving pre-submission animal data. Endocyte (USD 2.1B, 2018) brought radioligand therapy, yielding Pluvicto (approved 2022). The Medicines Company (USD 9.7B, 2019) brought inclisiran/Leqvio, licensed from Alnylam. Legal overhangs were cleared expensively: a USD 678 million US settlement over sham speaker programs and a USD 347 million FCPA settlement (both 2020). Chinook Therapeutics (up to USD 3.5B, 2023) added kidney-disease assets atrasentan (now Vanrafia) and zigakibart.

### 2024-2025: Growth through the cliff
Novartis entered its Entresto loss-of-exclusivity period from a position of strength. MorphoSys (~USD 2.9B, 2024) added pelabresib in myelofibrosis; Kate Therapeutics (~USD 1.1B) and Mariana Oncology (~USD 1.75B) added gene-delivery and radiopharma capabilities. In 2025 the company completed 17 BD&L/M&A deals: Anthos Therapeutics (USD 925M upfront, up to USD 3.1B with milestones; abelacimab, a factor XI antibody that originated in Novartis labs), Regulus Therapeutics (USD 800M cash plus up to USD 900M in contingent value rights; farabursen for polycystic kidney disease, closed June 2025), Tourmaline Bio (USD 1.4B; anti-IL-6 pacibekitug, completed October 2025), and — the capstone — a roughly USD 12 billion agreement for Avidity Biosciences and its antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates for neuromuscular disease (announced October 2025, expected to close H1 2026). FY2025 delivered USD 54.5 billion in sales (+8% cc) despite US generics hitting Entresto, Promacta and Tasigna; the year also brought 18 approvals and 13 regulatory filings. Kisqali (+57% cc) became the new growth engine after positive NATALEE early-breast-cancer data. Novartis also pledged USD 23 billion over five years (announced April 10, 2025) for US manufacturing and R&D expansion — ten new or expanded facilities including a USD 1.1 billion San Diego research hub — amid tariff pressure.

## Current strategic bets

Novartis guides to 5-6% constant-currency sales CAGR for 2025-2030 (raised in November 2025) and says it expects to "grow through the largest patent expiry in Novartis history" in 2026 (Entresto, Promacta, Tasigna generics). Management's growth case rests on in-market priority brands with raised peak-sales expectations — Kisqali (analyst coverage cites a ~USD 10B peak ambition, est.), Cosentyx, Kesimpta, Pluvicto, Scemblix, Leqvio (blockbuster status reached in 2025), Fabhalta and Rhapsido (remibrutinib), the oral BTK inhibitor that won FDA approval in 2025 for chronic spontaneous urticaria.

The modality thesis is distinctive: radioligand therapy (Pluvicto expanding into earlier prostate-cancer lines, PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive filing submitted; Lutathera; Mariana), siRNA and RNA-targeting (Leqvio, pelacarsen, Regulus, and Avidity's muscle-targeted AOC platform with del-desiran in myotonic dystrophy, del-zota in Duchenne, del-brax in FSHD — launches planned before 2030), and gene therapy (Zolgensma; Itvisma, the intrathecal formulation approved in the US in Q4 2025, extending SMA treatment to older patients). Pluvicto added a pre-taxane mCRPC approval in Q1 2025 and a positive Phase III readout (PSMAddition) in hormone-sensitive disease in June 2025.

## Key risks

- **2026 patent cliff:** Entresto (USD 7.7B in 2025), Promacta (USD 1.6B) and Tasigna (USD 1.1B) all face generic erosion simultaneously — company-described as the largest patent expiry in its history. Roughly USD 10B of 2025 revenue is exposed through 2027 (est.).
- **Kisqali concentration:** the growth story increasingly leans on Kisqali (USD 4.8B, +57% cc) continuing its trajectory before its ~2031 LOE (est.); a post-2030 cliff follows if pipeline launches slip.
- **IRA / US pricing:** Entresto was in the first Medicare negotiation cohort (negotiated prices effective 2026); further selections and Part D redesign pressure US net pricing. US policy risk includes pharma tariff threats — an explicit backdrop to the USD 23B US investment pledge (April 2025).
- **Pipeline binary events:** pelacarsen's Lp(a) outcomes readout and remibrutinib's two Phase III multiple sclerosis trials are high-value, high-uncertainty near-term catalysts (2026).
- **Integration/BD execution:** the 2025 deal spree (17 transactions; Avidity at ~USD 12B the largest) must convert to launches before 2030 to sustain the growth algorithm.
- **Litigation/compliance history:** the UK Patents Court rejected Accord Healthcare's challenge to the Entresto patent and supplementary protection certificate in August 2026, found the certificate valid through January 15, 2028, and held that Accord's intended product would infringe. This is a UK ruling only; separate US Entresto litigation and generic erosion continue. Lutathera US patent litigation, the 2020 kickback and FCPA settlements, and the 2019 Zolgensma data-integrity episode remain additional legal and reputational risks.
- **Radioligand supply chain:** Pluvicto depends on complex isotope manufacturing and delivery logistics; Novartis is adding US radioligand capacity (new Florida and Texas sites), but the modality remains operationally demanding.
