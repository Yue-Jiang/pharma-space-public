# Johnson & Johnson — audio deep-dive

Johnson and Johnson is the largest healthcare company in the world, and since spinning off its consumer business it is really two companies under one ticker: a medical-device segment and a pharmaceutical segment called Innovative Medicine, which is our focus here. Founded in eighteen eighty-six and headquartered in New Brunswick, New Jersey, it trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker J N J. In fiscal year twenty twenty-five the whole company booked about ninety-four point two billion dollars in revenue, up six percent, with guidance of roughly one hundred point five billion for twenty twenty-six; the pharmaceutical segment alone was sixty point four billion. Headcount is about one hundred thirty-eight thousand. Joaquin Duato, a three-decade company veteran from Spain, has been chairman and chief executive since January twenty twenty-two. The pharmaceutical focus areas are cancer, immunology, neuroscience, pulmonary hypertension, cardiovascular and metabolic disease, and infectious disease.

Start with the products. The colossus is Darzalex, generic name daratumumab, an antibody licensed from Denmark's Genmab that targets a protein called C D thirty-eight on multiple myeloma cells — a cancer of the bone marrow's plasma cells. Sold mostly as an under-the-skin injection called Darzalex Faspro, it passed fourteen billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, growing twenty-two percent, and is used across every stage of myeloma — the biggest drug in company history. Second, and heading the other direction, is Stelara, generic name ustekinumab, the immunology antibody for psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease: once an eleven-billion-dollar product, it lost United States exclusivity in January twenty twenty-five and fell forty-one percent to about six point one billion as biosimilar copies flooded in. Third is its designated successor Tremfya, generic name guselkumab, a next-generation antibody for psoriasis and now inflammatory bowel disease, past five billion dollars and growing seventy-five percent in the fourth quarter — management sees a ten-billion-dollar-plus peak. Fourth is Erleada, generic name apalutamide, an oral prostate cancer drug at roughly three point six billion and growing twenty percent. Fifth, the pulmonary arterial hypertension pair from the Actelion acquisition — Uptravi and Opsumit — about three point nine billion combined. And sixth, the multiple myeloma cell-therapy-and-bispecifics wave: Carvykti, a cell therapy made from a patient's own engineered immune cells, developed with China's Legend Biotech, crossed a billion dollars in its own right, and together with the bispecific antibodies Tecvayli and Talvey the trio reached three billion dollars. Also notable: the depression nasal spray Spravato, up fifty percent to about one and a half billion; the lung cancer combination Rybrevant plus Lazcluze; and Caplyta, the antipsychotic acquired with Intra-Cellular Therapies in twenty twenty-five.

Now the history. Through the late nineteen nineties the pharmaceutical arm rode the antipsychotic Risperdal and the anemia drug Procrit. The pivotal move was the nineteen ninety-nine acquisition of Centocor, which brought Remicade, generic name infliximab — the anti-inflammatory antibody that anchored the company's immunology franchise for two decades and taught it the biologics business. The two thousands brought diversification and trouble in roughly equal measure: the heartburn drug Propulsid was withdrawn in two thousand after fatal heart-rhythm events; Risperdal and the epilepsy drug Topamax went generic; and the response was a launch wave — Stelara, Simponi, and the long-acting antipsychotic Invega Sustenna in two thousand nine, the blood thinner Xarelto in twenty eleven, and the prostate cancer drug Zytiga, acquired with Cougar Biotech for under a billion dollars. The era was scarred by the twenty ten consumer-products recalls, a metal-on-metal hip recall, and a failed Alzheimer's antibody. Two licensing bets from that period became legendary: ibrutinib, licensed from Pharmacyclics in twenty eleven, which became the blockbuster leukemia pill Imbruvica; and daratumumab from Genmab.

The twenty tens transformed Johnson and Johnson into a top-tier cancer company on the strength of Imbruvica and Darzalex, while Actelion — acquired in twenty seventeen for thirty billion dollars, the company's largest pharmaceutical deal — added the pulmonary hypertension franchise. Litigation escalated in parallel: a four point seven billion dollar talc verdict in Missouri, Risperdal jury awards, and opioid rulings. The pandemic years brought a single-shot COVID-nineteen vaccine that succeeded scientifically but disappointed commercially and was discontinued after rare clotting events. The defining structural move came in twenty twenty-three: Kenvue, the consumer-health business — Tylenol, Band-Aid, Listerine — was spun off, and the pharmaceutical segment was rebranded Innovative Medicine. Meanwhile the company pursued the controversial Texas two-step strategy, placing talc liabilities into subsidiaries that then filed for bankruptcy; courts rejected these bankruptcies three times, most recently in March twenty twenty-five, sending roughly sixty thousand talc claims back to the regular courts and prompting the company to reverse about seven billion dollars of reserves.

The current strategy rests on absorbing the Stelara cliff and layering new growth on top. In cancer, the depth is in multiple myeloma — four franchises touching about eighty percent of treated patients — with Carvykti guided toward five billion dollars or more at peak; in lung cancer, the Rybrevant and Lazcluze combination has shown a survival benefit against the standard of care in a common mutation type; and in bladder cancer, Inlexzo, approved in twenty twenty-five, is a device placed in the bladder that slowly releases chemotherapy — pitched as another five-billion-dollar-plus asset. In immunology, Tremfya carries the Stelara volume, and a new oral peptide called icotrokinra — approved in the United States in March twenty twenty-six as Icotyde — offers pill-based dosing with antibody-like efficacy in psoriasis. Imaavy, generic name nipocalimab, approved in April twenty twenty-five for generalized myasthenia gravis, a neuromuscular autoimmune disease, is positioned as a pipeline-in-a-product across many antibody-driven diseases. Neuroscience was rebuilt by acquisition: Caplyta, acquired with Intra-Cellular for fourteen point six billion dollars — the largest pharmaceutical deal of twenty twenty-five — plus Spravato and late-stage candidates in depression and Alzheimer's. Notably, management has explicitly chosen not to chase obesity drugs, betting on cancer, immunology, and neuroscience instead. Deal-making stays aggressive: more than thirty-two billion dollars went into research and acquisitions in twenty twenty-five, and management claims ten pipeline or newly launched assets each with five-billion-dollar-plus peak potential.

Now the risks. Stelara keeps eroding toward low single-digit billions. Xarelto faces United States generics around twenty twenty-seven, and Imbruvica is declining against newer rivals; both carry government-negotiated Medicare prices from twenty twenty-six. The deeper worry is concentration: Darzalex alone is about fifteen percent of company revenue, and its key patents run to around twenty thirty-one — the next decade-defining cliff. Talc litigation is unresolved: after the third bankruptcy rejection, tens of thousands of claims are back in court; the company has won sixteen of seventeen ovarian cancer trials and vows to litigate, though press reports in July twenty twenty-six described a possible five and a half billion dollar settlement framework.

The way to remember Johnson and Johnson: it is the industry's great portfolio manager — broad enough that no single failure sinks it, disciplined about buying growth just before it needs it, from Centocor to Actelion to Intra-Cellular. It has absorbed the loss of Risperdal, Remicade, Zytiga, and now Stelara without a down decade. The bet is that depth in myeloma, Tremfya, and a string of new launches can do it again — while the courts decide what its talc past will cost.
