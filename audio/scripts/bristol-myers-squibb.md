# Bristol Myers Squibb — audio deep-dive

Bristol Myers Squibb is a company built by merger and sustained by acquisition. Bristol-Myers was founded in eighteen eighty-seven, Squibb in eighteen fifty-eight, and the two combined in nineteen eighty-nine. Today the company is headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, and trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker B M Y. In fiscal year twenty twenty-five it booked about forty-eight point two billion dollars in revenue — essentially flat versus the prior year — with roughly thirty-four thousand employees after several rounds of restructuring. Christopher Boerner, a PhD economist who rose through the commercial side, has been chief executive since November twenty twenty-three and board chair since twenty twenty-four. The therapeutic focus areas are cancer, blood diseases, immunology, cardiovascular medicine, and neuroscience. Management frames the company as two businesses in one: a growth portfolio, about twenty-six billion dollars and expanding seventeen percent a year, now more than half of revenue; and a legacy portfolio of older drugs, about twenty-two billion dollars, shrinking fifteen percent a year as patents expire.

Start with what the company sells. Its biggest product by far is Eliquis, generic name apixaban, a blood thinner that prevents strokes in people with atrial fibrillation, an irregular heart rhythm. It is shared with Pfizer, and Bristol Myers Squibb's share came to about fourteen point four billion dollars in twenty twenty-five — nearly a third of total revenue. Second is Opdivo, generic name nivolumab, a cancer immunotherapy that blocks a brake on the immune system called the programmed death receptor. Opdivo is approved across lung, skin, kidney, and gastrointestinal cancers and brought in about ten billion dollars; a new under-the-skin version called Opdivo Qvantig launched in January twenty twenty-five to extend the franchise. Third is Orencia, generic name abatacept, a rheumatoid arthritis biologic at about three point seven billion dollars. Fourth is Revlimid, generic name lenalidomide, the multiple myeloma pill inherited from Celgene, now at three billion dollars and falling forty-six percent a year as generics take over. Fifth is Yervoy, generic name ipilimumab — the first ever checkpoint-inhibitor immunotherapy — at about two point nine billion dollars, mostly in combination with Opdivo. And the growth engines behind those include Reblozyl, generic name luspatercept, for anemia in blood disorders, up a third to two point three billion dollars; Breyanzi, a cell therapy for lymphoma built from a patient's own engineered immune cells, up eighty-two percent; Camzyos, generic name mavacamten, for a thickened-heart-muscle disease called obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, up seventy-seven percent; the psoriasis pill Sotyktu; and Cobenfy, the first genuinely new schizophrenia medicine in decades.

Now the history, which is unusually eventful. In the late nineteen nineties the company rode the cholesterol drug Pravachol, the chemotherapy Taxol, and — through an alliance with Sanofi — the anti-clotting blockbuster Plavix. Then came a run of scandals. In two thousand one it paid about two billion dollars for a stake in ImClone Systems to co-develop a cancer antibody; when regulators refused the filing that December, ImClone's founder tipped off family and friends, triggering the insider-trading affair that sent both him and Martha Stewart to prison. In two thousand two and two thousand three, Bristol Myers Squibb admitted it had inflated revenue by roughly two and a half billion dollars by stuffing wholesaler channels with inventory, restated its results, paid a one-hundred-fifty-million-dollar settlement to securities regulators, and operated under a deferred prosecution agreement. In two thousand six, a botched patent settlement let a generic maker flood the market with copycat Plavix, and the chief executive was ousted.

The recovery strategy was called the string of pearls: shed everything that wasn't pharmaceuticals and buy small biotechs with big science. The defining pearl was Medarex, acquired in two thousand nine for two point four billion dollars. Medarex brought both Yervoy and Opdivo — arguably the best value-for-money acquisition in modern pharmaceutical history, seeding a franchise worth well over ten billion dollars a year. Opdivo reached the United States market in December twenty fourteen, first among the new immunotherapies. But in August twenty sixteen a pivotal trial called CheckMate zero-two-six, testing Opdivo alone in newly diagnosed non-small cell lung cancer, failed — and Merck's rival drug Keytruda seized the largest market in cancer medicine. Opdivo never fully recovered that ground.

The next defining move came in November twenty nineteen: the seventy-four-billion-dollar acquisition of Celgene, at the time the largest pure pharmaceutical deal ever. Celgene brought Revlimid, its sister drug Pomalyst, the anemia drug Reblozyl, two cell therapies, and a drug-discovery platform built on targeted protein degradation. Bolt-ons followed: MyoKardia for thirteen point one billion dollars, which delivered Camzyos, and Turning Point Therapeutics for four point one billion. When Boerner took over in late twenty twenty-three he immediately spent about twenty-three billion dollars more: Mirati Therapeutics for the lung cancer pill Krazati; Karuna Therapeutics, at fourteen billion dollars, for the schizophrenia drug that became Cobenfy; and RayzeBio, at four point one billion, for radiopharmaceuticals — cancer drugs that deliver radioactive particles directly to tumors. He also licensed a promising two-target antibody-drug conjugate from China's SystImmune and signed a deal worth up to eleven point one billion dollars with BioNTech for a bispecific immunotherapy — notably, two of the company's biggest oncology bets originated in China.

The current strategy hinges on twenty twenty-six, which management calls its pivotal data year, with six registrational trial readouts expected. The most important asset is milvexian, an oral clot-preventer developed with Johnson and Johnson that is designed to succeed Eliquis by blocking a different clotting protein, factor eleven-a; stroke-prevention data arrive in twenty twenty-six and atrial fibrillation data in early twenty twenty-seven. Also reading out: admilparant for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, a scarring lung disease; iberdomide and mezigdomide, next-generation oral myeloma drugs from the Celgene protein-degradation platform, with approval decisions expected in August twenty twenty-six and May twenty twenty-seven respectively; a new cell therapy for myeloma; and a radiopharmaceutical for neuroendocrine tumors. Cobenfy, meanwhile, is being tested in Alzheimer's disease psychosis — a potentially enormous expansion, though its first add-on schizophrenia trial missed in April twenty twenty-five.

Now the risks, and they are stark. Eliquis — thirty percent of revenue — loses European exclusivity in twenty twenty-six and United States exclusivity around April twenty twenty-eight, and its Medicare price was cut roughly in half by government negotiation starting January twenty twenty-six. The ten-billion-dollar Opdivo franchise faces biosimilar copies in the United States around late twenty twenty-eight. The remaining Celgene legacy drugs are eroding thirty to fifty-five percent a year. Against that, the growth case leans on unproven launches and binary trial readouts — and twenty twenty-five brought a string of misses, including Camzyos in a second heart condition and Opdualag in earlier-stage melanoma. The balance sheet carries about fifty billion dollars of debt from the Celgene and Karuna deals, limiting further large acquisitions. About sixty-nine percent of revenue comes from the United States, maximizing exposure to government pricing pressure, including a most-favored-nation pricing agreement signed with the administration in twenty twenty-five.

The way to remember Bristol Myers Squibb: this is a company perpetually racing its own patent cliffs, and its fate keeps turning on single molecules. Plavix carried it through the two thousands; Medarex's antibodies and the Celgene deal carried the twenty tens; now Eliquis and Opdivo — together half of revenue — expire within three years of each other. Management has bought and built a deep, science-rich pipeline of protein degraders, cell therapies, radiopharmaceuticals, and a would-be Eliquis successor. Whether that pipeline delivers before twenty twenty-eight is the whole question.
