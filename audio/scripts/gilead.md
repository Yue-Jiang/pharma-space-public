Gilead Sciences turned antiviral chemistry into one of the most profitable franchises in medicine. It has spent the last decade trying, with mixed results, to become more than a human immunodeficiency virus company.

Gilead is headquartered in Foster City, California, and was founded in nineteen eighty-seven by Michael Riordan, a twenty-nine-year-old physician. It trades on the Nasdaq under the ticker G I L D. Daniel O'Day, who came from Roche, has served as chairman and chief executive since twenty nineteen. Gilead employs roughly seventeen thousand people. In twenty twenty-five, it generated about twenty-nine point four billion dollars in total revenue, including about twenty-eight point nine billion in product sales.

The defining concentration is human immunodeficiency virus, or H I V. Those medicines produced about twenty point eight billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, roughly seventy-two percent of product sales. Biktarvy, a once-daily combination of bictegravir, emtricitabine, and tenofovir alafenamide, generated fourteen point three billion dollars by itself. It is the best-selling H I V treatment in history and accounts for nearly half of Gilead's product revenue.

Gilead began as a small antiviral company. Two events in nineteen ninety-six shaped its future. John Martin became chief executive, and Gilead licensed oseltamivir to Roche, which sold it as Tamiflu and paid royalties for decades. The acquisition of NeXstar Pharmaceuticals in nineteen ninety-nine brought AmBisome, a liposomal antifungal, along with a real commercial organization.

The H I V empire began in two thousand one with Viread, generic name tenofovir disoproxil fumarate. It became a backbone of modern treatment. Gilead bought Triangle Pharmaceuticals for about four hundred sixty million dollars to obtain emtricitabine, then assembled its antivirals into convenient daily combinations. Truvada launched in two thousand four. Atripla, developed with Bristol Myers Squibb, followed in two thousand six as the first complete H I V regimen in one daily tablet. In twenty twelve, Truvada became the first pill approved for pre-exposure prophylaxis, or PrEP, creating a new prevention market.

The deal that most clearly defined Gilead came in twenty eleven. It paid eleven point two billion dollars for Pharmasset, a company with no marketed products, to obtain the hepatitis C compound sofosbuvir. Sovaldi, the resulting medicine, launched in late twenty thirteen as a cure for hepatitis C. Its launch price of eighty-four thousand dollars per course triggered a national debate about drug pricing and earned it the nickname the thousand-dollar pill. Follow-on combination Harvoni expanded the franchise. Hepatitis C revenue peaked around nineteen billion dollars in twenty fifteen, lifting total company revenue above thirty-two billion.

Then the cure shrank its own market. Successfully treated patients no longer needed medicine, while competitors cut prices. Hepatitis C revenue declined year after year. Gilead's liver portfolio now generates far less, although Epclusa, generic names sofosbuvir and velpatasvir, remains important.

While hepatitis C fell, Gilead executed a quieter transition in H I V. It moved from the older tenofovir formulation to tenofovir alafenamide, a version designed for lower systemic exposure. Genvoya, Descovy, Odefsey, and Vemlidy launched before older patents expired. Biktarvy arrived in twenty eighteen and dominated the treatment market.

Diversification has been much less consistent. Gilead paid eleven point nine billion dollars for Kite Pharma in twenty seventeen, entering chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy with Yescarta, generic name axicabtagene ciloleucel. Yescarta treats lymphomas and generated about one point five billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, although sales declined as competition intensified. Tecartus, generic name brexucabtagene autoleucel, is a related cell therapy for mantle cell lymphoma and adult acute lymphoblastic leukemia.

In twenty twenty, Gilead spent twenty-one billion dollars on Immunomedics to obtain Trodelvy, generic name sacituzumab govitecan. This antibody-drug conjugate became a blockbuster in breast cancer, with about one point four billion dollars in twenty twenty-five sales, but its bladder-cancer approval was withdrawn and lung-cancer studies disappointed. Other expensive bets failed more completely. The Galapagos arthritis collaboration unraveled after an American regulatory rejection. Magrolimab, obtained through the four point nine billion dollar purchase of Forty Seven, was discontinued after safety and futility problems. A push into metabolic liver disease also failed.

The coronavirus pandemic brought a temporary windfall. Veklury, generic name remdesivir, became the first approved antiviral for coronavirus disease twenty nineteen and peaked above five billion dollars in annual sales. By twenty twenty-five, revenue had fallen by roughly half to about nine hundred million dollars.

The present story centers on lenacapavir, an H I V capsid inhibitor. A twice-yearly injectable version for prevention was approved in June twenty twenty-five as Yeztugo after showing near-complete efficacy in the PURPOSE trials. It produced about one hundred fifty million dollars in its first half-year. Gilead agreed to supply up to two million people through the Global Fund on a no-profit basis and licensed generic manufacturers across one hundred twenty lower-income countries, where versions priced around forty dollars per year are expected from twenty twenty-seven. The treatment version is sold as Sunlenca for people with multidrug-resistant H I V.

Lenacapavir is becoming a platform. A once-daily combination with bictegravir has been filed with the Food and Drug Administration, with a decision expected in August twenty twenty-six. If approved, it would be a compact switch regimen for people whose virus is already suppressed. A once-weekly oral lenacapavir formulation has also been filed for prevention. In treatment, a weekly combination of lenacapavir and Merck's islatravir produced positive phase three results and is targeted for a twenty twenty-seven launch. Gilead is also exploring once-yearly dosing.

The company has unusual room to make these bets. Settlements with generic challengers set April twenty thirty-six as the earliest American entry date for Biktarvy copies. That leaves a decade without a major cliff, although the wider tenofovir alafenamide family, worth several billion dollars, may face competition around twenty thirty-one and twenty thirty-two.

Gilead is spending that runway on oncology. In early twenty twenty-six, it announced almost thirteen billion dollars of acquisitions. It paid seven point eight billion dollars for Arcellx and anito-cel, generic name anitocabtagene autoleucel, a cell therapy directed at B-cell maturation antigen for heavily treated multiple myeloma. A regulatory decision is expected in December twenty twenty-six. Gilead also paid two point two billion dollars for Ouro Medicines and its T-cell engager technology. It paid three point one five billion dollars upfront for Tubulis, a next-generation antibody-drug conjugate company. The lead Tubulis asset, now called G S eight eight two four, produced an early confirmed response rate of about sixty-one percent in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer, and Gilead plans registrational development.

Beyond H I V and cancer, Hepcludex, generic name bulevirtide, became the first approved American treatment for chronic hepatitis D in twenty twenty-six. Livdelzi, generic name seladelpar, is a growing liver medicine for primary biliary cholangitis. Emvistegrast, an oral integrin inhibitor, is an early inflammation bet in ulcerative colitis.

The risks begin with concentration. Nearly three quarters of sales come from H I V, and almost half from Biktarvy. Public-health funding cuts or pricing policy could slow prevention uptake. Medicare redesign cost Gilead about one point one billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, and a most-favored-nation agreement added new pricing commitments. Oncology must still prove that the latest acquisitions can succeed where some earlier purchases failed. Yescarta and Tecartus are already losing ground, Trodelvy's record outside breast cancer is mixed, and anito-cel enters a crowded myeloma market.

The through-line is a company that repeatedly made bold antiviral bets and won: Viread, Sovaldi, Biktarvy, and now lenacapavir. With long patent protection and a prevention drug that could reshape the H I V epidemic, Gilead's next test is whether its newest oncology investments can finally create a durable second business.
