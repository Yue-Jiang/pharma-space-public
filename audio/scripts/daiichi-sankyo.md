Daiichi Sankyo is the Japanese pharmaceutical company that transformed one chemistry platform, the antibody-drug conjugate, into one of the most valuable franchises in modern cancer medicine.

The company is headquartered in Tokyo and was formed in two thousand five by merging Sankyo, founded in eighteen ninety-nine, with Daiichi Pharmaceutical, founded in nineteen fifteen. Hiroyuki Okuzawa is president and chief executive. Daiichi Sankyo employs roughly twenty thousand people. In the fiscal year ending in March twenty twenty-six, revenue reached about two point one trillion yen, or roughly fourteen billion dollars, up around thirteen percent. The company still has cardiovascular and specialty medicines, but its future is overwhelmingly tied to oncology.

Both parent companies left important scientific legacies. Sankyo was where Akira Endo discovered compactin, the starting point for the statin class of cholesterol medicines. Sankyo's pravastatin later became one of Japan's biggest drugs. Daiichi Pharmaceutical discovered the antibiotic levofloxacin. When the companies combined, their portfolio was anchored by the blood-pressure drug Benicar, generic name olmesartan, which at its peak supplied roughly a quarter of revenue.

That dependence helped set up the first major chapter of the merged company's history: the Ranbaxy disaster. In two thousand eight, anticipating the Benicar patent cliff, Daiichi Sankyo paid four point six billion dollars for a controlling stake in India's Ranbaxy Laboratories. It was the largest acquisition in Indian pharmaceutical history. Within months, the United States Food and Drug Administration blocked imports from key factories over data-integrity and manufacturing failures. Ranbaxy pleaded guilty in twenty thirteen and paid a record five hundred million dollars in American fines.

Daiichi Sankyo exited by selling Ranbaxy to Sun Pharmaceutical in twenty fourteen, then sold its resulting Sun stake for about three point six billion dollars the next year. It recovered less than it had invested. A Singapore arbitration panel later awarded Daiichi Sankyo about five hundred twenty-five million dollars from Ranbaxy's former owners for concealing the severity of the regulatory problems. The episode remains a standard lesson in acquisition due diligence.

The Benicar cliff arrived in the United States in twenty sixteen, alongside legal settlements over marketing and a rare intestinal side effect. Daiichi Sankyo restructured and leaned on its oral blood thinner edoxaban, sold as Lixiana in many countries and Savaysa in the United States. Edoxaban became a franchise worth roughly three hundred fifty billion yen a year, although it never matched Eliquis or Xarelto in the American market.

The decisive transformation came from Daiichi Sankyo's own laboratories. The company built an antibody-drug conjugate platform called D X d, named for its deruxtecan payload. An antibody-drug conjugate is a guided delivery system: an antibody recognizes a marker on a cancer cell and carries a powerful payload to it. Daiichi Sankyo's design uses a topoisomerase one inhibitor related to exatecan, connected through a cleavable linker. Its high payload capacity and ability to affect neighboring tumor cells helped distinguish it from older conjugates.

The lead medicine paired this system with trastuzumab, the antibody behind Herceptin, to target human epidermal growth factor receptor two, usually called H E R two. Striking early clinical data in twenty seventeen attracted AstraZeneca. In March twenty nineteen, AstraZeneca paid one point three five billion dollars upfront in a deal worth up to six point nine billion dollars to co-develop what became Enhertu, generic name trastuzumab deruxtecan. The partners share costs and profits outside Japan, while Daiichi Sankyo retains exclusive Japanese rights and pays AstraZeneca a royalty.

Enhertu won its first American approval in December twenty nineteen for advanced H E R two positive breast cancer. It then beat the previous standard of care, established treatment for the newly recognized category of H E R two low breast cancer, and expanded into stomach cancer, lung cancer, and other solid tumors. Combined global sales with AstraZeneca approached six billion dollars in calendar twenty twenty-five, while Daiichi Sankyo booked roughly six hundred seventy billion yen in its fiscal year. In December twenty twenty-five, Enhertu moved into first-line metastatic H E R two positive breast cancer. In May twenty twenty-six, it added treatment around surgery for early-stage disease.

The platform also produced Datroway, generic name datopotamab deruxtecan, which targets trophoblast cell-surface antigen two, usually called TROP two. AstraZeneca paid one billion dollars upfront in a deal worth up to six billion. Datroway is approved in breast cancer and lung cancer with mutations in the epidermal growth factor receptor, and in May twenty twenty-six it added first-line metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. Its initial broader lung-cancer strategy was reduced after mixed trial results, showing that the platform does not eliminate clinical risk.

Merck supplied another major endorsement in October twenty twenty-three. It paid four billion dollars upfront, plus continuation payments and potential milestones that could take the deal to twenty-two billion dollars, for three more D X d conjugates. They target human epidermal growth factor receptor three, B seven H three, and cadherin six. Daiichi Sankyo keeps manufacturing, Japanese rights, and roughly half of the economics while sharing development and commercialization elsewhere.

One of those programs has already stumbled. Patritumab deruxtecan, aimed at H E R three, first received a manufacturing-related rejection. Its American application was then withdrawn in May twenty twenty-five after a confirmatory lung-cancer trial failed to show a statistically significant overall-survival benefit. Development continues in biomarker-guided breast and lung studies, but the program has been reset.

The next near-term candidate is ifinatamab deruxtecan, targeting B seven H three. It is under priority review for previously treated extensive-stage small cell lung cancer, with an American regulatory decision expected in October twenty twenty-six. Approval would make it the first medicine against that marker and the first entirely new D X d launch since Datroway. Raludotatug deruxtecan, aimed at C D H six, is in phase three for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Earlier programs extend the platform into other solid tumors, immune-activating payloads, and blood cancers.

Daiichi Sankyo also won an important legal battle. Seagen, later acquired by Pfizer, argued that Enhertu infringed an antibody-drug conjugate linker patent. A Texas jury initially awarded about forty-two million dollars plus royalties. In December twenty twenty-five, a federal appeals court reversed the verdict after the patent was held invalid.

Management's five-year plan targets more than three trillion yen in revenue and more than six hundred billion yen in operating profit by fiscal twenty thirty. It also aims for twenty new indication launches across five oncology medicines and a place among the world's top five oncology companies by twenty thirty-five. The strategy remains platform-centric rather than acquisition-heavy.

That concentration creates the central risk. Enhertu represents roughly a third of revenue, and much of the pipeline uses the same payload and linker. A serious class-wide safety problem, especially interstitial lung disease, or a better competing conjugate could affect several programs at once. Half of profits outside Japan flow to partners. Edoxaban faces patent expiry around twenty twenty-eight in the United States and around twenty thirty in Europe. Enhertu itself may lose American exclusivity around twenty thirty-three, so the next franchise must arrive before then.

The arc is remarkable. Daiichi Sankyo moved from a cardiovascular company damaged by a failed generics acquisition to the partner of choice for AstraZeneca and Merck in one of oncology's fastest-growing fields. Now it must prove that D X d is a repeatable platform, not simply one exceptional medicine.
