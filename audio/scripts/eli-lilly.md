# Eli Lilly — audio deep-dive

Eli Lilly is, right now, the most valuable pharmaceutical company that has ever existed. Founded in eighteen seventy-six by Colonel Eli Lilly, a Civil War veteran and pharmacist, it is still headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana, and trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker L L Y. In twenty twenty-five its market capitalization passed one trillion dollars — the first pure-play pharmaceutical company to get there — and fiscal year twenty twenty-five revenue hit about sixty-five point two billion dollars, up a staggering forty-five percent in a single year, with guidance for twenty twenty-six raised to eighty-five to eighty-seven billion. The company employs roughly fifty thousand people. David Ricks has been chairman and chief executive since January twenty seventeen. The focus areas are cardiometabolic disease — meaning diabetes and obesity — plus cancer, immunology, neuroscience, and pain.

One molecule explains the moment: tirzepatide, a weekly injection that activates two gut-hormone receptors at once — glucagon-like peptide-one and glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide. Sold as Mounjaro for type two diabetes, it did about twenty-two point nine billion dollars in twenty twenty-five; sold as Zepbound for obesity and obstructive sleep apnea, another thirteen point six billion. Together that is thirty-six and a half billion dollars — about fifty-six percent of company revenue, and the best-selling drug in the world, having overtaken Merck's Keytruda. Behind the twins: Verzenio, generic name abemaciclib, an oral pill for the most common form of breast cancer, at five point seven billion; Trulicity, generic name dulaglutide, the older weekly diabetes injection, around four billion and declining twenty percent a year as patients move to Mounjaro; Jardiance, generic name empagliflozin, a diabetes and heart failure pill sold with Boehringer Ingelheim, about three and a half billion on Lilly's books; and Taltz, generic name ixekizumab, a psoriasis antibody at roughly three point four billion. Further back sit the insulins including Humalog, the migraine-prevention antibody Emgality, the leukemia pill Jaypirca, the eczema antibody Ebglyss, and Kisunla, generic name donanemab, the Alzheimer's antibody approved in July twenty twenty-four.

Now the history, because Lilly's present dominance grew directly out of past pain. Through the nineteen nineties the company was defined by Prozac, generic name fluoxetine, the antidepressant that peaked around two point six billion dollars a year — roughly a quarter of revenue. In August two thousand a court invalidated its patent extension; generics arrived a year later, and Prozac sales collapsed more than seventy percent within twelve months. That formative cliff pushed Lilly to diversify: the antipsychotic Zyprexa, the antidepressant Cymbalta, the erectile-dysfunction pill Cialis, the chemotherapy Alimta. Zyprexa became the top seller but also a legal disaster — over a billion dollars in product-liability settlements for diabetes risk, and in January two thousand nine a one point four billion dollar federal settlement for off-label marketing, including what was then the largest criminal fine in United States history.

Then came the second great cliff, which the company internally called the years Y Z: Gemzar went generic in twenty ten, Zyprexa in twenty eleven, Cymbalta in twenty thirteen, Evista in twenty fourteen — each of the big two worth about five billion dollars a year. Revenue fell from twenty-four billion to under twenty billion. Chief executive John Lechleiter made the defining choice in modern Lilly history: he refused the defensive megamergers that consumed rivals, protected the dividend, and kept research spending above twenty percent of sales, betting everything on the internal pipeline. The bet paid off in a launch wave — Trulicity in twenty fourteen, Jardiance the same year, Taltz in twenty sixteen, Verzenio in twenty seventeen, Emgality in twenty eighteen. There were famous failures too, notably the Alzheimer's antibody solanezumab in twenty sixteen. Ricks took over in twenty seventeen, spun off the animal-health business Elanco, and bought Loxo Oncology for eight billion dollars, which delivered Jaypirca.

The transformation arrived with tirzepatide: Mounjaro launched in May twenty twenty-two, Zepbound in November twenty twenty-three, and revenue went from twenty-eight and a half billion in twenty twenty-two to sixty-five billion in twenty twenty-five — the steepest growth in big-pharma history. Along the way Lilly cut insulin list prices about seventy percent and capped out-of-pocket costs at thirty-five dollars a month, defusing a decade of pricing criticism. Business development stayed deliberately bolt-on: Dice Therapeutics for oral immunology, Versanis for a muscle-preserving obesity antibody, POINT Biopharma for radioligand cancer therapies, Morphic for oral bowel-disease drugs, and in twenty twenty-five Verve Therapeutics — a one-time gene-editing treatment aiming to permanently lower cholesterol. Lilly has committed more than fifty billion dollars to United States manufacturing expansion since twenty twenty, and in November twenty twenty-five struck a landmark pricing agreement with the Trump administration covering Medicare and Medicaid access for obesity drugs in exchange for tariff relief.

The current strategy is, first, to defend and extend obesity dominance. Orforglipron, an oral small-molecule glucagon-like peptide-one drug — a pill, not an injection, and cheaper to make at scale — was approved for obesity on April first, twenty twenty-six as Foundayo, the fastest new-medicine approval in over two decades. Retatrutide, a triple agonist adding a third hormone pathway, glucagon, posted positive Phase three results in obesity and knee osteoarthritis. Behind them: bimagrumab to preserve muscle during weight loss, and eloralintide, targeting the hormone amylin. In cardiovascular disease, lepodisiran is a gene-silencing injection against lipoprotein little-a, an inherited risk factor with no approved treatment, in Phase three. In neuroscience, Kisunla is rolling out globally and a follow-on antibody, remternetug, is in Phase three. In cancer, Verzenio has survival data in early breast cancer, Jaypirca beat Imbruvica head-to-head in leukemia, and the oral breast cancer drug Inluriyo, generic name imlunestrant, launched in late twenty twenty-five. Unlike most large-cap peers, Lilly has done no headline China in-licensing; its China play is out-licensing and market access, with Mounjaro entering China's national reimbursement list in twenty twenty-six.

Now the risks, which are really one risk wearing different costumes: concentration. Tirzepatide is about fifty-six percent of revenue — any safety signal, supply problem, or share loss hits the entire company. Novo Nordisk launched an oral rival in January twenty twenty-six, and several biotechs have Phase three obesity candidates. Prices are already eroding: realized prices fell five percent company-wide in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-five, and the government deal caps Medicare prices at two hundred forty-five dollars a month. Meanwhile the older portfolio faces its own cliffs — Trulicity in twenty twenty-seven, Jardiance in twenty twenty-eight, Verzenio around twenty twenty-nine, Taltz around twenty thirty. There is litigation over alleged stomach-paralysis injuries from the drug class, ongoing insulin-pricing suits, and a slow launch for Kisunla in Alzheimer's. And the fifty-billion-dollar manufacturing buildout assumes the obesity market keeps growing for a decade.

The way to remember Eli Lilly: it is the industry's best argument for patience. Twice its biggest product collapsed overnight — Prozac in two thousand one, then Zyprexa and Cymbalta in the early twenty tens — and twice it refused the megamerger escape hatch, choosing to out-research the cliff instead. Tirzepatide is the reward: the biggest drug on earth, discovered in-house. The question that now hangs over a trillion-dollar valuation is whether one company can stay ahead of the entire industry in obesity — with a pill, a triple agonist, and a manufacturing empire as its moat.
