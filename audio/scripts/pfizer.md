# Pfizer audio deep dive

Pfizer is the archetypal American big pharma company. It was founded in Brooklyn in eighteen forty-nine, is now headquartered in Manhattan, and trades under the ticker P F E. In fiscal year twenty twenty-five, Pfizer reported about sixty-two point six billion dollars in revenue and employed roughly seventy-five thousand people. Albert Bourla, a veterinarian by training who spent his career at Pfizer, has served as chief executive since twenty nineteen. The portfolio spans oncology, vaccines, internal medicine, inflammation and immunology, rare disease, and anti-infectives.

Start with what Pfizer sells today. Its largest product is Eliquis, generic name apixaban, a Factor Ten A inhibitor used to prevent strokes and treat blood clots. Pfizer shares the product with Bristol Myers Squibb, and Pfizer recorded about eight billion dollars from the alliance in twenty twenty-five. Next is the Prevnar family of pneumococcal vaccines, at about six and a half billion dollars. The family is transitioning from Prevnar thirteen toward broader vaccines such as Prevnar twenty.

The Vyndaqel family, generic name tafamidis, generated about six point four billion dollars. Tafamidis treats transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, a disease in which misfolded protein accumulates in the heart and makes it progressively stiffer. Better diagnosis has turned this once obscure disease into a major commercial market. Comirnaty, the COVID-nineteen messenger R N A vaccine developed with BioNTech, brought in about four point four billion dollars, far below its pandemic peak. Ibrance, generic name palbociclib, contributed about four point one billion dollars from hormone-receptor-positive, HER-two-negative breast cancer, but sales are declining as patent expiry approaches.

Other important products include Paxlovid for COVID-nineteen, Xtandi for prostate cancer, Padcev for bladder cancer, Nurtec for migraine, Abrysvo for respiratory syncytial virus, Lorbrena for A L K positive lung cancer, and Elrexfio for multiple myeloma. The pattern matters. Pfizer has several large franchises, but many of its biggest earners are approaching loss of exclusivity at nearly the same time.

Pfizer's modern history explains why acquisition strategy is so central to the company. In the late nineteen nineties, Pfizer had famous primary-care drugs such as Viagra, but the decisive asset was Lipitor, generic name atorvastatin. Pfizer co-promoted Lipitor for Warner-Lambert. When Warner-Lambert tried to merge with another company in nineteen ninety-nine, Pfizer launched a hostile counterbid and acquired Warner-Lambert for roughly ninety billion dollars. Lipitor became the best-selling medicine in pharmaceutical history, peaking near thirteen billion dollars per year.

Pfizer then bought Pharmacia for about sixty billion dollars in two thousand three. Scale did not eliminate scientific risk. The painkiller Bextra was withdrawn in two thousand five. Torcetrapib, intended to become a major cholesterol medicine after Lipitor, was abandoned in two thousand six when a Phase three trial showed increased deaths. In two thousand nine, Pfizer paid two point three billion dollars to settle federal allegations of illegal off-label promotion. At the time, it was the largest healthcare-fraud settlement in United States history.

Also in two thousand nine, Pfizer bought Wyeth for sixty-eight billion dollars. The deal brought the Prevnar vaccine franchise and stronger biologics capabilities just before Lipitor lost United States exclusivity in twenty eleven. Pfizer then simplified itself. It sold nutrition to Nestle, spun animal health into Zoetis, and later separated its off-patent Upjohn business, which merged with Mylan to form Viatris. Attempts to acquire AstraZeneca and merge with Allergan failed, so Pfizer shifted toward smaller acquisitions and innovative products.

The COVID pandemic produced an extraordinary second windfall. Pfizer and BioNTech delivered the first authorized COVID-nineteen vaccine in December twenty twenty, followed by the antiviral Paxlovid. Pfizer's revenue reached one hundred point three billion dollars in twenty twenty-two, a pharmaceutical industry record. The reversal was equally dramatic. As COVID demand collapsed, revenue fell to fifty-eight and a half billion dollars in twenty twenty-three.

Pfizer invested the windfall in acquisitions. It bought Biohaven for migraine, Global Blood Therapeutics for sickle-cell disease, and Seagen for forty-three billion dollars to gain a leading antibody-drug-conjugate platform. Results have been mixed. Oxbryta, the centerpiece of the Global Blood Therapeutics deal, was withdrawn worldwide in twenty twenty-four. The oral obesity drug danuglipron was discontinued in twenty twenty-five after a liver-injury case. Pfizer also recorded several large pipeline impairments. Those setbacks explain why the current strategy is both aggressive and urgent.

The first strategic bet is obesity. Pfizer acquired Metsera in November twenty twenty-five after a public bidding contest with Novo Nordisk. The deal was worth about seven billion dollars in cash, with contingent payments that could bring the total close to ten billion. Its lead asset is P F zero eight six five three nine four four, formerly called M E T zero nine seven eye. This ultra-long-acting glucagon-like peptide-one receptor agonist is designed for weekly and eventually monthly injection. Pfizer is advancing a ten-trial Phase three program in twenty twenty-six and targets a possible twenty twenty-eight launch. The deal also brought a monthly amylin analog and an oral glucagon-like peptide-one program. This is a long-dated bet, and management expects it to dilute earnings through twenty thirty.

The second bet is oncology. Pfizer wants eight or more blockbuster cancer medicines by twenty thirty, supported by the Seagen antibody-drug-conjugate engine and a high volume of pivotal trials. P F zero eight six three four four zero four, also called S S G J seven-oh-seven, is a bispecific antibody against P D one and vascular endothelial growth factor. Pfizer licensed it from Three S Bio for one point two five billion dollars upfront, plus as much as four point eight billion in milestones, and has moved it into Phase three lung and colorectal cancer programs.

The breast-cancer pipeline includes atirmociclib, a selective C D K four inhibitor intended to succeed Ibrance, and prifetrastat, an epigenetic drug for patients whose disease has progressed after C D K four and six inhibitors. Sasanlimab has positive Phase three data in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer. Several Seagen-originated antibody-drug conjugates remain important, including sigvotatug vedotin, fetrastobart vedotin, and a C E A C A M five-directed program. But the risk is real. A second-line lung-cancer trial of sigvotatug vedotin missed overall survival in June twenty twenty-six, although first-line studies continue.

Pfizer also participated in a genuine platform milestone in May twenty twenty-six. Veppanu, generic name vepdegestrant, became the first Food and Drug Administration approved P R O T A C, or proteolysis-targeting chimera. It degrades the estrogen receptor in a genetically defined form of advanced breast cancer. Global rights have since been licensed to Rigel, with Pfizer and Arvinas sharing royalties, so the approval is scientifically important without becoming a fully owned Pfizer launch.

The third strategic bet is policy positioning. In September twenty twenty-five, Pfizer became the first drugmaker to sign a most-favored-nation pricing agreement with the Trump administration. The arrangement included lower Medicaid pricing, discounted direct-to-consumer sales, most-favored-nation launch pricing for new drugs, and a pledge of seventy billion dollars in United States research and manufacturing investment. In return, Pfizer received a three-year exemption from threatened pharmaceutical tariffs. The exact commercial tradeoff remains difficult to judge because some pricing terms are confidential.

The central risk is the patent cliff. Products representing more than twenty billion dollars of twenty twenty-five revenue could lose exclusivity by about twenty thirty. Ibrance and Xtandi face pressure around twenty twenty-seven. Eliquis and the Vyndaqel family follow around twenty twenty-eight. The Prevnar franchise must transition successfully to newer vaccines. At the same time, COVID revenue is still declining, Medicare-negotiated prices are arriving, and Pfizer's largest new growth assets remain clinically unproven.

The simplest way to remember Pfizer is this: Pfizer repeatedly buys the platform or product for its next era. Warner-Lambert brought Lipitor. Wyeth brought Prevnar. The BioNTech partnership brought Comirnaty. Seagen brought antibody-drug conjugates. Metsera brought a return to obesity. That model has produced historic successes, but it has also produced expensive disappointments. The question for the next five years is whether obesity and oncology can mature fast enough to replace the revenue lost from Eliquis, Vyndaqel, Ibrance, Xtandi, and older vaccines.
