# Radioligand therapy (radiopharmaceuticals)

## What it is
A radioligand therapy (RLT) is a targeting molecule chemically welded to a radioactive atom. The targeting half — a peptide, small molecule, or antibody — binds a protein enriched on tumour cells; the radioactive half sits in a chemical cage (a *chelator*) and destroys nearby DNA by emitting radiation from inside the tumour. It is radiotherapy delivered by the bloodstream rather than aimed from outside the body, which is why it reaches metastases that an external beam cannot. Pluvicto is the clearest example: a small molecule binding **PSMA** (prostate-specific membrane antigen, present in more than 80% of prostate cancers) carrying **lutetium-177**, a beta emitter. The same targeting chemistry, loaded with a weaker imaging isotope instead, produces a companion PET scan — so the modality comes with a built-in biomarker test: scan first, treat only patients whose tumours light up.

## Why it matters
Roughly 30–50% of cancer patients receive radiotherapy at some point, and the strategic pitch is to replace that blunt instrument with a targeted one. The commercial logic is unusual: the barrier to entry is not the molecule but the **supply chain**. Lutetium-177 has a half-life of about 6.6 days and actinium-225 about 10 days, so a dose cannot be stockpiled — it is manufactured, released, shipped, and infused against a decaying clock, patient by patient, with no inventory buffer. That makes the moat physical rather than intellectual: reactors and cyclotrons that produce the isotope, radiochemistry plants near the patients, licensed radiopharmacies at the hospital end, and a logistics network that hits a delivery window. Novartis quotes **delivery to US treatment sites within 5 days** and has **five US RLT manufacturing sites operational or under construction**. A competitor with a better molecule and no isotope pipeline cannot launch.

**Pluvicto's 2026 expansion is the year's most consequential RLT event.** On **31 July 2026** the FDA approved Pluvicto with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor for **PSMA-positive metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer**, based on Phase 3 **PSMAddition**: risk of progression or death cut 28% at primary analysis (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.58–0.90) and 33% at an updated analysis (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.55–0.82), with an immature but favourable overall survival trend (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.63–1.01). Novartis says this **nearly doubles the eligible population**. Grade ≥3 adverse events were 50.7% with Pluvicto plus standard of care versus 43.0% for standard of care alone. The open question is the final overall survival readout — approval in a hormone-sensitive setting without mature survival data invites payer and guideline scrutiny.

**How the field was assembled — by acquisition, in two waves.** Novartis moved first and alone: **Advanced Accelerator Applications (AAA), ~$3.9B, closed January 2018**, which brought Lutathera and the European radiopharmacy network; then **Endocyte, $2.1B ($24/share), closed 21 December 2018**, which brought 177Lu-PSMA-617 — the molecule that became Pluvicto. Everyone else waited for Pluvicto to prove the market, then bought in within six months of each other: **Eli Lilly / POINT Biopharma, ~$1.4B (October 2023)**; **Bristol Myers Squibb / RayzeBio, $4.1B (December 2023)**; **AstraZeneca / Fusion Pharmaceuticals, ~$2.0B cash plus a $3/share contingent value right for up to $2.4B (announced 19 March 2024, closed 4 June 2024** — AstraZeneca's accounts record total consideration of $2,195M, of which $848M was assigned to the FPI-2265 programme). Novartis added **Mariana Oncology (~$1.75B, 2024)** to keep the pipeline fed. Note what was bought in three of four cases: not just a molecule but **actinium supply**, because alpha emitters are the next generation and the isotope is scarce.

## The graveyard
Radioligand therapy has a thinner graveyard than most modalities, and that thinness is itself informative: the mechanism — put a radiation source inside the tumour — is not in scientific doubt, so failures cluster around *delivery* and *commercial fit* rather than biology.

- **Target-level lessons are rare.** PSMA and somatostatin receptor 2 both worked at first pass, largely because both were validated as *imaging* targets years before anyone tried therapy. The diagnostic scan is a cheap dress rehearsal that most modalities do not get.
- **Molecule/logistics-level failures dominate.** Bayer's **Xofigo (radium-223)** is the KB's cautionary tale: acquired for roughly $2.9B via Algeta in 2014, technically effective in bone metastases, but a bone-seeking agent with no tumour-targeting ligand had a narrow niche and never became a franchise (the drug index carries it as `withdrawn`). Earlier US bone-pain radiopharmaceuticals — samarium-153, strontium-89 — were the same shape: approvable, unscalable.
- **Supply is the recurring near-death experience.** Pluvicto's own launch was constrained by manufacturing rather than demand, which is why Novartis lists site count in press releases and why the KB flags the radioligand supply chain as a Novartis risk. For the entrants, the equivalent risk is an approved actinium-225 drug and no isotope to make it with — Ac-225 comes from a handful of facilities worldwide.
- **Untested:** whether the alpha-over-beta thesis survives head-to-head data. AstraZeneca's FPI-2265 is positioned in patients who have already progressed on Pluvicto — a sequencing claim, not a superiority claim.

## Competitive dynamics
Novartis is not the leader in this modality; it is, for now, effectively the entire market. Two marketed products, ~$2.8B of combined 2025 revenue, both of the field's proven targets, the deepest manufacturing footprint, and — after the July 2026 mHSPC approval — the only PSMA agent covering the full metastatic spectrum of prostate cancer. That position was bought for about $6B in 2018, before anyone else believed in the category, and it is the clearest example in the KB of a platform bet paying off on a 6-8 year horizon.

The attackers have chosen chemistry as their axis of differentiation: **alpha emitters instead of beta**. Alpha particles deposit far more energy over a much shorter distance, which should mean more DNA damage inside the tumour and less to surrounding tissue. BMS (RayzeBio) and AstraZeneca (Fusion) both bought actinium-225 platforms with in-house isotope supply for exactly this reason. Lilly's POINT deal is the odd one out — lutetium-based, so competing with Pluvicto on the same physics — and Lilly's own file frames radioligands as one of several modality bets alongside siRNA, in vivo editing and gene therapy, not as a franchise-defining commitment. Bayer, having been in the field a decade before Novartis via Algeta, is now a spectator.

Two head-to-head fights are forming. In **prostate cancer**, AstraZeneca's FPI-2265 targets the population Pluvicto has already treated — a strategy that grows as Pluvicto moves earlier, because every patient treated in the hormone-sensitive setting becomes a post-Pluvicto patient later. In **neuroendocrine tumours**, BMS's actinium-based RYZ101 attacks Lutathera directly, with Phase 3 data due in 2026 and Lutathera simultaneously defending US patent litigation — a rare case where the incumbent faces a mechanism upgrade and an IP challenge in the same year.

The unresolved structural question is the hospital constraint. Every dose requires a licensed radiopharmacy, radiation-safety handling, and a nuclear medicine department — infrastructure concentrated at academic centres and thin in community oncology. Until that changes, this modality's ceiling is set less by biology or competition than by how many sites can physically administer it.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radioligand / radiopharmaceutical | A targeting molecule joined to a radioactive atom. "Radioconjugate" (AstraZeneca's term) means the same thing. |
| Isotope / radionuclide | The radioactive atom doing the killing. Lu-177, Ac-225, Ra-223. |
| Beta emitter | Emits electrons; moderate energy over a few millimetres. Lu-177 (Pluvicto, Lutathera). |
| Alpha emitter | Emits helium nuclei; very high energy over a few cell diameters. Ac-225, Ra-223. The next-generation bet. |
| Half-life | Time for half the radioactivity to decay. Lu-177 ≈ 6.6 days, Ac-225 ≈ 10 days — the reason doses cannot be stockpiled. |
| Chelator | The chemical cage holding the isotope so it doesn't detach in the bloodstream. The "tetraxetan" in Pluvicto's generic name is one. |
| PSMA | Prostate-specific membrane antigen; present in >80% of prostate cancers. |
| SSTR2 | Somatostatin receptor 2; the neuroendocrine-tumour target (Lutathera, RYZ101). |
| PRRT | Peptide receptor radionuclide therapy — the older name for the Lutathera approach. |
| Theranostic | A paired diagnostic scan and therapy using the same targeting molecule. Scan selects the patient; therapy treats them. |
| mCRPC / mHSPC | Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (progressing despite hormone suppression) vs metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (earlier, still responding). Moving from the first to the second is how a prostate drug doubles its market. |
