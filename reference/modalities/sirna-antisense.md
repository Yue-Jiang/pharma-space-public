# siRNA and antisense oligonucleotides (RNA silencing)

## What it is
Conventional drugs act on proteins — they block an enzyme or a receptor after the body has already made it. RNA-silencing drugs act one step earlier, on the **messenger RNA** that carries a gene's instructions to the protein factory, so the protein is never produced. Two chemistries do this. **Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs)** are short single strands of modified DNA/RNA that pair with the target mRNA and either recruit an enzyme (RNase H) to chop it up or bend how it is spliced. **Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs)** are double-stranded and hijack a natural cellular machine, the RNA interference (RNAi) pathway, which shreds any mRNA matching the guide strand. Practically, ASOs are more versatile (they can *change* splicing, not just silence) while siRNAs are more durable — an siRNA dose can suppress its target for months because the loaded machinery keeps cutting.

## Why it matters
The modality solves a targeting problem and a compliance problem at once. **Targeting:** if a disease is caused by a protein that small molecules cannot grip — an intracellular scaffold, a lipoprotein, an amyloid precursor — sequence complementarity gives you a drug design rule instead of a screening campaign. Any gene with a known sequence is in principle addressable, which is why these platforms move from target to target faster than antibody or small-molecule discovery.

**Compliance:** this is the commercially larger point, and it comes from the liver. Nearly all marketed siRNAs are **GalNAc conjugates** — the drug carries a sugar (N-acetylgalactosamine) that binds a receptor found almost exclusively on liver cells (hepatocytes), which vacuum the drug out of the bloodstream. That is a free delivery system to the organ that makes cholesterol, clotting factors, transthyretin, and most circulating proteins of cardiometabolic interest, and it lets a subcutaneous injection last months. **Leqvio (inclisiran) is dosed twice a year after two loading doses.** In cardiovascular prevention, where roughly half of patients stop taking their daily statin within a year, "two injections at the doctor's office annually" is not a convenience feature — it is the entire commercial thesis. It also changes the buyer: a twice-yearly injectable administered under a medical benefit can be delivered through health systems and quality-metric programmes rather than pharmacy adherence.

**The Lp(a) race is the modality's biggest single question.** Lipoprotein(a) is a genetically determined, statin-immune cardiovascular risk factor affecting perhaps one in five people, with **no approved therapy at all**. Amgen's olpasiran (siRNA) and Novartis/Ionis's pelacarsen (ASO) are both running cardiovascular-outcomes trials, and Amgen has already extended into first-event primary prevention with OCEAN(a)-PreEvent. Lowering Lp(a) by >90% is settled; whether that prevents heart attacks is not. If it does, RNA silencing acquires a population-scale preventive indication and the modality's centre of gravity shifts permanently out of rare disease.

## The graveyard
The failure pattern here is unusually clean, and the split between target-level and molecule-level lessons is the thing to learn from this page.

| Asset | Company | What happened | Lesson type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tominersen | Roche (Ionis-originated) | Huntington's ASO halted 2021 for lack of efficacy; higher doses appeared to do harm | **Target/organ-level.** Silencing huntingtin worked biochemically; the CNS delivery, dose, and disease-stage assumptions did not. |
| Mongersen (GED-0301) | Celgene/BMS | Phase 3 Crohn's futility after $710M in-licensing; data-monitoring-committee stop; 4 trials terminated | **Molecule + diligence.** Un-replicated single-country Phase 2 data is the classic overpay setup. |
| Eplontersen in ATTR-CM | AstraZeneca / Ionis | CARDIO-TTRansform missed its endpoint in 2026 | **Indication-level, not program death** — Wainua remains marketed in polyneuropathy; the cardiomyopathy expansion (the big market) is gone. |
| Diranersen (BIIB080) | Biogen / Ionis | Phase 2 CELIA primary endpoint missed May 2026 | **Target-level doubt.** Engaging the target (tau fell) without clinical benefit is the classic neurology failure signature. |
| Revusiran | Alnylam | Phase 3 ENDEAVOUR in ATTR cardiomyopathy stopped in 2016 after an imbalance in deaths; the molecule was abandoned (not currently in `05_graveyard.md`) | **Molecule-level, most instructive on this page.** Same company, same target, same disease — and the successors patisiran, vutrisiran and now nucresiran all worked. First-generation conjugate chemistry failed; RNAi did not. |

Two structural cautions the registry makes visible. First, **RNA silencing has an organ problem, not a mechanism problem**: the wins cluster in the liver (PCSK9, TTR, APOC3, prekallikrein, LPA) where GalNAc solves delivery, while the losses cluster in the central nervous system (tominersen, diranersen) where intrathecal injection into spinal fluid delivers unevenly, and in tissues nobody can reach efficiently at all — muscle, kidney, heart. Novartis buying Avidity's antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate platform is a direct bet on that being the next problem worth solving. Second, **partnered readouts reprice both sides**: Ionis is the counterparty behind an entire modality, so its 2026 eplontersen setback hit AstraZeneca's cardiometabolic pipeline and Ionis's own valuation simultaneously (see `players/ionis.md`).

## Competitive dynamics
There are two platform originators and a set of large-cap licensees. **Ionis** (Carlsbad, founded 1989 as Isis) industrialised antisense and licensed most of its early output out; Spinraza is Biogen's, Wainua is AstraZeneca's, pelacarsen is Novartis's, bepirovirsen is GSK's. It now markets seven medicines itself, and targets cash-flow breakeven in 2028 — the classic platform-company transition from royalty collector to commercial operator. **Alnylam** owns the siRNA side, and 2025 was the year the thesis paid: product revenue rose roughly 81% to about $3.0B on the strength of Amvuttra's ATTR-cardiomyopathy launch, with FY2026 guidance around $5.3B at the midpoint (~71% growth) under its "Alnylam 2030" plan.

Among the covered 22, **Novartis has the most coherent RNA strategy** — Leqvio marketed and growing 57%, pelacarsen in outcomes trials, plus Regulus and Avidity acquired to extend the platform beyond the liver. Its file describes siRNA/RNA-targeting as one of three defining modality bets alongside radioligands and gene therapy. **Biogen's** exposure is narrower and older: Spinraza is a mature franchise defending against Zolgensma and oral risdiplam, and the newer ASOs (Qalsody, diranersen, salanersen) are attempts to renew a neurology position rather than expand a platform. **Amgen** treats olpasiran as a single high-conviction cardiovascular bet, layered onto the Repatha franchise it already runs against PCSK9 — meaning Amgen and Novartis compete on PCSK9 (antibody vs siRNA) and then again on Lp(a) (siRNA vs ASO). **AstraZeneca** and **GSK** are licensees with one asset each.

The head-to-head worth watching is **Leqvio versus Repatha**, because it is a clean test of modality economics rather than biology. Both lower LDL by roughly 50-60% via PCSK9; Repatha is a fortnightly or monthly antibody with completed outcomes data (and a 2025 label extension into high-risk primary prevention), Leqvio is a twice-yearly siRNA whose own outcomes trial is still running. Leqvio grew 57% in 2025 while priced against an established competitor — early evidence that dosing interval alone can win share. If the Lp(a) trials then read out positive for either sponsor, RNA silencing stops being a rare-disease platform and becomes preventive cardiology's delivery mechanism.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mRNA (messenger RNA) | The transcript carrying a gene's instructions to the ribosome. The target of this whole modality. |
| ASO (antisense oligonucleotide) | Single-stranded synthetic nucleic acid. Degrades the mRNA (RNase H mechanism) or redirects splicing. Generic names end in **-rsen** (nusinersen, tofersen, eplontersen). |
| siRNA / RNAi | Double-stranded RNA that loads into the cell's RNA interference machinery to cut matching mRNA. Generic names end in **-siran** (inclisiran, olpasiran, vutrisiran, zilebesiran). |
| GalNAc conjugate | N-acetylgalactosamine sugar attached to the drug; binds the asialoglycoprotein receptor on liver cells. The reason the liver is this modality's home organ. |
| Splice-switching | Changing how a gene's RNA is assembled rather than destroying it. Spinraza makes cells read the backup gene SMN2 correctly. |
| Intrathecal | Injected into the spinal fluid — how CNS ASOs are given, because oligonucleotides do not cross the blood-brain barrier. |
| Lp(a) / lipoprotein(a) | Genetically set, statin-unresponsive cardiovascular risk particle. No approved therapy; the modality's largest prize. |
| PCSK9 | Liver protein that degrades LDL receptors. Blocking it lowers LDL cholesterol; targeted by antibodies (Repatha, Praluent) and by siRNA (Leqvio). |
| ATTR / ATTR-CM | Transthyretin amyloidosis — misfolded TTR protein depositing in nerves (polyneuropathy) or heart muscle (cardiomyopathy). The cardiomyopathy population is far larger, which is why every TTR programme chases it. |
| Loading dose | Initial closely spaced doses to reach steady state before maintenance. Leqvio: day 0, month 3, then every 6 months. |
