# Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs)

## What it is
An ADC is a three-part molecule: a **monoclonal antibody** that binds a protein found mostly on tumour cells (the *target* or *antigen*), a highly potent chemotherapy drug (the *payload* or *warhead*), and a chemical tether between them (the *linker*). Given intravenously, the antibody circulates until it finds the antigen, the cell swallows the complex, and the linker releases the payload inside the cell — the idea being that chemotherapy too toxic to give systemically becomes tolerable when delivered to the tumour. Two design numbers matter: the **drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR)**, how many payload molecules ride on each antibody, and whether the linker is *cleavable* (payload can leak out and kill neighbouring cells — the **bystander effect**, which is why ADCs work on tumours with only low antigen levels). The class dates to the 1990s but became the industry's favourite oncology modality only after 2019, when a new generation of linker-payload chemistry made the therapeutic window wide enough to matter.

## Why it matters
ADCs solve a specific problem: many validated tumour antigens are not *drivers* of the cancer, so blocking them does nothing — but they still make excellent postal addresses. That converts a long list of "undruggable" surface proteins into targets, and it lets a company re-enter tumour types where naked antibodies plateaued. The economics are the other half of the story: **the platform, not the drug, is the asset.** A proven linker-payload chemistry can be pointed at antigen after antigen, so buyers pay platform prices — Pfizer paid **$43B for Seagen** (2023) for the vedotin platform, Gilead **$21B for Immunomedics** (2020), AbbVie **$10.1B for ImmunoGen** (2024). Daiichi Sankyo never sold itself and instead rented its DXd platform out twice: AstraZeneca paid roughly **$2.35B upfront** across the Enhertu (March 2019) and Datroway (July 2020) collaborations, and Merck paid **$4B upfront, up to $22B** in October 2023 for three more DXd ADCs. The cost is permanent profit-sharing: headline "combined sales" for Enhertu overstate what Daiichi Sankyo keeps.

**The category that Enhertu invented.** Before 2022, "HER2-negative" breast cancer meant no HER2 drug applied. **DESTINY-Breast04** (2022) showed Enhertu beat physician's-choice chemotherapy in tumours with only faint HER2 staining (median progression-free survival 9.9 vs 5.1 months; overall survival 23.4 vs 16.8 months, HR 0.64), creating **HER2-low** as a treatment category and a diagnostic market overnight. That is the modality's signature achievement: a bystander-effect payload redefined who counts as a candidate.

**China is now a primary ADC source.** GSK licensed Hansoh's B7-H3 ADC HS-20093 in December 2023 for **$185M upfront plus up to $1.525B in milestones** (after $85M upfront for the B7-H4 asset HS-20089 two months earlier). Merck's TROP2 franchise is Kelun-Biotech's molecule: sac-TMT has four China approvals, met its primary endpoint in first-line metastatic TNBC (OptiTROP-Breast03, May 2026), and in **OptiTROP-Lung06 (July 2026) became the first ADC-plus-checkpoint-inhibitor combination to win a Phase 3 in first-line PD-L1-negative non-squamous NSCLC**; a separate ASCO 2026 readout showed a 65% reduction in progression risk versus Keytruda alone in PD-L1-positive first-line NSCLC. Chinese biotechs run large trials fast and cheap, and out-licensing became their business model during the 2021–24 domestic funding winter.

**The class risk to watch: interstitial lung disease (ILD).** Deruxtecan ADCs cause inflammation and scarring of lung tissue at rates no oncologist can ignore — adjudicated ILD/pneumonitis was **12.1% in DESTINY-Breast04 with three grade 5 (fatal) events (0.8%)**, and all-grade 16.7% versus 3.4% for Kadcyla in DESTINY-Breast03. Datopotamab deruxtecan has shown roughly 8% ILD in NSCLC including fatal cases. Because the mechanism is thought to relate to high DAR plus payload permeability, it is a **platform-level** risk: one class-wide safety action would hit every DXd asset at once, which is Daiichi Sankyo's central concentration risk.

## Competitive dynamics
Daiichi Sankyo leads on merit and on chemistry. Enhertu is the modality's only true mega-brand and moved to **first-line HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer in December 2025** — the first new option in that setting in a decade, triggering a $150M milestone to Daiichi. But the leadership is shared by contract: half of ex-Japan Enhertu and Datroway profits go to AstraZeneca, and the Merck-partnered DXd assets carry the same structure. Analysts at Jefferies argued in 2026 that Daiichi's next-generation pipeline lags rivals just as its two lead ADCs approach peak, and Enhertu US loss of exclusivity around 2033 (est.) means the successor franchise has to be built now.

Pfizer bought scale rather than growing it, and the Seagen integration has been uneven: Padcev is a genuine franchise in bladder cancer, but sigvotatug vedotin missed in 2026 and disitamab vedotin was deprioritised. Roche occupies a curious position — it created the class commercially with Kadcyla, watched Enhertu take the second-line standard away, and now runs Polivy as its healthiest ADC (+38% in FY2025) in lymphoma, where Daiichi is only now arriving. Gilead's Trodelvy is solid in breast cancer and unconvincing outside it. AbbVie bought two: Elahere is a clean first-in-class win in ovarian cancer and Emrelis extends the vedotin logic to c-Met lung cancer, but Rova-T remains the cautionary tale on its own balance sheet.

The live battle is **TROP2**, where three platforms now collide: Gilead's Trodelvy (approved, entrenched in breast), Daiichi/AstraZeneca's Datroway (approved, expanding into lung), and Merck/Kelun's sac-TMT (China-approved, and the first to combine an ADC with a checkpoint inhibitor successfully in first-line lung cancer). Whoever converts first-line lung cancer takes the largest oncology revenue pool in the world. The second battle is **B7-H3**, where Daiichi/Merck's ifinatamab deruxtecan and GSK/Hansoh's risvutatug rezetecan are racing in small-cell lung cancer — a tumour type with no good options and, in Rova-T, a memorable graveyard entry.

## Key terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Payload / warhead | The cytotoxic drug carried by the antibody. DXd and govitecan are topoisomerase I inhibitors; vedotin (MMAE) and mafodotin (MMAF) are microtubule inhibitors; PBD dimers are DNA cross-linkers. |
| Linker | Chemistry joining payload to antibody. *Cleavable* linkers release payload that can diffuse to neighbouring cells; *non-cleavable* ones do not. |
| DAR (drug-to-antibody ratio) | Average payload molecules per antibody. DXd runs ~8; higher DAR means more potency and usually more toxicity. |
| Bystander effect | Released payload killing adjacent cells that lack the target — the reason HER2-low tumours respond. |
| HER2-low | Faint HER2 staining (IHC 1+, or 2+ without gene amplification). Not a biological discovery so much as a commercial category created by DESTINY-Breast04. |
| ILD / pneumonitis | Interstitial lung disease: inflammation and scarring of lung tissue. The signature deruxtecan-class toxicity. |
| Accelerated approval | US approval on a surrogate endpoint, conditional on a confirmatory trial. A miss triggers withdrawal (Blenrep, Trodelvy-bladder). |
| Alliance revenue | Money booked from a partner's sales rather than your own — why Enhertu "combined sales" ≠ any one company's revenue. |
