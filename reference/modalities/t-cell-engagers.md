# T-cell engagers (TCEs)

## What it is
A T-cell engager is an antibody-derived protein with two different grippers. One arm binds **CD3**, part of the receptor complex on essentially every T cell; the other binds a marker on the cancer cell. Holding both at once drags a T cell against the tumour cell and triggers it to kill — no genetic engineering, no cells removed from the body. Because CD3 is a universal handle on any patient's T cells, the modality reduces to choosing the second arm: CD19, CD20, BCMA, GPRC5D, DLL3, STEAP1. The marketed-assets table below contains **10 T-cell engagers across 7 companies**. This modality count is more reliable than counting the graph's CD3 target node, because graph target edges can omit the recruiting arm or include anti-CD3 antibodies that are not engagers.

## Why it matters
TCEs deliver much of CAR-T's mechanism as an **off-the-shelf** drug: no apheresis, no weeks-long manufacturing wait, no factory slot, no certified cell-therapy centre — a vial in a fridge, given in a clinic. Responses are typically less deep and less durable than CAR-T's, and treatment is continuous rather than one-and-done, but the addressable population is far larger and the cost of goods is that of an ordinary biologic. That trade is why TCEs are eroding CAR-T's late-line franchises (Gilead's Yescarta/Tecartus fell ~7% in 2025) while their own sales compound: J&J's Tecvayli and Talvey sit inside a Carvykti+Tecvayli+Talvey block worth ~$3.0B in FY2025.

The frontier moves in two directions. **Into solid tumours**, historically the hardest ground for T-cell redirection, where DLL3 (Imdelltra) proved the concept and STEAP1/KLK2 in prostate are the next tests. And **earlier in the treatment line**: J&J is pushing Tecvayli + Darzalex Faspro combinations into second-line myeloma, directly against CAR-T. Both raise the bar on tolerability, since less sick outpatients will not accept the toxicity late-line patients do.

## The graveyard
See `reference/05_graveyard.md`. The instructive entries are Amgen's two BCMA engagers, killed for **format**, not target:

- **pacanalotamab (AMG 420)** — a canonical BiTE requiring continuous IV infusion. It hit ~70% overall response in relapsed myeloma at the maximum tolerated dose, then was shelved (2021) because the delivery burden was unacceptable. Lesson: a working molecule with an unusable dosing schedule is not a drug.
- **pavurutamab (AMG 701)** — the half-life-extended follow-up. Discontinued August 2022 after a 2021 CRS-driven FDA enrollment pause; >50% CRS incidence, and by then teclistamab and cilta-cel had taken the BCMA window. Lesson: third-to-market on a validated target with a slower asset is a kill criterion.

BCMA × CD3 was then validated three times over (teclistamab, elranatamab, linvoseltamab). This is a **molecule-level** graveyard, not a target-level one — and the distinction matters, because the target-level reading would have condemned a mechanism that went on to produce three approvals.

Regulatory attrition is the live risk. Regeneron's odronextamab has taken **two US complete response letters** (2024, 2025) and remains marketed only in the EU. Roche took a **CRL for Columvi plus chemotherapy in second-line DLBCL in July 2025**, the FDA questioning whether the STARGLO trial's largely ex-US population applied to US practice. Neither mechanism is in doubt; both are launch-level failures of trial design and geography.

## Competitive dynamics

**The CD20 cluster is four companies fighting over label breadth, not biology.** Roche fields two (Lunsumio in follicular lymphoma, Columvi in DLBCL) and is the only player with a franchise strategy spanning both; AbbVie and Genmab share Epkinly, whose subcutaneous administration is its differentiator against Columvi's IV; Regeneron's odronextamab is stranded in Europe. All four are broadly comparable in mechanism, so the fight is over dosing convenience, which line of therapy the label permits, and whether combination data hold up in front of the FDA — precisely where Roche just lost.

**Myeloma is the crowded battlefield.** Three BCMA engagers (Tecvayli, Elrexfio, Lynozyfic) plus one GPRC5D engager (Talvey) plus two BCMA CAR-Ts (Carvykti, Abecma) chase the same relapsed population. J&J's structural advantage is owning both antigens: when a tumour stops expressing BCMA — **antigen escape** — the patient moves to Talvey and stays inside the J&J franchise. Management claims roughly 80% of myeloma patients touch a J&J drug. Pfizer and Regeneron, holding a single BCMA asset each, arrived into a market already sequenced around someone else's portfolio, which is why both remain sub-$0.5B.

**Amgen owns the format's history and, currently, its most interesting frontier.** It bought the BiTE platform with Micromet ($1.16B, 2012), turned it into blinatumomab, then spent a decade failing to repeat the trick in myeloma. Imdelltra vindicated the platform in small cell lung cancer — a disease with almost no therapeutic options — and converted to full FDA approval in November 2025 after the Phase 3 DeLLphi-304 confirmatory trial showed an overall-survival benefit versus chemotherapy. Rova-T, AbbVie's DLL3 **ADC**, had failed on the same target in 2019 (worse overall survival than topotecan, $5.8B written off). Same target, different modality, opposite outcome: DLL3 was a fine target that the antibody-drug-conjugate format could not exploit.

**The shared constraint with CAR-T is toxicity management.** TCEs cause CRS and ICANS by the same mechanism, so they inherit **step-up dosing** (small priming doses to blunt the first cytokine surge) and, initially, inpatient monitoring. The direction of travel is toward outpatient administration and simplified monitoring — the same logistics-not-biology competition seen in cell therapy, and the reason format choices made a decade ago (Amgen's no-Fc BiTE versus the IgG-like designs everyone else adopted) still determine who can be dosed in a community clinic.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bispecific antibody | Any antibody-derived molecule with two different binding specificities. The parent category — not all bispecifics are T-cell engagers (e.g. Hemlibra bridges clotting factors; Rybrevant binds EGFR and MET on the same cell). |
| T-cell engager (TCE) | The subset of bispecifics whose second arm binds CD3 to recruit T cells as the killing agent. |
| BiTE | **Bi**specific **T**-cell **E**ngager — Amgen's trademarked format: two scFv fragments joined by a linker, no Fc region. Small (~55 kDa), hence a short half-life. |
| HLE BiTE | Half-life-extended BiTE — an Fc or albumin-binding domain bolted on to allow intermittent dosing instead of a pump. |
| IgG-like TCE | Built on a full antibody scaffold with an Fc region (Lunsumio, Columvi, Epkinly, Tecvayli, Talvey). Half-life of days to weeks → weekly or biweekly dosing, often subcutaneous. |
| Fc region | The antibody's constant tail. It binds the recycling receptor FcRn, which is what keeps a molecule in circulation for weeks. Its absence is why blinatumomab needs a pump. |
| Continuous infusion | Blinatumomab's ~2-hour half-life means it is given as a multi-week uninterrupted IV infusion via a portable pump — the single biggest practical drawback of the no-Fc format. |
| Step-up (priming) dosing | Escalating from small initial doses to full dose, to reduce first-dose CRS. Standard for every marketed engager. |
| CRS / ICANS | Cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity — the shared toxicity signature of T-cell redirection. See `car-t.md`. |
| Antigen escape | Tumour loses expression of the targeted marker. Drives BCMA → GPRC5D switching in myeloma. |
| CRL (complete response letter) | FDA's formal rejection of an application. Not necessarily a verdict on the science — often trial design, population, or manufacturing. |
