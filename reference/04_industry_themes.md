# Industry themes — the four stories driving pharma right now

*Cross-company context: company files tell you who; this file tells you why everyone is doing what they're doing. Asset lists and figures are seeded from `graph/` (marketed, 22 covered companies) plus the company files' Current-bets sections. Sections ordered by how often they'll come up in daily news.*

## 1. The GLP-1 / obesity gold rush

The biggest commercial phenomenon in pharma history: two molecules — tirzepatide (Lilly: Mounjaro + Zepbound, ~$36.5B FY2025) and semaglutide (Novo: Ozempic + Wegovy + Rybelsus, ~$34B FY2025) — together outsell most companies' entire portfolios, and the market is still supply- and access-constrained. First-generation GLP-1s (Trulicity ~$4B and declining, Victoza/Saxenda ~$1B) are already legacy.

**Why it reshapes everything, not just diabetes:** outcomes trials keep expanding the label footprint — cardiovascular risk, sleep apnea, heart failure, kidney disease, MASH, and (in trials) Alzheimer's and addiction. Every additional indication turns "weight-loss drugs" into "cardiometabolic platform therapy" and pulls more payers in. The strategic consequence: every large company needed an answer, and the KB's company files show four postures:

| Posture | Who | The bet |
|---|---|---|
| Incumbent duopoly | Lilly, Novo | Next-gen: orforglipron (oral small-molecule GLP-1, Ph3, Lilly), retatrutide (GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple), CagriSema (GLP-1+amylin, Novo); Novo also bolting on Metsera-adjacent amylin assets and defending semaglutide pricing (Medicare MFP ~$245/mo from 2027) |
| Bought in | Roche (Carmot, $2.7B, 2023), AstraZeneca (Eccogene oral GLP-1 license), Regeneron (Hansoh olatorepatide license, 2025) | Late but credible entry via BD, mostly oral or combination angles |
| Platform challenger | Amgen (MariTide — monthly GLP-1R agonist/GIPR *antagonist*, Ph3), Boehringer (survodutide, GLP-1/glucagon dual, Ph3) | Differentiate on dosing interval or mechanism rather than follow the leaders |
| Adjacent/defensive | Pfizer (danuglipron discontinued, then Metsera acquisition battle vs Novo, 2025), Merck (oral Lp(a) + weight-management BD), Vertex/Gilead/Biogen | Either bought optionality or explicitly stayed out |

**What to watch in the news feed:** orforglipron launch economics (an oral changes manufacturing and pricing completely), CagriSema vs Zepbound head-to-head data, MariTide Ph3 readouts, compounding-pharmacy litigation, and IRA/MFN pricing spillover (see §3).

## 2. The ADC boom

Antibody-drug conjugates — an antibody that homes to a tumor antigen, carrying a chemotherapy payload — went from niche (~2019) to the industry's favorite oncology modality. The graph currently holds 9 marketed ADCs across 8 companies; the defining asset is trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu, Daiichi Sankyo w/ AstraZeneca, ~$4.5B Daiichi-booked FY2025 / ~$6B combined), whose DESTINY-Breast04 trial created an entirely new treatable category ("HER2-low") and validated the platform economics.

**The platform, not the drug, is the asset.** Daiichi's DXd platform produced Enhertu, Datroway (TROP2), and three more ADCs that Merck paid $4B upfront (up to $22B) to share in 2023 — after AstraZeneca had already paid ~$2.35B upfront across two deals. Pfizer bought Seagen outright ($43B, 2023) for its vedotin platform (Adcetris, Padcev). Gilead bought Immunomedics ($21B, 2020) for Trodelvy. AbbVie's Elahere came via ImmunoGen ($10.1B, 2023). GSK licensed a B7-H3 ADC from China's Hansoh (2025), and Merck's sacituzumab tirumotecan comes from Kelun-Biotech — the ADC boom and the China-BD wave (§4) are the same story in many deals.

**Marketed ADC scoreboard (from graph):** Enhertu (HER2, Daiichi/AZ), Padcev (Nectin-4, Pfizer, ~$1.9B), Polivy (CD79b, Roche, ~$1.7B), Trodelvy (TROP2, Gilead, ~$1.4B), Elahere (FRα, AbbVie, ~$0.7B), Adcetris (CD30, Takeda ex-US), Datroway (TROP2, Daiichi/AZ), Blenrep (BCMA, GSK, relaunching), Emrelis (c-Met, AbbVie, 2025).

**Risks the files flag:** interstitial lung disease as a DXd class effect; payload resistance; bispecific-ADC leapfrog risk (mostly China-originated); and for Daiichi specifically, one-platform concentration — a single class-wide safety event hits everything.

## 3. IRA and the US pricing regime change

The 2022 Inflation Reduction Act ended pharma's four-decade free-pricing era in Medicare. Mechanics that matter: Medicare negotiates "maximum fair prices" for selected high-spend drugs (first negotiated prices effective **January 2026**), small molecules become eligible 9 years post-approval vs 13 for biologics (the "pill penalty" that visibly tilts R&D toward biologics), plus Part D redesign shifting catastrophic-phase costs onto manufacturers, and inflation-capped price increases.

**Round-1 casualties, live since Jan 2026 (from company files):** Eliquis (BMS/Pfizer, ~56% list discount), Enbrel (Amgen, ~67%), Entresto (Novartis), Januvia (Merck), Stelara/Xarelto/Imbruvica (J&J), Jardiance (Lilly/Boehringer), Farxiga (AZ), insulins (Sanofi/Novo/Lilly). Round 2 (2027) reaches semaglutide — Medicare MFP ~$245/month, an existential number for Novo's US economics. Later cycles will reach Keytruda-scale biologics as they age.

**Second-order effects visible across the KB:** companies now *plan LOE and IRA together* (a negotiated price 2 years before patent expiry destroys the tail that used to fund the next pipeline); "subcutaneous conversion" strategies (Opdivo Qvantig, Darzalex Faspro) partly exist to start new patent/negotiation clocks; and the 2025-26 layer on top — most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing deals and tariff threats — pushed Gilead, AbbVie, AZ, and Novo into direct-to-consumer/Medicaid pricing agreements with the administration. Nearly every company file's Key-risks section now has an IRA/MFN line; it is the industry's shared macro risk.

## 4. China biotech as the new licensing engine

The fastest structural change in BD: China-originated molecules went from curiosity to a primary pipeline source in ~3 years. Roughly a third of big pharma's recent in-licensed assets trace to Chinese biotechs, at upfronts typically 30-50% below US-biotech comparables. The graph's deal cache already holds a representative sample across 7 of our 22 companies:

| Year | Deal | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | J&J / Legend Biotech — Carvykti CAR-T | The proof-of-concept: a Chinese-originated asset became a best-in-class US myeloma therapy |
| 2019 | Amgen / BeiGene — $2.7B equity | Early platform bet on China commercial + R&D |
| 2023-24 | Merck / Kelun-Biotech — sac-TMT + ADC portfolio | China as ADC engine (see §2) |
| 2024 | Merck / Hengrui — oral Lp(a), ~$0.2B up | Cardiometabolic small molecules too |
| 2025 | Pfizer / 3SBio — SSGJ-707 PD-1×VEGF bispecific, $1.25B up (record) | The Akeso/ivonescimab shock made PD-1×VEGF the hottest class in oncology; Pfizer paid record money to catch up |
| 2025 | GSK / Hansoh + Hengrui — B7-H3 ADC; 12-program deal (~$0.5B up) | Portfolio-scale sourcing, not one-off |
| 2025 | Regeneron / Hansoh — olatorepatide | Even antibody-platform purists buy Chinese obesity assets |
| 2026 | Sanofi / Sino Biopharm — rovadicitinib, ~$1.5B | Immunology joins the wave |

**Why it works:** Chinese biotechs run faster, cheaper early-stage trials with huge patient enrollment; the 2021-24 domestic funding winter made out-licensing the business model; and US buyers get Ph2-ready differentiated assets (especially bispecifics and ADCs) at a discount. **The tensions:** data-transferability questions at FDA (ivonescimab's China-only survival data), BIOSECURE-style political risk, and the strategic irony that big pharma is now dependent for innovation on a geography Washington treats as a rival. AstraZeneca's China exposure (largest MNC pharma in China, plus a 2024 import-violation probe) is the cautionary tale several files cite.

**What to watch:** whether an in-licensed Chinese asset wins a major FDA approval on global data (normalizes everything), PD-1×VEGF pivotal readouts (Summit/Akeso, Pfizer/3SBio, BioNTech/Biotheus), and any BIOSECURE legislative revival.

## How the themes interlock

GLP-1 profits fund everyone's ADC and China shopping; IRA's pill penalty pushes investment toward the biologics that ADCs and bispecifics are; China supplies the assets that fill post-IRA pipeline gaps at acceptable prices; and obesity's expansion into cardiometabolic outcomes is exactly the kind of high-Medicare-spend territory IRA negotiation will target next. One sentence version: **the industry is spending GLP-1-era cash to buy cheaper Chinese innovation in IRA-favored modalities, with ADCs as the flagship class.**
