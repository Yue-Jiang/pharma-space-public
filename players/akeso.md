# Akeso

## What it is
Akeso is a Chinese antibody company founded by Michelle Xia (Xia Yu) that develops bispecifics on its Tetrabody platform and commercializes them itself in China. It reported record 2025 commercial sales of RMB 3,033.1 million, up 51.5%, with all approved products and indications on China's National Reimbursement Drug List. Its lead molecule, ivonescimab, was approved in China in May 2024 and is licensed outside greater China to Summit Therapeutics.

## Relationship to the covered 22
- **Merck (2015)** — out-licensed the CTLA-4 antibody that became quavonlimab, Akeso's first deal with a large-cap.
- **Merck (as competitor, 2024-2026)** — HARMONi-6 in China is the first Phase 3 to show superiority over an anti-PD-(L)1-containing regimen, with positive overall survival presented in the ASCO 2026 plenary session — the first China-developed asset to hold that slot.
- **Pfizer, BMS, Merck (indirect)** — ivonescimab's data set is what made PD-1/L1xVEGF bispecifics a must-have, driving Pfizer's 3SBio deal, BMS's BioNTech partnership and Merck's LaNova license.

## Why it matters
Akeso is the single clearest case in the KB of Chinese biotech setting the agenda rather than supplying it. One molecule from a Zhongshan company forced four of the covered 22 to buy into a mechanism, and the November 2026 US decision on ivonescimab is the class's first Western regulatory verdict.
