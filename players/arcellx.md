# Arcellx

## What it is
Arcellx was a cell-therapy company built around the D-Domain binder — a compact, non-antibody binding domain used in place of a conventional scFv in CAR constructs. Its lead program, anitocabtagene autoleucel (anito-cel), is the first BCMA-directed CAR-T in multiple myeloma to use that binder. Gilead's Kite unit partnered on the program in December 2022 and bought the company outright in 2026, so Arcellx now exists inside the KB as an acquired origin entity rather than an active counterparty.

## Relationship to the covered 22
- **Gilead / Kite (Dec 9, 2022)** — strategic collaboration to co-develop and co-commercialize CART-ddBCMA (later anito-cel) in multiple myeloma, with shared US economics.
- **Gilead (announced Feb 23, 2026; closed Apr 28, 2026)** — acquisition at $115 per share in cash plus a $5-per-share contingent value right, ~$7.8 billion implied equity value. Gilead already held ~11.5% of the shares. The CVR pays only if cumulative global anito-cel sales reach $6.0 billion from launch through the end of 2029. Gilead guided the deal to reduce 2026 diluted EPS by roughly $5.57–5.67 and to turn accretive from 2028.

## Why it matters
The buyout is a clean worked example of why partners get bought: Gilead stated the rationale as consolidating control and eliminating future profit-share, milestone and royalty obligations before launch. It also sets the competitive frame for myeloma cell therapy — anito-cel versus Legend/J&J's Carvykti and BMS's Abecma — and the CVR structure ties Gilead's price to commercial performance rather than approval.
