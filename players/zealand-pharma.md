# Zealand Pharma

| HQ | Founded | Ticker | Status | Why it matters to the KB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Søborg (Copenhagen), Denmark | 1998 | Nasdaq Copenhagen: ZEAL | Independent, clinical-stage | Peptide house behind two of the biggest non-Novo/Lilly obesity assets: petrelintide (Roche) and survodutide (Boehringer Ingelheim) |

## What it is
Zealand Pharma discovers peptide therapeutics and concentrates on metabolic and rare disease. Its strategy is to invent the molecule and partner the scale: petrelintide, a long-acting amylin analog, went to Roche in the largest single-asset partnership on record, and survodutide is wholly Boehringer's to develop and commercialize.

## Relationship to the covered 22
- **Roche (March 12, 2025)** — petrelintide collaboration and license: $1.65 billion upfront ($1.4 billion at closing, $250 million over two anniversaries), $1.2 billion of development milestones tied largely to Phase 3 starts, $2.4 billion of sales milestones — up to $5.3 billion total — with US and European profits and losses shared 50/50. Zealand pays Roche $350 million in four instalments across 2026-2027 for CT-388's contribution to the combination product.
- **Boehringer Ingelheim** — survodutide is licensed out entirely; Boehringer runs the Phase 3 obesity and MASH programs.
- **Sanofi** — lixisenatide, discovered at Zealand and marketed by Sanofi (deal terms not verified this session).

## Why it matters
Zealand is how two of the covered 22 got into obesity without discovering an incretin. It also anchors the amylin thesis — the leading argument that the next wave of weight-management drugs competes on tolerability rather than peak weight loss — and its Roche deal reset the price of a single Phase 2 metabolic asset.
