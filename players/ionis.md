# Ionis Pharmaceuticals

| HQ | Founded | Ticker | Status | Why it matters to the KB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carlsbad, California | 1989 (as Isis Pharmaceuticals; renamed 2015) | NASDAQ: IONS | Independent, commercial-stage | The antisense oligonucleotide platform — origin of Spinraza, Qalsody and Wainua, and a partner to Biogen, AstraZeneca, Novartis and GSK |

## What it is
Ionis invented and industrialised antisense oligonucleotides (short synthetic nucleic acids that silence or modulate a target RNA), and licensed most of its early output to larger companies. It now markets seven medicines — Tryngolza (olezarsen), Dawnzera (donidalorsen), Wainua (eplontersen), Spinraza (nusinersen), Qalsody (tofersen), Tegsedi (inotersen) and Waylivra (volanesorsen) — several independently. 2025 revenue was $944 million with a $381 million net loss; H1 2026 revenue was $514.0 million with a $207.2 million net loss, and management targets cash-flow breakeven in 2028.

## Relationship to the covered 22
- **Biogen** — nusinersen and tofersen; Spinraza was Biogen's growth engine in the late 2010s and remains the platform's proof point.
- **AstraZeneca** — eplontersen co-development and US co-commercialization; the ATTR-CM failure in 2026 removed the largest expansion opportunity.
- **Novartis** — pelacarsen, the most advanced attempt to prove Lp(a) lowering reduces cardiovascular events.
- **GSK** — bepirovirsen for chronic hepatitis B.

## Why it matters
Ionis is the counterparty behind an entire modality. When the KB asks who supplies the RNA-targeting assets in Biogen's neurology franchise or AstraZeneca's cardiometabolic pipeline, the answer is one company — and its 2026 eplontersen setback shows how a single partnered readout can reprice both sides of a deal.
