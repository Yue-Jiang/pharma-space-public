# Genmab

| HQ | Founded | Ticker | Status | Why it matters to the KB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copenhagen, Denmark | 1999 | NASDAQ: GMAB | Independent, profitable | Antibody-engineering platform behind Darzalex, Kesimpta, Epkinly and Tivdak — it collects royalties from four of the covered 22 |

## What it is
Genmab is an antibody company whose technology platforms (DuoBody bispecifics, HexaBody complement enhancement) produced molecules now marketed by larger partners. That royalty base funds a shift to wholly owned products: revenue was $2,051 million in H1 2026, up 25%, and 2026 guidance was raised to $4.325–4.525 billion. In December 2025 it closed the ~$8.0 billion all-cash acquisition of Merus, adding petosemtamab and taking on about $5.5 billion of debt.

## Relationship to the covered 22
- **J&J (2012)** — daratumumab license; the molecule became Darzalex, J&J's myeloma backbone.
- **Novartis** — ofatumumab, originally partnered with GSK, reached Novartis with the 2015 oncology-portfolio transfer and became Kesimpta in multiple sclerosis.
- **AbbVie (2020)** — epcoritamab co-development; Genmab pays AbbVie a profit share on Epkinly US/Japan sales, visible as rising cost of product sales.
- **Pfizer (via Seagen)** — Tivdak co-development and profit-sharing.

## Why it matters
Genmab shows how much of large-cap oncology revenue is licensed-in science: four separate covered companies pay it. Its pivot from royalties to owned products — Rina-S, petosemtamab, the Merus deal — is the test of whether a platform company can become a commercial one before its biggest royalties (Darzalex) face biosimilars.
